CoinShares PLC | Preliminary Memo for Theia
1. Business Overview

CoinShares PLC (NASDAQ: CSHR) is Europe's largest and longest-established digital-asset investment manager — a regulated, institutional-grade platform that builds and operates crypto exchange-traded products (ETPs) and runs a balance-sheet-driven trading business. Founded in 2014 by commodities-hedge-fund veterans Daniel Masters, Russell Newton, and Jean-Marie Mognetti (who together launched one of the first regulated Bitcoin funds via Global Advisors in 2014), CoinShares has spent more than a decade building crypto infrastructure strictly inside the regulated financial perimeter — a decade-long head start over the Wall Street asset managers that only entered the category after the U.S. approved spot Bitcoin ETFs in 2024. The company is headquartered in St. Helier, Jersey, and operates a "dual-engine" model: a recurring, fee-paying Asset Management segment that earns management fees on aggregate assets under management (AUM), and a Capital Markets segment that uses the group's own balance sheet for non-directional (delta-neutral) market-making, liquidity provisioning, digital-asset lending, and optimized proof-of-stake staking. Together the two engines let CoinShares capture upside in crypto bull markets while remaining one of only two European crypto-ETP issuers (alongside 21Shares) that operate profitably through the cycle.

CoinShares manages a diversified, 30+ product suite across four platforms. The legacy XBT Provider — acquired out of a Swedish bankruptcy estate in 2016 with just ~$10M in AUM and grown to a ~$5B peak — remains the group's "cash cow," charging a premium 2.50% (250 bps) fee on Nordic-listed Bitcoin and Ethereum ETNs and generating $92.6M of management fees in FY2025 (73% of total management fees). CoinShares Physical is the institutional flagship: physically-backed ETPs listed on SIX, Xetra, Euronext, and the LSE, priced at 15–150 bps, which ranked #1 in Europe by net organic inflows in FY2025 ($1.1B). The Staked ETP suite (ETH, SOL, SEI, TON, BNB) charges 0% management fees and instead shares on-chain staking yield with noteholders, while the BLOCK Index (licensed to Invesco EMEA) and the U.S. Valkyrie platform (spot Bitcoin ETF BRRR, Bitcoin miners ETF WGMI, altcoins ETF DIME) round out the offering. CoinShares commands an estimated ~34% share of the European digital-asset ETP market — a leadership position protected by the region's fragmentation across ~44 national markets, each requiring localized regulatory approvals, distribution, and clearing that foreign entrants find expensive to replicate.

On March 31, 2026, CoinShares International Limited completed a business combination with Vine Hill Capital Investment Corp. (a SPAC), transitioning its primary listing from Nasdaq Stockholm (ticker CS) to the U.S. Nasdaq, where the newly formed parent, CoinShares PLC, began trading under CSHR on April 1, 2026. The deal — structured as a reverse recapitalization valuing the enterprise at ~$1.53B and anchored by a ~$49M PIPE — places the platform in the world's largest capital market, home to roughly half of global financial assets. In July 2025 CoinShares Asset Management became the first continental European regulated manager authorized under MiCA, opening access to the €33 trillion EU asset-management market, and the pending acquisition of FCA-regulated Bastion Asset Management (announced Oct 2025, $4.5M) is intended to extend the group beyond passive beta into actively-managed, higher-fee crypto alpha strategies.

Valuation Summary

CoinShares is one of the most unusual valuation setups in the Theia universe: a profitable, cash-generative, dividend-paying market leader trading at an enterprise value of essentially zero. At $4.03, the $551M market cap is backed by a $481.4M available-capital position (cash plus hedged accrued fees plus principal investments) and zero debt — so the market is valuing the #1 European digital-asset ETP franchise, its 30+ product suite, its proprietary Galata/MATRIX technology stack, and its dual-engine earnings power at roughly $70M, or under 0.8x forward EBITDA. On a P/E basis the stock trades at 6.7x FY2026E and 5.8x FY2027E consensus earnings, and it yields ~8.2% on the FY2025 dividend. The de-rating has been violent — down 62% YTD and 61% over one year, sitting at just 29% of its 52-week high — driven by a broad crypto-price drawdown that cut Q4 2025 AUM by $2.5B, the technical overhang of a fresh de-SPAC listing (the "DeSPAC reset"), and near-total analyst neglect (one covering broker). The single sell-side target of $9.00 implies +123% upside. The core tension is that CoinShares' earnings are highly crypto-beta — management fees accrue daily on AUM that swings with BTC/ETH prices, and Capital Markets yields spike in high-volatility regimes — so consensus FY2026E net income of $78.8M is set below the FY2025 actual of $114.3M as it normalizes out non-operational gains. That crypto sensitivity is exactly what produces the wide outcome dispersion Theia targets: a sum-of-the-parts at a peer-median 6x EBITDA plus net capital implies ~$7.50/share today, with meaningful upside if a crypto up-cycle re-inflates AUM.

3. Bull & Bear Cases
Bull Cases

EV ≈ Zero — Balance Sheet Backs the Whole Market Cap. With a $481M available-capital position, no debt, and a $551M market cap, an investor is effectively buying the operating business — Europe's #1 crypto-ETP franchise generating $114M of net income in FY2025 — for ~$70M of enterprise value. Any normalization of the market's fear premium, plus continued dividend payments (~8% yield) and potential buybacks funded from the capital surplus, could re-rate the equity toward the SOTP fair value of ~$7.50/share (a peer-median 6x EBITDA plus net capital) without any improvement in crypto prices.

Crypto Up-Cycle Re-Inflates AUM. Management fees accrue daily on ~$6.4B of fee-paying AUM. Because CoinShares' cost base is largely fixed (~$40M annual overhead) with ~69% segment EBITDA margins, incremental AUM flows almost entirely to the bottom line. In prior bull markets XBT alone reached ~$5B and total AUM peaked well above current levels; a return of BTC/ETH to cycle highs, combined with net inflows into CoinShares Physical (the #1 European ETP by inflows), could push fee-paying AUM to $10B+ and drive earnings materially above the FY2025 $114M peak.

MiCA + U.S. Listing Unlock Distribution. In July 2025 CoinShares became the first continental European manager authorized under MiCA — a passportable license into the €33 trillion EU asset-management market — while the April 2026 Nasdaq listing places its model in the world's largest capital market. Deepening distribution via European neobanks and brokers (BoursoBank's 7M clients, Bourse Direct, Nordea's 9M customers, Trade Republic, Scalable Capital) and a growing U.S. presence via Valkyrie provide multiple, non-price-competitive growth vectors.

Active Alpha & Staking Mix-Shift Lifts Fees. The pending Bastion acquisition adds FCA-regulated, quant-driven active management — higher-fee (0.75–1.85%) products that lift blended economics beyond passive beta. The zero-management-fee staked-ETP suite (ETH/SOL/SEI/TON/BNB) monetizes native on-chain yield, and the Capital Markets desk earns 8–10% delta-neutral yields during volatile periods — giving CoinShares revenue lines that grow even when passive ETP fees compress.

Strategic Acquisition Target. A profitable, regulated, ~$550M-cap platform with 34% European market share, MiCA authorization, U.S. registrations, and a cash-rich balance sheet is a highly logical bolt-on for a traditional asset manager (which would gain an instant, compliant crypto franchise) or a larger digital-asset player seeking European scale. The 62% drawdown makes the platform far cheaper to acquire today; even a modest control premium to fair value implies a double from current levels.

Bear Cases

Crypto Beta Cuts Both Ways. The same operating leverage that amplifies bull-market earnings works violently in reverse. AUM fell $2.5B in Q4 2025 purely on price, and a sustained crypto winter — BTC/ETH down 50%+ for an extended period, coupled with net redemptions — would compress AUM, management fees, and Capital Markets yields simultaneously, potentially cutting earnings by half or more and validating the market's discount.

U.S. Fee War Bleeds Into Europe. Post-spot-ETF, BlackRock, Fidelity, and peers have driven U.S. spot-Bitcoin fees below 0.30%, treating them as loss-leaders. CoinShares already cut its flagship BITC fee to 0.25% in early 2025. If European fragmentation erodes and mega-scale issuers pursue share aggressively, the blended ~170 bps fee that underpins the model could compress structurally, permanently lowering the earnings base.

Custody, Counterparty & DeFi Failures. CoinShares has a documented history of severe counterparty losses in market dislocations: a $38.4M full impairment on its FlowBank stake (2024), a £17M loss on the Anchor/Terra collapse (2022), and an FTX write-off (2022). Its ETPs depend on third-party custodians (Komainu, Zodia, BitGo), and the Capital Markets desk engages in on-chain staking and lending — exposing it to hacks, validator slashing, smart-contract bugs, and counterparty defaults that could cause permanent capital loss and reputational damage.

Regulatory Reclassification Risk. MiCA imposes stringent capital, reserve, and licensing demands; the SEC remains cautious on altcoins; and — critically — because CoinShares holds and trades a large volume of digital assets, any determination that assets beyond Bitcoin and Ether are "securities," or changes to the Investment Company Act of 1940, could restrict its product range, raise compliance costs, or alter its status as an investment manager.

Governance & Related-Party Concentration. Leadership is highly centralized around CEO Mognetti and Director Masters, who together own ~33% of shares. AlphaSense flags extensive related-party transactions — the group trades on behalf of both executives and holds digital-asset balances due to them (e.g., 9.08 BTC for Mognetti; $7.9M of trades for Masters in 2024), maintains recharge agreements with Komainu (where Mognetti is a director/shareholder), and transferred control of CoinShares GP I to a Masters-influenced entity. A five-member board and foreign-private-issuer reporting reduce disclosure granularity that institutional allocators expect.

4. Helmer's 7 Powers

1. Scale Economies Strong
CoinShares operates with a largely fixed cost base of ~$40M annual overhead against a scalable AUM-fee revenue model. Because central costs stay roughly flat as assets grow, incremental AUM flows directly to the bottom line — segment EBITDA margin expanded to ~69% in FY2025 from ~63% in FY2024 despite adverse late-year prices. RedEye's analysis notes that using CoinShares' fixed cost base as an industry proxy, only CoinShares and 21Shares operate profitably among European crypto-ETP issuers, meaning the group's scale is a genuine structural cost advantage that sub-scale competitors cannot match. In a business where dozens of issuers list near-identical BTC/ETH wrappers, being able to run profitably at low breakeven AUM is the decisive edge.

2. Network Effects Weak
ETPs are not a two-sided network — a Bitcoin ETP does not become more valuable to a holder because other investors own it. There are mild indirect benefits: deeper AUM and trading volume improve secondary-market liquidity and tighten bid-ask spreads on CoinShares' products (supported by its own Capital Markets market-making), which marginally attracts more flows. But this is a liquidity effect, not a true network effect, and it does not create the compounding winner-take-all dynamics of a genuine network business.

3. Counter-Positioning Moderate
For more than a decade, incumbent Wall Street asset managers could not offer crypto products without regulatory, reputational, and career risk — leaving the category to specialist pure-plays like CoinShares, which built regulated infrastructure the incumbents were structurally unwilling to touch. That counter-position has weakened in the U.S. since 2024 as BlackRock and Fidelity entered aggressively. It remains more durable in Europe, where CoinShares' active/staked/index product mix and hedge-fund DNA differ from the plain-vanilla passive products the giants prefer to launch as loss-leaders — but the moat is eroding rather than strengthening, hence a Moderate rating.

4. Switching Costs Weak
For end investors, switching between crypto ETPs is easy — they are fungible, exchange-traded wrappers, and holders readily rotate toward lower fees or better liquidity (evidenced by ongoing XBT outflows as investors take profits). Switching costs are somewhat higher at the distribution layer: neobanks and brokers that have integrated CoinShares Physical ETPs (BoursoBank, Bourse Direct, Nordea) face integration friction to swap issuers, and institutional allocators value CoinShares' custody, attestation, and compliance track record. But at the product level, stickiness is low — this is a fee-and-liquidity-competitive market, not a lock-in market.

5. Branding Moderate
CoinShares is one of the most recognized and trusted brands in European digital assets, with a decade-long regulated track record, ISO/IEC 27001 certification since 2019, daily public reserve attestations (LedgerLens), and a reputation for operating strictly inside the regulated perimeter. In a category defined by FTX-style blow-ups, that trust is a real asset — it wins distribution partnerships and institutional mandates and supports a premium fee on legacy products (XBT's 250 bps persists despite cheaper alternatives). However, the brand does not command durable consumer pricing power against BlackRock/IShares-scale competitors, so it is a facilitator rather than a standalone power.

6. Cornered Resource Moderate
CoinShares controls several scarce, hard-to-replicate assets: the MiCA authorization (first continental European manager to obtain it) and its stack of Jersey/AMF/SEC/FINRA/NFA licenses represent years of regulatory work that act as a moat against new entrants; the XBT Provider platform, acquired for ~$10M and now a $2.5B, 250-bps cash cow, is an irreplaceable legacy asset; and the founding team's decade of crypto-and-commodities expertise is genuinely rare. The Komainu custody JV (built with Nomura and Ledger) is another differentiated relationship asset, though CoinShares has moved to a secondary role there. These licenses and legacy platforms are real cornered resources, tempered by the fact that regulatory approvals are increasingly attainable by well-capitalized rivals.

7. Process Power Strong
CoinShares' proprietary Galata operating system — an in-house-built bridge connecting TradFi networks with DeFi protocols, executing 100,000+ trades/day across 200+ real-time algorithms and 30+ counterparties — plus the next-generation MATRIX execution-and-risk platform (delivered 2024, ~1,000x faster matching) embed operational capabilities that took a decade to build. This technology lets the Capital Markets desk run delta-neutral market-making and statistical arbitrage that hedge ETP liabilities without directional risk, isolating the balance sheet from crypto volatility while generating high-margin yield. Combined with automated 30-minute collateral reconciliation and multi-custodian controls, this is a genuine, hard-to-replicate process advantage that few crypto peers possess.

Summary: The strongest moats here are Scale Economies (profitable at low breakeven where most European peers lose money) and Process Power (the Galata/MATRIX technology stack that runs a delta-neutral, balance-sheet-driven trading engine), supported by Moderate Cornered Resource (MiCA + multi-jurisdictional licenses, the XBT legacy platform) and Branding (a decade-long regulated trust advantage). The business is weaker on Network Effects and Switching Costs, because crypto ETPs are fungible, fee-competitive wrappers — which is why fee compression is the key structural risk. This is a real but incomplete moat profile: durable enough to keep CoinShares profitable through the cycle, but not wide enough to prevent long-run fee pressure.

5. Porter's 5 Forces

1. Threat of New Entrants Moderate
Barriers are meaningful but falling. A credible new entrant must obtain multi-jurisdictional regulatory approvals (MiCA, national ETP listings, custody arrangements), build institutional-grade custody and attestation, secure authorized participants and market-makers, and earn distribution — a stack that took CoinShares a decade. Europe's fragmentation across ~44 national markets raises the cost further, insulating incumbents. However, the 2024 U.S. spot-ETF approvals demonstrated that well-capitalized giants can enter fast and at scale, and MiCA's harmonization — while a moat for CoinShares' first-mover license — ultimately lowers the entry barrier for others over time. Net: Moderate and directionally rising.

2. Bargaining Power of Suppliers Moderate
CoinShares' key "suppliers" are third-party custodians (Komainu, Zodia, BitGo), staking/validator providers (Blockdaemon, Twinstake), exchanges and trading counterparties, and the underlying blockchains themselves. Custodians hold the crypto backing the ETPs, giving them real leverage, though CoinShares mitigates this with a multi-custodian strategy and its own Komainu stake. The deepest supplier dependency is on external internet infrastructure, exchanges, and DeFi protocols — a systemic failure or insolvency at an integrated venue could impair the delta-neutral hedging the model relies on. Model providers and blockchains are largely commoditized/substitutable, keeping aggregate supplier power moderate.

3. Bargaining Power of Buyers High
Buyers — retail and institutional investors — hold strong power because crypto ETPs are fungible and price-transparent. With dozens of near-identical BTC/ETH wrappers listed across Europe and the U.S., investors readily rotate toward the lowest fee or deepest liquidity, and the post-spot-ETF fee war has handed them the whip hand. CoinShares defends with brand trust, product breadth (staked/active/index products competitors lack), and captive legacy XBT holders, but it has still been forced to cut its flagship BITC fee to 0.25%. High buyer power is the single most important structural headwind to long-run fee durability.

4. Threat of Substitutes High
Investors seeking crypto exposure have abundant substitutes for CoinShares' ETPs: direct spot ownership on exchanges, self-custody wallets, competing issuers' ETPs/ETFs (BlackRock IBIT, 21Shares, Bitwise, Grayscale), crypto-proxy equities (MicroStrategy, Coinbase, miners), and futures. For the passive beta portion of the business, substitution is intense and largely fee-driven. CoinShares mitigates this on the margin with regulated, physically-backed, staking-enabled wrappers that many institutions can only access through a compliant vehicle — but the substitute set is broad and cheap, keeping this threat High.

5. Competitive Rivalry High
Rivalry is intense and bifurcated. In Europe, CoinShares (~34% share) competes with 21Shares, WisdomTree, VanEck, DeFi Technologies/Valour, and Bitwise; in the U.S., its Valkyrie unit sits against BlackRock, Fidelity, Grayscale, and Bitwise in a commoditized, fee-compressing arena. The product is highly substitutable and competition is increasingly on price. CoinShares avoids the worst of the U.S. war by focusing on differentiated active, staked, and index products rather than plain passive, but the overall environment is a crowded, low-differentiation market where scale and cost discipline determine survival.

Summary: The competitive environment is challenging: buyer power, substitute threat, and rivalry are all High because crypto ETPs are fungible, fee-competitive wrappers in an increasingly crowded field, and the U.S. fee war is a structural drag on the whole industry. CoinShares' defense is not a wide product moat but rather cost leadership and process power — being one of only two European issuers that operate profitably, run a differentiated active/staked/index mix, and use a proprietary trading engine to earn balance-sheet yield. That makes it a survivor and consolidator in a brutal market, but it does not confer the pricing power of a structurally protected franchise. The investment case therefore rests more on the near-zero EV and crypto-beta optionality than on Porter-style competitive insulation.

6. What Drives the Stock?

The following are the 6 variables that would move CSHR's share price materially in the next 12 months — the metrics analysts would build tracking models around and PMs would ask about in morning meetings.

1. BTC / ETH Prices and Fee-Paying AUM. This is the master variable. Management fees accrue daily on ~$6.4B of fee-paying AUM that moves almost one-for-one with digital-asset prices — Q4 2025 alone saw AUM fall $2.5B on price. Rising crypto prices lift AUM, fees, and Capital Markets mark-to-market simultaneously and flow to the bottom line at ~69% incremental margin; falling prices do the reverse. Bullish: BTC/ETH breaking to new highs with net ETP inflows. Bearish: a sustained crypto winter with redemptions.

2. Net Flows into CoinShares Physical & Staked ETPs. Distinct from price, organic net inflows show franchise health. CoinShares Physical was #1 in Europe by net inflows in FY2025 ($1.1B); continued share gains via neobank/broker distribution (BoursoBank, Bourse Direct, Nordea, Trade Republic) would signal the platform is compounding independent of price. Persistent XBT/legacy outflows outpacing Physical inflows would be a warning that the franchise is shrinking under the price noise.

3. Blended Fee Rate / Fee Compression. The ~170 bps blended fee has been remarkably stable across FY2023–25, but the U.S. sub-0.30% fee war and CoinShares' own BITC cut to 0.25% make fee trajectory the key structural swing factor. Bullish: mix-shift toward higher-fee active (Bastion) and staked products holds or lifts the blend. Bearish: European fee compression accelerates as mega-issuers pursue share.

4. Capital Markets Yield & Volatility. The Capital Markets desk earns ~92 bps on associated assets, with delta-neutral strategies generating 8–10% yields in high-volatility regimes and staking/lending adding recurring income. Because these yields rise with volatility and crypto prices, CM revenue is a powerful — but lumpy — swing factor each quarter. Watch the segment's contribution and any principal-investment gains/losses.

5. Capital Return & Balance-Sheet Deployment. With EV near zero and a $481M capital surplus, what management does with the cash is pivotal. Continued/growing dividends (~8% yield), a buyback at a near-zero-EV valuation, or accretive M&A (Bastion-style active-alpha deals) would each help close the gap between price and net asset backing. Signs of value-destructive principal investments (à la FlowBank) would do the opposite.

6. Coverage, Index Inclusion & De-SPAC Overhang Clearing. With just one covering broker and a fresh de-SPAC listing, CSHR suffers from neglect and technical overhang (PIPE/founder unlocks, sponsor selling). Initiation of U.S. sell-side coverage, index inclusion, improved liquidity, and the lapse of lock-ups would broaden the shareholder base and could re-rate a name currently priced for maximum obscurity. Bullish: new coverage and a stabilizing float. Bearish: continued forced/technical selling.

7. What Drives the Business?

CoinShares' revenue decomposes into its two engines using fundamental unit-economics drivers:

Key driver quantification (FY2025 actuals):

  • Asset Management ($126.4M): ~$6.4B fee-paying AUM × ~170 bps blended fee. XBT alone = $92.6M (250 bps on ~$2.5B, 73% of all management fees); CoinShares Physical, BLOCK (65 bps on >$1B, via Invesco), and Valkyrie make up the balance.
  • Capital Markets ($73.1M): ~92 bps blended yield on balance-sheet/associated assets — ETH staking ($5.6M Q1 + $4.3M Q2 2025), digital-asset lending (~4.5% yields), and delta-neutral trading (8–10% in high-vol periods).
  • Fixed cost base (~$40M): largely flat overhead; ~69% segment EBITDA margin means AUM growth converts to profit at very high incremental rates.
  • "Hidden" non-cash revenue: $120.3M of FY2025 revenue was non-cash accrued XBT/Physical fees held in (hedged) digital assets — $280M of the $481.4M capital position — realized as cash on ETP redemption.

Leverage analysis: The three highest-leverage drivers are: (1) Fee-Paying AUM, which is dominated by BTC/ETH prices — a 25% move in crypto prices moves AUM ~25% and, at ~170 bps and ~69% incremental margin, moves management-fee EBITDA by roughly $19M (25% × $6.4B × 1.70% × 69%), or ~$0.10 of EPS; over a full crypto cycle this is the difference between the bear and bull scenarios. (2) Blended Fee Rate — every 10 bps of blended-fee compression on $6.4B AUM removes ~$6.4M of high-margin revenue, so the long-run fee trajectory is as important as AUM growth. (3) Capital Markets Volatility Multiplier — CM revenue can swing from ~$40M in calm markets to $70M+ in volatile ones, making it the primary source of quarter-to-quarter earnings variability. The upshot: CoinShares is a levered, two-sided bet on crypto-asset value and volatility, with a fixed-cost engine that magnifies both directions.

8. AlphaSense Insights
AlphaSense Research Reports · Subject: CoinShares PLC (CSHR) · Reports Reviewed: "Company Primer & Overview" (18 pp), "Comprehensive Analysis of Business Fundamentals" (14 pp), FY2025 earnings release, F-1/424B3 prospectus, 13D/A & ownership filings, FactSet snapshot
Key themes synthesized from AlphaSense competitive intelligence, management commentary, and industry research.

The Accounting Distortion That Masked True Profitability. The single most important analytical insight is that CoinShares' historical financials systematically understated its economics. Under IFRS (IAS 38), digital assets were treated as intangibles, forcing revaluation gains through Other Comprehensive Income while corresponding ETP certificate liabilities were marked to market through the P&L — producing large, artificial net losses that led databases like Morningstar to report negative EPS and obscured a cash-generative business. A Q3 2024 IFRS reclassification (IAS 2 inventories / IFRS 9) and the FY2025 conversion to U.S. GAAP now push all fair-value movements directly through earnings, revealing the real picture: ~69% segment EBITDA margins and $114M of FY2025 net income. Investors screening on stale GAAP/IFRS history may still be mispricing the true earnings power.

The "Hidden Assets" on the XBT Platform. A widely misunderstood balance-sheet feature: XBT management fees are denominated in fiat but held in digital assets (BTC/ETH) inside the ETP collateral structure, recognized daily as they accrue and economically hedged via FX and digital-asset swaps so they are insulated from crypto volatility. As of Dec 31, 2025 these accrued fees had grown to ~$280M of the group's $481.4M available-capital position — earned, risk-free economic value that is systematically unlocked as cash during normal ETP redemption cycles. This is real, hedged capital that the near-zero EV does not credit.

The Legacy XBT "Cash Cow" and Distressed-M&A Playbook. AlphaSense repeatedly highlights CoinShares' disciplined, value-accretive M&A: XBT Provider was bought out of a Swedish bankruptcy estate in 2016 with ~$10M AUM and now generates $92.6M of annual fees at a premium 250 bps (73% of all management fees) despite ongoing structural outflows — an extraordinary return on a distressed purchase. The pattern repeats with Elwood (2021, BLOCK index IP), Napoleon (2021, first AIFM-regulated crypto AM), Valkyrie (2024, U.S. beachhead), and the pending Bastion deal (active alpha). Management has proven it can buy distressed/strategic assets cheaply and extract durable cash flows.

Structural European Moat vs. Scale-Driven U.S. Market. The research frames a stark regional divergence: the U.S. market is homogeneous, scale-driven, and fee-compressed (spot ETFs as sub-0.30% loss-leaders from BlackRock/Fidelity), whereas Europe is fragmented across ~44 national markets with diverse languages, distribution channels, and clearing infrastructures. That fragmentation is a natural barrier that has let CoinShares hold ~34% share (peaking near 50%) and maintain a stable ~170 bps blend where U.S. peers cannot. RedEye notes only CoinShares and 21Shares operate profitably among European issuers, underscoring how sub-scale competitors are structurally disadvantaged.

AI, Governance & the Bear Case. On technology, CoinShares uses AI internally for workflow automation and execution optimization on Galata/MATRIX (contrast with rival DeFi Technologies, which is packaging AI as a customer-facing feature via a Neuronomics stake). On governance, AlphaSense is direct about the risks: a close-knit, founder-dominated structure (Mognetti + Masters), extensive related-party transactions and trading on behalf of executives, recharge agreements with affiliated custodian Komainu, and a five-member board — plus a documented history of counterparty losses (FlowBank $38.4M, Anchor/Terra £17M, FTX). Key investor questions: (1) What is the normalized, through-cycle earnings base once non-operational gains are stripped out? (2) How durable is the ~170 bps blended fee as U.S. compression spreads? (3) How will the $481M capital surplus be deployed — buybacks, dividends, or M&A? (4) What governance changes will accompany the shift toward U.S. domestic reporting if FPI status lapses? (5) Can active/staked mix-shift (Bastion) offset passive fee erosion?
