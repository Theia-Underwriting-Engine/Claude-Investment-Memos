Blend Labs, Inc. | Preliminary Memo for Theia
1. Business Overview

Blend Labs, Inc. (NYSE: BLND) is a cloud-based software platform provider that powers digital lending and consumer banking experiences for financial institutions across the United States. Founded in 2012 by Nima Ghamsari — a former Palantir engineer who recognized that the mortgage origination process was still largely manual and paper-driven — Blend has evolved into the dominant digital point-of-sale (POS) platform for residential mortgage origination, processing approximately 17-19% of all US mortgage applications by HMDA volume at its peak. The company's platform sits at the critical intersection between borrowers and lenders, digitizing the end-to-end workflow from application through closing. Blend operates two core product suites: the Mortgage Suite, which enables lenders to offer a seamless digital borrower experience for home purchase, refinance, and home equity loans; and the Consumer Banking Suite, which extends the same platform to deposit account opening, personal loans, auto lending, and solar financing. In March 2026, Blend launched Autopilot — an agentic AI product that completes document reviews in 15-25 seconds, targeting the ~$4,000 of operational labor cost embedded in every $11,000 mortgage origination.

Blend's business model is primarily success-based: the company earns revenue on a per-funded-loan basis through its "economic value per funded loan" (evPFL) metric, which stood at $83 in Q4 2025 and has been trending upward from the $60-70 range in earlier years. This per-transaction model creates direct exposure to mortgage origination volumes — a structural feature that is both the company's greatest opportunity (in a rate normalization or refi wave) and its key risk (in a prolonged high-rate environment). The Consumer Banking Suite, now 37% of total revenue and growing 34% year-over-year, provides critical diversification away from mortgage cyclicality. The company has penetrated 30-40 of the top 100 US financial institutions and counts nearly 100 home equity customers including 7 of the top 10 US originators. In FY2025, Blend achieved its first full year of positive non-GAAP operating income ($15.1M vs. a $12.8M loss in FY2024) and free cash flow ($2.8M), marking a critical inflection point. Following the sale of Title365 — the title insurance business acquired for $417.7M in 2021 and divested to Covius in June 2025 — Blend now operates a clean, debt-free balance sheet with $68.3M in cash, a $50M share buyback authorization, and singular focus on its core software platform.

Valuation Summary

Blend currently trades at 3.7x NTM EV/Sales and 20.1x NTM EV/EBITDA — a significant discount from its post-IPO peak but still at a premium to the broader fintech peer median of 2.9x EV/Sales. The stock is down 49% over the past year and 65% from its 52-week high of $4.49, driven by persistent mortgage market headwinds (30-year rates above 7%), the Title365 overhang (now resolved), and broader small-cap de-rating. However, the consensus analyst target of $4.10 (6 brokers) implies 159% upside — an unusually wide gap that suggests the sell-side sees significant mispricing. The NTM gross margin profile of 77.6% and EBITDA margin trajectory from negative (FY2025 GAAP) to 18.6% (FY2026E) to 21.3% (FY2028E) reflects a company in genuine margin inflection, with operating leverage accelerating as Consumer Banking scales and Autopilot AI drives evPFL expansion. On a FY2028E basis, the valuation compresses to 2.9x EV/Sales and 13.4x EV/EBITDA — cheap for a high-gross-margin SaaS company with multiple expansion levers (AI, rate normalization, refi wave). The $68M net cash position (zero debt) and $50M buyback authorization provide downside protection, while the success-based per-loan model creates natural operating leverage in any mortgage recovery.

3. Bull & Bear Cases
Bull Cases

Autopilot AI Transforms Mortgage Unit Economics. Blend Autopilot, launched March 3, 2026, completes document reviews in 15-25 seconds — targeting ~$4,000 of the $11,000 cost to originate a mortgage that is pure operational labor. Within one week of launch, 7 large customers activated Autopilot. If Autopilot achieves broad adoption, it could double evPFL from $83 to $150-170 (top 5 customers already average $177 total evPFL), driving Mortgage Suite revenue re-acceleration independent of volume. The April 2026 launch of an MCP server for Autopilot enables programmatic access to the full lending lifecycle — creating platform lock-in. The Better.com/OpenAI partnership validates the thesis that AI in mortgage is real, and Blend is the only scaled platform positioned to capture it.

Rate Normalization Triggers Refi Wave. The mortgage market is operating at historically depressed levels (~4.5M units vs. 8-10M in normal years). If 30-year rates decline from 7%+ to 5.5-6%, the resulting refinance wave could double mortgage origination volumes within 12-18 months. Blend's per-funded-loan model would translate this directly into revenue — a 50% increase in volumes at constant evPFL would add ~$35M to Mortgage Suite revenue. The Rapid Refi product, which showed 50%+ conversion improvement in pilots and commands 1.9x higher evPFL, is purpose-built for exactly this scenario. With the average homeowner sitting on ~$315,000 in equity, the home equity opportunity alone could drive significant incremental volume through Blend's platform.

Consumer Banking Becomes the Growth Engine. Consumer Banking grew from 29% to 37% of total revenue in one year and is on a trajectory to become the majority of Blend's revenue by FY2027. The multi-product flywheel — consumer banking customers cross-sell into mortgage, and mortgage customers expand into deposit accounts, personal loans, and auto lending — is creating durable, non-cyclical revenue growth. With nearly 100 home equity customers (7 of top 10 originators) and new product launches in deposit account opening and solar financing, Consumer Banking has a long runway. Each new large bank customer represents $300K-$1M+ of recurring annual revenue.

Clean Balance Sheet Enables Aggressive Buybacks. With $68.3M cash, zero debt, and the completed sale of Title365 eliminating $144.5M of legacy acquisition debt, Blend's balance sheet is the cleanest it has been since IPO. The $50M buyback authorization (12% of market cap) at these depressed prices is highly accretive — buying back shares at $1.58 when the consensus target is $4.10 is a compelling use of capital. Management completed the prior $25M program (5.1M shares at ~$3.13 avg), and the new program at current prices would retire ~32M shares (12% of diluted count), meaningfully reducing the share count and boosting per-share economics.

Strategic Acquisition Target. At $510M EV with $68M cash, zero debt, 78% gross margins, a dominant market share in digital mortgage origination, and a nascent AI platform, Blend is an attractive acquisition target for ICE (Encompass parent), Fiserv, Jack Henry, or a financial-services-focused PE firm. ICE acquired MERS and Ellie Mae to control the mortgage technology stack; Blend would complete the borrower-facing layer. At even a modest 50% premium, the implied takeout price of $2.37 represents the Bear Mild scenario — meaning downside is protected by strategic value while upside is uncapped.

Bear Cases

Persistent Rate Environment Crushes Mortgage Volumes. If 30-year mortgage rates remain above 7% through 2028 — a plausible scenario given structural inflation and fiscal deficits — mortgage origination volumes could stay at 4-5M units annually, far below the 6-8M historical average. Blend's success-based model means revenue is directly tied to funded loans; prolonged volume depression would cap Mortgage Suite revenue at $65-70M regardless of evPFL improvements. The market has not priced a "rates stay higher for much longer" scenario, and the stock could revisit $1.00-1.30 if volume expectations reset lower.

Mr. Cooper / Rocket Migration Risk. Rocket Companies' acquisition of Mr. Cooper creates a concentrated churn risk. Mr. Cooper represented ~60 bps of HMDA market share on Blend's platform, with a contract running through June 2028. Rocket has its own proprietary technology stack and no incentive to remain on Blend. Post-contract expiration, Blend could lose ~$10-15M of annual Mortgage Suite revenue — a ~15-20% hit to the segment. HMDA market share already declined from 21.7% (2023) to 18.6% (2024) with an expected ~100bp headwind in 2026 from this transition. Management has been transparent about this risk but has not fully quantified the revenue impact.

Customer Concentration and Execution Risk. Top 5 customers represent 39% of revenue, and 25 customers generating >$1M account for 75% of total revenue. This concentration creates outsized risk from any single customer churn or renegotiation. Additionally, Blend identified a material weakness in internal controls over financial reporting in Q4 2025 — a red flag for enterprise customers evaluating vendor risk. The revolving door of executives (CEO transition from Ghamsari to Dogan, departure of Amir Jafari, multiple leadership changes) raises questions about operational continuity and whether the company can execute on its ambitious AI roadmap.

AI Commoditization — Autopilot May Not Differentiate. The Better.com/OpenAI partnership demonstrates that mortgage AI is not proprietary to Blend. If generic AI tools (OpenAI, Google) become capable of document review and loan processing, Blend's Autopilot premium could be competed away. Lenders with internal engineering teams (JPMorgan, Wells Fargo, Rocket) may build proprietary AI mortgage assistants, reducing demand for Blend's platform. The agentic AI opportunity is real, but Blend's right to win is not guaranteed — particularly against well-funded competitors like ICE/Encompass that control the loan origination system (LOS) layer below Blend's point-of-sale.

Preferred Stock Overhang Limits Equity Upside. The $161M preferred stock (FactSet EV adjustment) creates a structural overhang on common equity value. In any enterprise value below ~$230M, the preferred holders' liquidation preference would consume most of the value, leaving common shareholders with minimal residual. This preferred overhang effectively acts as a "dead zone" — even if the business stabilizes, the common stock may not participate proportionally in value recovery. The Haveli investment (~20% ownership via preferred/common) provides operational discipline but also creates a governance dynamic where Haveli's interests may not perfectly align with common shareholders.

4. Helmer's 7 Powers

1. Scale Economies Moderate
Blend's cloud platform has meaningful fixed-cost leverage: once the core POS, document processing, and compliance engines are built, the marginal cost of processing an additional loan or opening an additional deposit account is near zero. This is reflected in the gross margin expansion from 52% (FY2023, inclusive of Title365) to 74% (FY2025) and projected 81% (FY2028E) — a dramatic improvement as revenue scales across a fixed engineering and infrastructure base. R&D and G&A costs are largely fixed, meaning each incremental dollar of revenue drops through at high contribution margins. However, Blend's TAM is constrained by the number of US financial institutions (a few hundred meaningful lenders) and mortgage origination volumes (cyclical, not secular growth), which limits the absolute scale advantage relative to horizontal SaaS companies. The per-funded-loan pricing model also means revenue scales with volume rather than with customer count, creating operating leverage only when mortgage markets cooperate.

2. Network Effects Weak
Blend does not exhibit meaningful network effects. More lenders using Blend does not make the platform more valuable for other lenders — in fact, competitors sharing a platform could be a detractor. There is a modest data network effect: as Blend processes more loans and banking applications, its AI models (particularly Autopilot) improve from training on a larger dataset of document patterns, exception types, and underwriting outcomes. This data flywheel could become meaningful over time as Autopilot scales, but today it is nascent and unproven. Blend also benefits from an indirect ecosystem effect — as more third-party integrations (Truework for income verification, Covered Insurance Solutions) connect to Blend's platform, the integration layer becomes more valuable — but this is an ecosystem advantage rather than a true network effect.

3. Counter-Positioning Strong
Blend's strongest competitive dynamic is counter-positioning against ICE Mortgage Technology (Encompass), the dominant loan origination system (LOS). Encompass is deeply embedded in the back-office workflow — it is the system of record for loan processing, underwriting, and compliance. But Encompass was designed for loan officers and processors, not borrowers. Blend provides the borrower-facing digital experience that sits on top of the LOS. ICE cannot build a competitive borrower POS without cannibalizing its LOS customers' existing workflows and admitting its own interface is inadequate — a classic counter-positioning dynamic. The same applies to nCino in the consumer lending space: nCino's strength is in commercial lending and bank back-office, not in the consumer-facing digital experience where Blend dominates. Blend effectively occupies the "borrower experience layer" that incumbents cannot serve without undermining their core value proposition to loan officers.

4. Switching Costs Strong
Switching costs are Blend's most durable moat. The platform is deeply integrated into each lender's technology stack — connected to the LOS (Encompass, Black Knight), CRM, document management, compliance systems, and third-party data providers (credit bureaus, income verification, title). Ripping out Blend would require: (a) rebuilding all integrations with a new POS vendor, (b) retraining hundreds of loan officers and operations staff, (c) migrating in-progress loan applications, (d) recertifying compliance workflows with regulators, and (e) disrupting the borrower experience for months during transition. This creates extreme stickiness: Blend reported zero net churn in 2025 (vs. meaningful churn in 2023 during the cost-cutting wave), and the only 4 customers that churned in Q3 2025 were small, representing ~$200K aggregate annual revenue. The 7 of top 10 US originators using Blend's home equity product demonstrates that even during a difficult rate environment, these deep integrations are not being renegotiated away.

5. Branding Weak
Blend is a B2B infrastructure company — borrowers interacting with Blend's platform generally see their bank's branding, not Blend's. The Blend brand matters in enterprise sales (CIOs and CTOs evaluating digital lending vendors) but does not command consumer-facing pricing power or habitual purchasing behavior. Within the industry, Blend has strong recognition among mortgage technology buyers — the Palantir heritage, Nima Ghamsari's reputation, and the company's Customer Forum (120 executives attended in 2025) create credibility. But branding is not a meaningful competitive advantage at the B2B level; it facilitates sales cycles rather than creating structural barriers.

6. Cornered Resource Moderate
Blend's most valuable cornered resource is its HMDA processing data — having originated or facilitated ~17-19% of all US mortgage applications over several years, Blend has accumulated a proprietary dataset of borrower behavior, document patterns, exception handling, and underwriting outcomes that is difficult to replicate. This data is the training foundation for Autopilot. Additionally, Nima Ghamsari's relationships with top-25 bank CEOs (cultivated over 14 years since founding) represent a personal cornered resource — a top bank CEO specifically told Ghamsari that his two priorities were "stablecoins and Agentic AI." The Haveli investment and Brian Sheth's board involvement provide access to PE-grade operational discipline. However, these resources are not fully irreplaceable: Ghamsari's transition to Chairman (with Ozgur Dogan as CEO) introduces execution risk around relationship continuity.

7. Process Power Moderate
Blend has refined its enterprise implementation process over 200+ lender deployments, developing deep institutional knowledge about the complex workflows, compliance requirements, and change management challenges specific to mortgage lending and consumer banking. The Rapid product suite (Rapid Refi, Rapid Home Equity) reflects this process expertise: these products were designed based on specific workflow bottlenecks identified through years of implementation experience. The Customer Forum format — bringing 120+ executives together to share best practices — creates a community-driven process advantage. However, the revolving door of executives (multiple C-suite departures in 2024-2025) suggests that process knowledge may be concentrated in the engineering team and product organization rather than broadly embedded in institutional culture. The appointment of Matt Thomson (who scaled Alkami to $440M ARR) as Head of Revenue in January 2026 signals an attempt to institutionalize sales process power.

Summary: Blend is a 2-power business with genuine strength in Counter-Positioning (ICE/Encompass cannot build a borrower-facing POS without cannibalizing its LOS business) and Switching Costs (deep integration into lender tech stacks creates extreme replacement cost). Scale Economies and Cornered Resource are moderate and growing — particularly as Autopilot's data flywheel compounds. Network Effects and Branding are weak. This is a defensible but not fortress-like competitive position, with the AI layer (Autopilot) potentially upgrading Cornered Resource to Strong if the data advantage proves durable.

5. Porter's 5 Forces

1. Threat of New Entrants Moderate
The barriers to entry in digital mortgage POS are real but not insurmountable. A new entrant would need: (a) deep integration with the major LOS platforms (Encompass, Black Knight), (b) compliance with mortgage lending regulations (TILA, RESPA, ECOA, HMDA), (c) bank-grade security and SOC 2 compliance, (d) relationships with risk-averse bank CTOs who prefer proven vendors, and (e) several years of deployment track record. Capital requirements are moderate ($50-100M to build a competitive platform), but the regulatory complexity and integration burden create meaningful friction. However, the AI wave is lowering barriers: Better.com partnered with OpenAI to build AI-powered mortgage processing, demonstrating that well-funded startups with AI expertise could leapfrog Blend's traditional POS approach. The bigger threat is not new pure-play entrants but adjacent incumbents (ICE, nCino, Fiserv) extending into Blend's layer.

2. Bargaining Power of Suppliers Low
Blend's primary "suppliers" are cloud infrastructure providers (AWS/GCP), AI model providers (for Autopilot), third-party data providers (credit bureaus, income/employment verification), and its own engineering talent. Cloud infrastructure is commodity-priced and multi-vendor. AI models are rapidly commoditizing with open-source alternatives. Blend has strategically shifted from in-house services to partnership models — Truework for income verification (VOIE/VOA), Covered Insurance Solutions for homeowner insurance — which reduces supplier dependency while improving margins. The company's partnership model with these providers is mutually beneficial rather than extractive. Engineering talent in San Francisco is competitive but Blend's 419-person workforce is modest, reducing talent cost pressure relative to larger tech companies.

3. Bargaining Power of Buyers High
This is Blend's most challenging competitive force. Top 5 customers represent 39% of revenue, and 25 customers account for 75% — extreme concentration for a $124M revenue company. These customers are among the largest US financial institutions with sophisticated procurement functions and significant leverage in pricing negotiations. The success-based (per-funded-loan) pricing model means buyers only pay when loans close, giving them free optionality during low-volume periods. Large banks can credibly threaten to build internal digital lending solutions (JPMorgan, Wells Fargo have large technology organizations) or switch to competitors (SimpleNexus, ICE POS). The Mr. Cooper/Rocket situation demonstrates that customer M&A can eliminate major revenue streams with limited recourse for Blend. However, switching costs partially offset buyer power — once integrated, banks rarely change POS vendors mid-cycle.

4. Threat of Substitutes Moderate
The primary substitute for Blend's digital POS is the traditional paper-based or lender-built mortgage application process. Many mid-size lenders still use Encompass's native POS or proprietary solutions rather than a third-party digital POS like Blend. For consumer banking, banks can build deposit account opening and personal loan workflows in-house or through their core banking platform (FIS, Fiserv, Jack Henry). The AI revolution creates a new substitution vector: if large language models can handle document review, borrower communication, and compliance checking natively, the "digital POS" layer that Blend provides could be bypassed entirely. However, the regulatory complexity of mortgage lending — where errors result in legal liability and regulatory penalties — creates a meaningful barrier to substitution by generic AI tools. Lenders cannot afford AI "hallucinations" in compliance-critical workflows.

5. Competitive Rivalry High
The digital lending technology market is intensely competitive. Blend faces competition from: ICE Mortgage Technology (Encompass POS, owned by Intercontinental Exchange — $90B market cap parent), nCino (cloud banking platform, $3.5B market cap), SimpleNexus/nCino (recently acquired, strong in independent mortgage bank segment), Black Knight/ICE (integrated servicing and origination), and a long tail of smaller fintech POS vendors. ICE's ownership of both the dominant LOS (Encompass) and the dominant POS competitor creates a vertically integrated threat — ICE can bundle pricing and create preferential integrations that disadvantage Blend. The mortgage technology market is also slow-growing (tied to housing activity), meaning competitors are fighting over a relatively fixed pie during high-rate environments. Differentiation is moderate — all POS vendors claim "digital, fast, integrated" — and pricing pressure from per-loan competition compresses margins at the margin.

Summary: Blend's five-force structure is mixed: favorable supplier dynamics (Low) and meaningful entry barriers (Moderate), but challenged by high buyer power (concentrated customer base), moderate substitution risk (in-house build, generic AI), and intense competitive rivalry (ICE, nCino, SimpleNexus). The competitive environment is manageable but not structurally favorable — Blend must continuously innovate (Autopilot, Rapid products) to maintain its position against well-funded competitors with adjacent advantages. The AI layer is both an opportunity (if Blend leads) and a threat (if competitors or generic tools catch up).

6. What Drives the Stock?

The following are the 6 variables that would move BLND's share price materially in the next 12 months. These are what analysts build tracking models around and PMs ask about in morning meetings.

1. Mortgage Origination Volumes and Interest Rates. BLND's success-based revenue model creates direct, mechanical sensitivity to mortgage market volumes. Every 10% increase in HMDA origination volume translates to approximately $6-7M of incremental Mortgage Suite revenue (holding market share and evPFL constant). The 30-year mortgage rate is the single most important exogenous variable — a 100bp decline from 7% to 6% could trigger a refinance wave that doubles origination volumes over 12-18 months. Conversely, rates staying above 7% would keep volumes at trough levels and cap Mortgage Suite revenue. The May 7 Q1 2026 earnings will include Q2 2026 volume guidance (management guided 1.50-1.60M units) — any upside surprise would be immediately bullish.

2. Consumer Banking Suite Growth Trajectory. Consumer Banking revenue grew 34% YoY in FY2025 and now represents 37% of total revenue. The deceleration from 48% (Q1 2024 YoY) to 21% (Q4 2025 YoY) is the key bear concern — analysts are watching whether growth re-accelerates as new products (deposit account opening, solar lending) launch and existing customers expand, or whether the 20%+ growth rate was a one-time catch-up. The target metric is whether Consumer Banking can sustain 20%+ growth and exceed 50% of total revenue by FY2027, which would fundamentally change the narrative from "mortgage company" to "banking platform."

3. Autopilot AI Adoption and evPFL Expansion. Blend Autopilot is the company's most important strategic initiative. Early data — 7 large customers activated within one week of launch, document review in 15-25 seconds — is promising but insufficient to model revenue impact. The market is watching for: (a) number of lenders deploying Autopilot by Q2 2026, (b) incremental evPFL attributable to Autopilot (management has guided toward $150-170 total evPFL for top customers), (c) whether Autopilot drives competitive wins against non-Blend platforms, and (d) pricing model (per-transaction surcharge vs. bundled). Every $10 increase in evPFL on Blend's ~834K annual funded loans = ~$8.3M incremental Mortgage Suite revenue.

4. Mr. Cooper / Rocket Churn Quantification. The Rocket acquisition of Mr. Cooper creates a binary risk: when the contract expires (June 2028), Blend likely loses this revenue. The market needs management to quantify the exposure (estimated $10-15M annual revenue) and demonstrate offsetting growth from new logos and evPFL expansion. Until this risk is quantified and absorbed, it creates an overhang on the stock. Any acceleration in the migration timeline (before June 2028) would be a material negative catalyst.

5. Profitability Trajectory and FCF Generation. Blend crossed the non-GAAP profitability threshold in FY2024 and generated its first positive FCF year in FY2025 ($2.8M). The market is watching whether the margin expansion trajectory continues — FY2025 non-GAAP operating margin of 12.2% expanding toward 18-20% by FY2026E. The May 7 earnings will be the first quarter where Autopilot could impact margins. Consistent quarterly FCF generation would shift the narrative from "turnaround story" to "compounding machine" and attract a broader investor base.

6. Share Buyback Execution and Capital Allocation. The $50M buyback authorization (12% of market cap) is a powerful signal at $1.58 per share. The pace and scale of buyback execution will signal management's conviction. The completed $25M program retired 5.1M shares at ~$3.13 average — buying at $1.58 is 2x more accretive per dollar. Aggressive buyback execution combined with FCF generation could retire 10-15% of the diluted share count over 24 months, meaningfully boosting per-share economics and creating a positive feedback loop for the stock price.
