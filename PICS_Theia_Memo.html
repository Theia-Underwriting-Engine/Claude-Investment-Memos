PicS N.V. (PicPay) | Preliminary Memo for Theia
1. Business Overview

PicS N.V. (NASDAQ: PICS), operating as PicPay, is Brazil's third-largest digital bank and seventh-largest financial institution, serving over 67 million total accounts and 42.7 million active customers through a comprehensive two-sided financial ecosystem. Founded in 2012 in Vitória, Espírito Santo as a peer-to-peer (P2P) payment app using QR codes, PicPay was acquired by the J&F Group in 2015, which provided the capital to accelerate its transformation from a simple digital wallet into a full-service super-app encompassing consumer banking, lending, insurance, investments, merchant acquiring, and e-commerce. The company completed its Nasdaq IPO on January 29, 2026 at $19 per share — the top of its range — raising $434 million in an offering that was 12x oversubscribed, valuing the company at approximately $2.5 billion. PicS N.V. is incorporated under Dutch law; the Batista family maintains approximately 98% of voting power through a dual-class share structure via J&F Participações.

PicPay's business model centers on a "wallet-first" acquisition engine: the digital wallet attracts users at near-zero cost through Pix payments, P2P transfers, and bill payments, generating rich behavioral and transactional data that powers proprietary AI-driven underwriting. This data flywheel enables efficient cross-selling of higher-margin financial products — credit cards, personal loans, payroll-deducted lending, insurance, and investment products — within a single integrated application. The company achieves up to 2.7x greater accuracy in its credit models compared to institutions relying solely on standard credit bureau data, enabling pre-approved credit offers to 87% of users with established platform history. By Q4 2025, Average Revenue Per Active Client (ARPAC) reached R$71 — more than 3x the R$20.4 cost to serve — demonstrating powerful structural operating leverage as users adopt multiple financial products. Revenue surged 85% to R$10.3 billion in 2025, with adjusted net income nearly doubling (+99%) to R$502 million and annualized adjusted ROE reaching 24.4% in Q4 2025.

The company operates across four segments: Consumer Banking (credit products, digital wallet transactions, interchange, insurance distribution — the largest segment), Small & Medium-Sized Businesses (SMBs) (merchant acquiring, MDR, corporate benefit cards — opening ~60,000 new business accounts/month), Audiences & Ecosystem Integration (PicPay Shop marketplace, PicPay Ads), and Institutional (treasury, funding, cash management). The credit portfolio expanded 128% YoY to R$24.1 billion, with a deliberate pivot toward secured products (two-thirds of new originations), particularly private payroll loans reaching ~R$500 million in monthly originations. PicPay is among the top 10 global users of OpenAI technology, utilizing ~61 billion tokens through Azure OpenAI, with AI initiating 100% of customer service chat interactions and eliminating the need for 1,000+ additional call center hires.

Valuation Summary

PICS trades at 8.1x FY2026E P/E, 0.6x P/S, and 18.4x EV/EBITDA — a dramatic discount to its IPO price of $19 (down 41%) and significantly below the consensus target of $22.77 (+104% upside). The stock has fallen 35.6% in the past month alone, reflecting post-IPO lock-up fears, Brazil macro concerns (elevated Selic rate at 14.25%), and credit quality anxieties after NPLs over 90 days reached 7.2%. However, the forward multiples compress rapidly: by FY2028E, PICS trades at just 2.2x P/E and 4.4x EV/EBITDA — pricing the company as if no credit growth materializes and margins stagnate. The 5 broker contributors unanimously rate PICS a Buy with an average target of $22.77, implying the sell-side views the current sell-off as a severe overreaction. PicPay's FY2025 ROE of 40.4% and projected FY2028E ROE of 31.5% at a 0.7x P/BV suggest the market is deeply mispricing the profitability trajectory. Relative to Brazilian fintech peers — PagSeguro at 6.3x FY1 P/E and StoneCo at a similar range — PICS trades at a premium on FY1 EV/EBITDA (18.4x vs. PAGS 5.9x) due to its deposit-heavy balance sheet inflating EV, but at a comparable equity multiple. The real question is whether the credit portfolio can scale from R$24B to R$50B+ without material deterioration in asset quality — the answer to that question determines whether PICS is a $6 stock or a $30+ stock.

3. Bull & Bear Cases
Bull Cases

Secured Lending Pivot De-Risks the Credit Portfolio. PicPay's deliberate shift toward secured products — now two-thirds of new originations — with private payroll loans reaching ~R$500M in monthly originations, fundamentally changes the risk profile. Payroll-deducted loans have structurally lower default rates (payments are deducted before the borrower receives their salary), and as the secured mix increases from ~33% to 60%+ of the total portfolio, the NPL rate should decline from 7.2% toward 4-5%, compressing cost of risk and expanding net interest margins. If the credit portfolio reaches R$40B by end-2027 with 65% secured mix and a 4.5% NPL rate, the resulting net interest income would support EPS well above consensus.

ARPAC Expansion Runway Is Massive. At R$71 ARPAC in Q4 2025, PicPay monetizes only 6% of its active users' total credit limits available in the market. Nubank's ARPAC exceeds R$110 on a more mature cohort base. As PicPay's 42.7M active users mature — adopting credit cards, insurance, investments, and payroll loans — the ARPAC trajectory toward R$120-150 over 3-4 years is highly plausible without any new user growth. Each R$10 increase in ARPAC on 42.7M users translates to ~R$1.7B of incremental annual revenue at near-zero marginal acquisition cost, flowing through at high incremental margins.

AI-Powered Operating Leverage Accelerates Margins. PicPay's deployment of generative AI across customer service (100% of chat interactions AI-initiated, eliminating 1,000+ hires), marketing (fully AI-generated campaigns), and internal operations (1,000+ AI agents serving 4,000 employees) creates a structural cost advantage that scales with the user base. As revenue grows 30-40% annually, the cost base grows at a fraction of that rate, driving an efficiency ratio from ~55% toward ~40% over the next 3 years. This operating leverage is real and observable in the widening gap between ARPAC (R$71) and cost-to-serve (R$20.4) — a 3.5x ratio that should expand toward 5x+ as AI reduces the denominator.

Pix and Open Finance Secular Tailwinds. Brazil's instant payment system Pix processed over 45 billion transactions in 2025, and PicPay captures 11% market share — positioning it as a primary gateway into the Brazilian financial system. Open Finance regulations, where PicPay holds 12.3% of consumer consents, enable account aggregation that pulls external bank data into PicPay's ecosystem, enriching credit models and deepening the wallet relationship. These regulatory-driven structural changes are irreversible and continue to shift financial activity from incumbents to digital platforms, with PicPay as a primary beneficiary.

Valuation Floor From Profitability Inflection. At $11.14, PICS trades at 8.1x FY26E earnings and 2.2x FY28E earnings for a company growing revenue 30%+ and delivering 40%+ ROE. The $19 IPO price (just 3 months ago) reflected sophisticated institutional demand at 12x oversubscription. The post-IPO decline creates a classic "broken IPO" opportunity: forced sellers (flippers, momentum funds) exit, creating a temporary liquidity vacuum that depresses the price below fundamental value. As the company reports Q1 2026 results on June 3 confirming the earnings trajectory, the stock should re-rate toward the $18-23 consensus target range.

Bear Cases

Credit Quality Deterioration in Rising Rate Environment. NPLs over 90 days have climbed to 7.2%, attributed to portfolio seasoning and the progressive limit strategy. With Brazil's Selic rate at 14.25% and potentially heading higher, borrower debt-servicing capacity deteriorates — particularly for unsecured personal loans (still one-third of originations). If NPLs reach 10%+ as the R$24B portfolio seasons, provisions would consume a significant portion of net interest income, potentially pushing the company to operating losses. The 128% YoY credit portfolio growth rate is aggressive by any standard, and the true asset quality will only be known 12-18 months after origination — meaning the worst may be ahead.

Capital Adequacy Risk and Dilution. PicPay failed to meet the required 10.5% capital ratio in 2025, necessitating an R$800M+ capital injection from the controlling family. As the credit portfolio scales toward R$30-40B, the capital requirements grow proportionally. If internal capital generation (retained earnings) cannot keep pace with credit growth, the company will need additional equity raises — diluting the Class A shareholders who hold only ~33% of shares. The IPO itself was partly motivated by capital needs, not just growth investment, which raises questions about future dilution risk.

Governance Concerns — Dual-Class Structure and Batista Family Control. The J&F/Batista family maintains ~98% of voting power through the dual-class structure, effectively making PICS a controlled company where public shareholders have minimal influence. The Batista family has been involved in past corruption investigations in Brazil (Operation Lava Jato), and related-party transaction risk exists given the broader J&F conglomerate structure. PicPay Bank has provided temporary financing to J&F entities, creating potential conflicts of interest. For institutional investors with ESG mandates or governance screens, this structure may be a permanent "no-fly zone."

Brazil Macro and FX Risk. PicPay generates 100% of revenue in BRL. The Real has depreciated ~15% against the USD over the past year, and with fiscal deficits widening and political uncertainty ahead of 2026 elections, further depreciation is possible. For USD-denominated shareholders, even strong BRL earnings growth can be offset by FX losses. Additionally, a Brazilian recession would simultaneously reduce consumer spending (lower TPV), increase loan defaults, and compress SMB revenue — a triple hit to PicPay's business model.

Intense Competition From Nubank, MercadoLibre, and Incumbents. Nubank (NU) has 100M+ users with a stronger organic brand, superior NPS, and proven credit track record. Mercado Pago (MELI) has the advantage of its e-commerce ecosystem creating captive merchant and consumer relationships. Traditional banks — Itaú, Bradesco, Banco do Brasil — have begun investing heavily in digital capabilities. PicPay's early growth was fueled by aggressive cashback subsidies during the pandemic; experts question whether the user retention is durable now that subsidies have been rationalized. If PicPay cannot differentiate beyond price, it becomes a subscale player in a market dominated by Nubank and incumbents.

4. Helmer's 7 Powers

1. Scale Economies Moderate
PicPay's digital platform has high fixed costs (technology, compliance, AI infrastructure) that are amortized over 42.7 million active users, driving the cost-to-serve down to R$20.4 per user — a metric that should continue declining as revenue scales without proportional headcount growth. The AI deployment across customer service, marketing, and operations provides genuine scale economies: the 1,000+ avoided hires represent fixed cost savings that compound with every additional user. However, the lending business (52% of revenue) requires proportional capital allocation for each loan originated, limiting the pure operating leverage of a traditional software platform. Scale economies are moderate — real on the platform side, constrained by capital intensity on the lending side.

2. Network Effects Strong
PicPay exhibits strong two-sided network effects: as 42.7M consumers adopt the wallet for Pix payments, P2P transfers, and bill payments, the platform becomes more attractive for merchants (SMBs), which in turn drives more consumer adoption. Before Pix existed, PicPay revolutionized informal payments by allowing users to pay personal trainers, gardeners, and other service providers via credit card without POS machines — creating a viral adoption loop in the informal economy. The company's 11% share of all Pix transactions and the identification that 10M of its individual consumers also own businesses creates a powerful flywheel: each new user adds value to existing users, and each new merchant adds utility for consumers. The social payment features (splitting bills, group payments) further reinforce virality within social circles.

3. Counter-Positioning Moderate
PicPay's counter-positioning is most visible against traditional Brazilian banks (Itaú, Bradesco, Banco do Brasil). These incumbents generate enormous profits from high-fee, branch-heavy models; aggressively pursuing a zero-fee digital wallet strategy would cannibalize their existing revenue streams. However, Nubank has already proven the digital-first model in Brazil, meaning PicPay is not the first mover in counter-positioning — it is a follower. Against Nubank specifically, PicPay lacks counter-positioning advantages, as both pursue similar strategies. The counter-positioning is moderate: strong against traditional banks, but weak against other digital-native fintechs.

4. Switching Costs Moderate
As 35% of active customers now treat PicPay as their primary banking relationship, switching costs are building — users have direct deposit, credit history, insurance policies, investments, and merchant relationships tied to the platform. The progressive credit limit strategy, where users must establish 12+ months of platform history before accessing unsecured credit, creates a time-based switching cost: leaving PicPay means restarting the credit-building process elsewhere. However, for the 65% of users who have not yet made PicPay their primary bank, switching costs remain low — Pix is interoperable, and Brazil's Open Finance regulations explicitly facilitate bank-switching. Switching costs are moderate and increasing with credit penetration.

5. Branding Weak
PicPay has high brand awareness in Brazil (67M accounts) but limited brand loyalty compared to Nubank, which benefits from organic word-of-mouth and a "cult following" among Brazilian consumers. AlphaSense expert calls reveal that PicPay's marketing is perceived as imitating Nubank rather than forging a unique identity, with a former brand specialist noting the company "acts as a market follower rather than a pioneer in consumer connection." The pandemic-era growth fueled by cashback subsidies created transactional loyalty (users came for the incentives) rather than brand loyalty (users came for the experience). The PicPay brand is functional and widely recognized but does not command pricing power or habitual preference.

6. Cornered Resource Strong
PicPay's most defensible asset is its proprietary behavioral and transactional data from 60+ million users, which provides a foundation for machine learning algorithms that traditional banks cannot easily replicate. A former Head of Data Science at StoneCo emphasized that PicPay's access to the daily transactional habits of its user base provides "a superior foundation for machine learning algorithms" with up to 2.7x greater accuracy in credit models versus standard bureau data. Combined with 12.3% of Brazil's Open Finance consents (account aggregation data from external banks), this creates a data moat that compounds with each transaction. Additionally, PicPay's authorization from the Brazilian Central Bank to operate as a payment institution and bank is a regulatory cornered resource — new entrants face multi-year licensing processes.

7. Process Power Moderate
PicPay has developed sophisticated AI-powered processes for credit underwriting (progressive limit strategy, 87% pre-approval rates), customer service automation (100% AI-initiated chat), and fraud prevention (multi-vendor facial recognition and document verification). The integration of Banco Original's retail operations in 2023 — migrating millions of accounts and substantial assets into the PicPay ecosystem — demonstrated meaningful organizational capability in complex banking migrations. However, the company has also experienced "severe growing pains" according to former executives, including operational inefficiencies and recent layoffs, suggesting process power is still maturing. The Hub AI platform (1,000+ AI agents for 4,000+ employees) represents an emerging process advantage that should compound over time.

Summary: PicPay is a 2–3 power business with genuine strength in Network Effects (two-sided wallet/merchant ecosystem with 11% Pix market share) and Cornered Resource (proprietary data from 60M+ users enabling 2.7x credit model accuracy advantage). Scale Economies, Switching Costs, Counter-Positioning, and Process Power are moderate and building as the credit portfolio scales and users deepen engagement. Branding is the weakest dimension — PicPay is perceived as a follower to Nubank rather than a category-defining brand. The structural moat is real but still maturing; the trajectory favors strengthening as credit penetration increases and data advantages compound.

5. Porter's 5 Forces

1. Threat of New Entrants Low
Entering the Brazilian digital banking market requires: (a) Central Bank authorization as a payment institution or bank — a multi-year regulatory process; (b) substantial capital to fund a credit portfolio (PicPay's portfolio is R$24B); (c) a massive user base to generate the behavioral data required for competitive underwriting (PicPay has 67M accounts built over 14 years); and (d) compliance infrastructure for a highly regulated financial environment. While the Pix system lowered barriers for payments, the lending and banking businesses require regulatory licenses, capital adequacy compliance, and institutional capabilities that take years to build. International fintechs (Revolut, N26) have not successfully entered Brazil due to regulatory complexity and intense local competition.

2. Bargaining Power of Suppliers Low
PicPay's primary "suppliers" are: (a) funding sources — customer deposits, CDIs, and capital markets, where PicPay's funding costs have improved from 122% of CDI to ~94% by late 2025; (b) technology providers — Microsoft Azure (OpenAI), cloud infrastructure; (c) third-party service providers like Celcoin for Pix processing. The diversification of funding sources (deposits from 42.7M users, IPO proceeds, institutional markets) reduces dependence on any single supplier. Technology partnerships are competitive (multiple cloud and AI providers available). PicPay evaluates multiple vendors for fraud prevention, payment processing, and other services, maintaining negotiating leverage.

3. Bargaining Power of Buyers Low
PicPay's 42.7 million active consumers and hundreds of thousands of active merchants represent an extremely fragmented buyer base with no customer concentration risk. No single customer represents a meaningful percentage of revenue. Consumer financial products (credit cards, personal loans, payments) are largely standardized, and buyers are price-sensitive — but PicPay's zero-fee wallet and competitive credit rates, combined with the convenience of an integrated super-app, create sufficient differentiation. For SMBs, PicPay's ability to offer a unified personal/business account experience reduces buyer power by increasing switching costs and creating operational dependency.

4. Threat of Substitutes Moderate
The primary substitutes for PicPay are: (a) Nubank — the dominant digital bank in Brazil with 100M+ users and a stronger brand; (b) Mercado Pago — the fintech arm of MercadoLibre with a captive e-commerce ecosystem; (c) traditional banks' digital offerings — Itaú's Iti, Bradesco's Next, Banco do Brasil's digital platform; and (d) other fintechs — StoneCo, PagSeguro, C6 Bank, Inter. The threat is moderate because while alternatives exist, PicPay's integrated ecosystem (wallet + banking + credit + insurance + marketplace + benefits) creates friction in switching the full relationship. However, for individual products (just credit card, just payments), substitution is relatively easy.

5. Competitive Rivalry High
Brazil's digital banking market is intensely competitive, with multiple well-funded players pursuing similar strategies of wallet-led acquisition followed by credit monetization. Nubank has a massive scale advantage (100M+ users), MercadoLibre has a captive e-commerce ecosystem, and traditional banks control the majority of deposits and lending. Competition manifests in: take rate compression on merchant acquiring, cashback and promotional spend to attract users, and aggressive credit pricing. The market is growing rapidly (digital banking penetration still expanding), which moderates the zero-sum dynamic, but rivalry for the most profitable customers (higher-income, multi-product users) is fierce.

Summary: PicPay operates in a favorable supply-side environment (low new entrant threat, low supplier and buyer power) but faces a challenging competitive landscape (high rivalry from Nubank, MercadoLibre, and incumbents; moderate substitution risk). The competitive intensity is the primary structural concern — PicPay must differentiate through data-driven underwriting, AI-powered efficiency, and ecosystem breadth rather than price alone. The company's position as Brazil's third-largest digital bank provides scale that smaller competitors cannot match, but it remains subscale relative to Nubank.

6. What Drives the Stock?

The following are the 6 variables that would move PICS's share price materially in the next 12 months:

1. Credit Portfolio Growth and NPL Trajectory. The credit portfolio (R$24.1B, +128% YoY) is the single most important driver. Every quarterly report will be scrutinized for: (a) absolute portfolio growth — Q1 2026 guidance implies R$26.5B; (b) NPL 90+ days — currently 7.2%, management attributes to seasoning; (c) provision expense as a % of revenue; and (d) secured vs. unsecured mix evolution. If NPLs stabilize at 7-8% and the secured mix continues to increase, the market will gain confidence in the credit-led growth model. If NPLs breach 10%, the stock could re-test its lows.

2. ARPAC and Monetization Trajectory. ARPAC (R$71 in Q4 2025, +52% YoY) is the clearest measure of PicPay's cross-selling success. The gap between ARPAC (R$71) and cost-to-serve (R$20.4) represents structural operating leverage. Analysts will track whether ARPAC continues expanding toward R$80-90+ or plateaus as the easiest monetization gains are captured. ARPAC growth directly translates to revenue per user without requiring new user acquisition — making it the highest-quality growth driver.

3. Brazil Macro: Selic Rate and BRL/USD. With Brazil's Selic rate at 14.25%, any movement in monetary policy directly impacts: (a) PicPay's cost of funding (CDI-linked deposits), (b) borrowers' ability to service debt (higher rates = higher defaults), (c) demand for credit (higher rates = less borrowing), and (d) the USD translation of BRL earnings. A Selic rate cut cycle would be unambiguously positive for PICS; further tightening would be negative. The BRL/USD rate matters because PICS reports in USD but earns in BRL — a 10% BRL depreciation wipes out 10% of reported earnings regardless of operating performance.

4. Post-IPO Lock-Up Expiry and Insider Activity. PICS completed its IPO just 3 months ago. Lock-up expiration schedules, insider selling patterns, and secondary offerings will create periodic supply/demand imbalances. The Batista family holds ~98% voting power via Class B shares; any signal that they are selling or that additional capital raises are planned would pressure the stock. Conversely, insider buying would be a powerful confidence signal. The company's capitalization needs (failed 10.5% capital ratio test in 2025) create a real risk of follow-on equity issuance.

5. Competitive Dynamics with Nubank. As the Brazilian digital banking market matures, the market will increasingly evaluate PicPay relative to Nubank. Key comparative metrics: user growth, ARPAC, credit portfolio quality, and profitability per user. If PicPay can demonstrate competitive or superior unit economics (higher ARPAC, lower cost-to-serve) despite smaller scale, it validates the bull case of a profitable #2 player. If Nubank's metrics pull further ahead, PicPay may be re-rated as a structurally disadvantaged competitor.

6. Regulatory Developments — Capital Requirements and Open Finance. The Brazilian Central Bank's capital adequacy requirements directly constrain PicPay's growth rate: faster credit growth requires more capital, which may require dilutive equity raises. Any regulatory changes to capital requirements, Open Finance rules, or Pix monetization policies could materially impact PicPay's economics. Favorable developments (lower capital requirements, expanded Open Finance access) are bullish; unfavorable developments (higher provisioning requirements, Pix fee restrictions) would constrain growth.

Data Moat: The Most Misunderstood Aspect of the Business. The single most important analytical insight from the AlphaSense research is that PicPay is fundamentally misunderstood as a "high-burn payment application" when it has actually evolved into a deeply integrated, data-driven financial institution. The proprietary behavioral and transactional data from 60+ million users provides a foundation for machine learning algorithms that traditional banks cannot replicate. A former Head of Data Science at StoneCo emphasized that while legacy banks possess broader regulatory privileges, PicPay's daily transactional data provides "a superior foundation for machine learning algorithms." The company achieves 2.7x greater accuracy in credit models by combining internal wallet data with Open Finance consents, enabling 87% pre-approved credit rates for users with established platform history. This data-to-credit flywheel — wallet generates data, data powers underwriting, underwriting enables credit products, credit products generate revenue and more data — is the core value creation engine that the market prices as if it were a commoditized payment app.

Expert Debate: Is User Retention Durable Without Subsidies? AlphaSense expert calls reveal a genuine debate about PicPay's moat durability. A former strategy expert at Nubank observed that PicPay's pandemic-era growth was "heavily fueled by aggressive marketing and cashback subsidies" — questioning whether users will stay once incentives are removed. However, a former COO at PicPay argued the opposite: the company "carved out a unique behavioral moat by capturing the offline, informal economy early on" — before Pix existed, PicPay was the only way for consumers to pay informal service providers via credit card without POS machines. A former regional executive highlighted PicPay's ongoing product innovation, including "facial recognition payments at supermarket checkouts" as capabilities unmatched by local peers. The truth appears nuanced: casual users attracted by cashbacks may churn, but the 35% who use PicPay as their primary bank have deeply embedded financial relationships that are increasingly sticky.

Leadership and Culture: Aggressive but Rationalizing. The leadership perception has shifted significantly. Historically, experts viewed PicPay's management as "highly aggressive, prioritizing massive cash-burn strategies." The current CEO Eduardo Chedid (formerly CEO of Elo and VP at Cielo) is actively rationalizing the P&L to demonstrate sustainable economics — the efficiency ratio improved from ~70% to ~55% over 2024-2025. However, internal culture is described as having "severe growing pains" — the aggressive expansion phase led to operational inefficiencies and recent layoffs. A former brand specialist criticized PicPay's marketing as "imitating rivals like Nubank rather than forging a unique identity," suggesting leadership occasionally acts as a market follower. On the positive side, external partners describe the culture as "highly ambitious" and "very open to collaborating with new vendors to aggressively disrupt the market."

Regulatory and Capital Structure Risks. The transition to a full-service financial conglomerate brought stringent Central Bank capital requirements. PicPay's failure to meet the 10.5% capital ratio — requiring an R$800M+ injection — signals that credit growth has outpaced capital generation. The dual-class structure (Batista family holding 98% voting power) limits public shareholder influence and creates governance concerns tied to the family's historical legal issues. Related-party transaction risk exists: PicPay Bank has provided temporary financing to J&F entities. These structural governance issues may create a permanent discount relative to Nubank (which has a single-class structure and founder-led management with strong corporate governance reputation).

Key Investor Questions. (1) What is the realistic steady-state NPL rate for the blended portfolio as it seasons — is 7.2% the peak or just the beginning? (2) Can PicPay maintain 30%+ revenue growth while simultaneously improving the credit mix toward secured products (which have lower yields but lower risk)? (3) What is the capital plan: can retained earnings fund growth, or is a follow-on equity raise inevitable? (4) How will the Kovr insurance acquisition and payroll lending expansion affect the revenue and margin mix in FY2026-2027? (5) At what point does ARPAC growth decelerate as the easiest cross-selling gains are captured — is R$100+ ARPAC achievable?
