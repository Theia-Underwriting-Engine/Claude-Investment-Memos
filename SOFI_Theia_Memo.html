SoFi Technologies | Preliminary Memo for Theia
1. Business Overview

SoFi Technologies (NASDAQ: SOFI) is a digitally-native national bank and fintech platform headquartered in San Francisco, CA, founded in 2011 and led by CEO Anthony Noto (former Goldman Sachs, NFL, Twitter) since 2018. Operating under a federally-chartered national bank license (obtained in 2022), SoFi offers a fully integrated one-stop financial services platform covering borrowing (personal, student, home loans), saving and spending (SoFi Money, credit card, SoFi Pay), investing (brokerage, crypto, private markets, ETFs), and protection (insurance). Additionally, SoFi's Technology Platform segment — consisting of Galileo (card processing infrastructure) and Technisys (core banking platform) — provides white-label BaaS infrastructure to 200+ fintechs and enterprise clients processing 8B+ transactions annually. The company employs approximately 6,500 people.

The SoFi thesis rests on three structural advantages: (1) targeting "overachievers" — high-income, high-credit borrowers (avg. income $158K, FICO 746+) historically underserved by banks optimizing for ROE over member need; (2) a cross-buy flywheel where 40% of new products opened by existing members amplifies LTV without incremental acquisition cost; and (3) the national bank charter enabling unique capabilities including the first bank-issued stablecoin (SoFiUSD, Dec-2025), the first bank-offered consumer crypto trading (SoFi Crypto, Nov-2025), and blockchain-based international payments (SoFi Pay, Oct-2025). As of Q4 2025, SoFi has 13.7M members (+35% YoY), $37.5B in deposits, 22.9% total capital ratio, and is guiding ≥30% adjusted net revenue CAGR with 38–42% EPS CAGR through 2028.

3. Helmer's 7 Powers
1. Scale EconomiesStrong

SoFi's cost to acquire members falls meaningfully with brand scale — marketing dollars now reach a 9.6% unaided awareness base vs. 2% in 2018, with effectiveness doubling per the Josh Allen campaign. Galileo processes 8B+ transactions/year for 200+ clients, spreading fixed tech infrastructure costs across a massive volume base. The $37.5B deposit franchise is self-reinforcing — a larger deposit base allows SoFi to offer best-in-class APY without repricing its entire book, a structural edge over both large banks and neo-banks.

2. Network EffectsModerate

Indirect data network effects: 13.7M members generate behavioral and credit data that trains proprietary ML underwriting models and personalizes the AI-driven "Coach" product, creating a feedback loop that improves product quality for all users. SoFiUSD's value as a stablecoin grows with distribution breadth (8B Galileo transactions/year) — more adopters reduces friction and increases merchant/partner acceptance. These are data/distribution network effects, not direct user-to-user effects, limiting the strength.

3. Counter-PositioningStrong

This is SoFi's sharpest structural advantage. JPMorgan Chase earns $60B+ in credit card revenue; offering a product that refinances $1 trillion of card debt from 24% to 12% would directly cannibalize its most profitable business. Traditional banks are structurally incapable of adopting SoFi's member-centric model without destroying their own income statements. The same logic applies to crypto — Coinbase built its business on uninsured, non-interest-bearing wallets, and cannot match the FDIC-insured, interest-bearing experience SoFi offers without abandoning its product architecture.

4. Switching CostsModerate

Multi-product members face real switching friction: direct deposit re-routing, auto-pay updates, brokerage account transfers, and credit history with SoFi's underwriting engine all create stickiness. The 97% direct deposit rate confirms SoFi is a primary banking relationship for the vast majority of depositors. However, banking switching costs are lower than enterprise software — determined consumers can migrate. The upcoming AI "Coach" product — which uses all SoFi's data to optimize a member's financial life — should meaningfully deepen this moat.

5. BrandingModerate

SoFi's brand has grown from 2% to 9.6% unaided awareness in eight years, aided by naming rights (SoFi Stadium, LA), the Josh Allen NFL partnership, and SoFi TGL golf. Management targets mid-20s awareness to reach "top 10 financial institution" status. The brand communicates a clear aspirational identity — "get your money right" for overachievers — which is genuinely differentiated from generic bank positioning. Still, 90% of consumers don't spontaneously think of SoFi when considering financial products, limiting current brand power.

6. Cornered ResourceModerate

The national bank charter is a genuine scarce resource — obtaining one requires years of regulatory investment, capital commitment, and demonstrated management competence. SoFi is the only nationally chartered bank offering crypto trading, a stablecoin on a public blockchain, and blockchain-based international remittance. The Galileo/Technisys platform, built through ~$1.2B in acquisitions, is a proprietary technology stack that competitors would take years to replicate. The Fed master account (enabling SoFiUSD reserves at zero credit/liquidity risk) is available only to bank holding companies.

7. Process PowerStrong

SoFi's internal tech development velocity is remarkable: Smart Card launched in 4.5 months; SoFiUSD from concept to stablecoin issuance in under a year; SoFi Crypto live in months after the March 2025 OCC guidance. With 40+ AI proof-of-concepts across fraud, marketing, lending, and member engagement — and the entire Galileo/Technisys stack owned in-house — SoFi can move significantly faster than bank competitors that rely on legacy core systems. This embedded capability is the organizational equivalent of a moat that compounds with use.

Summary: The strongest powers are Counter-Positioning (vs. traditional banks), Process Power (tech velocity via owned infrastructure), and Scale Economies. The bank charter plus crypto-first strategy creates a window that incumbents cannot close without self-harm and that pure fintechs cannot close without regulatory investment.

4. Porter's 5 Forces
1. Threat of New EntrantsModerate

National bank charters create a formidable barrier — SoFi spent years and significant capital to obtain one, enabling FDIC insurance, Fed account access, and crypto permissibility. However, sponsor-bank models allow fintechs (Chime, Robinhood) to offer bank-like products through partner banks without regulatory investment. Post-2025 OCC guidance, more institutions will attempt to enter SoFi's crypto-banking niche, though obtaining a comparable charter remains a multi-year, capital-intensive undertaking.

2. Bargaining Power of SuppliersLow

Deposit funding comes from 13.7M individual members — atomized and price-elastic at the margin but overall not concentrated. Capital markets buyers for LPB originations are numerous and competitive (SoFi just signed a new partner in Jan 2026 with several at final term sheet stage); demand exceeds supply. Technology vendors (AWS, Visa, Mastercard) have limited pricing power over SoFi given scale. The Galileo platform reduces dependence on third-party processing infrastructure.

3. Bargaining Power of BuyersModerate

Individual consumers have abundant alternatives in every product category, but multi-product engagement reduces price sensitivity — a member with direct deposit, personal loan, brokerage, and crypto on SoFi is unlikely to comparison-shop aggressively. LPB institutional buyers (asset managers, insurance companies) do have negotiating leverage at the margin, though SoFi's demand > supply position and credit quality track record limits their power. The Smart Card's 5% grocery cashback illustrates SoFi's willingness to compete on economics to win and retain relationships.

4. Threat of SubstitutesHigh

Every SoFi product has credible substitutes: Coinbase/Robinhood for crypto; Rocket/United Wholesale for mortgages; Marcus/Ally/Discover for HYSAs; Chase Sapphire/AmEx for credit cards; Betterment/Robinhood for investing. The key mitigant is that no single competitor offers the full integrated stack with a national bank charter — so product-level substitution doesn't necessarily mean relationship loss. But product quality must remain best-in-class to prevent leakage.

5. Competitive RivalryHigh

SoFi competes with the largest financial institutions in the world: JPMorgan ($500B+ mkt cap), American Express, Discover, and Chase in lending and deposits; Coinbase and Robinhood in invest/crypto; Galileo competes with FIS, Fiserv, and Jack Henry. The competitive intensity is structural and will increase as large banks accelerate digital investment. SoFi's counter-positioning and bank charter create a distinctive niche but not insulation from well-resourced rivals.

Summary: The competitive environment is challenging, with high rivalry and substitution risk. The primary protection comes from SoFi's structural differentiation — the bank charter enables what competitors literally cannot offer, and the counter-positioning vs. traditional bank business models reduces head-to-head competition at the core thesis level.

5. What Drives the Stock?

1. Member growth velocity. The primary investor KPI. SoFi added 1M members in Q4 2025 alone (35% YoY). An acceleration toward 40%+ would signal the brand is achieving "escape velocity" — bullish re-rate. Any sustained deceleration below 20% would indicate market saturation or competitive loss and would compress the multiple significantly. Watch quarterly member adds as the leading signal.

2. Financial Services segment revenue per product. At $104/product/year in Q4 2025 (up from $81 in Q4 2024), this is the flywheel metric. The bull case for the stock is this growing to $150+ as crypto, stablecoin, and AI-personalization monetize at scale. Each dollar increase in FS revenue/product flows nearly entirely to contribution profit given the fixed-cost nature of the digital platform. This is the highest-multiple driver and the one to watch in every earnings call.

3. Lending credit quality (personal loan charge-offs). SoFi trades at a premium multiple in part because its credit metrics have remained pristine (WA FICO 746, charge-off 2.8% annualized, below 8% lifetime tolerance). Any meaningful deterioration in the 90-day delinquency rate or charge-off trajectory — especially in a rising unemployment environment — would compress multiple rapidly. Beta of 2.01 reflects this asymmetric downside. Monitor: 90-day DQ rate (currently 52bps), net charge-off rate (280bps), and management guidance on vintage performance.

4. Loan Platform Business (LPB) execution. LPB annualized at $775M in Q4 2025, up from <$250M a year prior. This is pure capital-light fee income — no balance sheet risk, no credit exposure. New partner signings, renewal rates, and fee economics (currently ~106% execution on personal loans) are the KPIs. A slowdown in LPB demand from institutional investors would force SoFi to either originate less or hold more on balance sheet, pressuring capital ratios and reducing the fee mix story.

5. Crypto / SoFiUSD monetization. Near-zero revenue today but management explicitly describes this as a "game changer." Key to watch: SoFiUSD circulation outstanding, number of Galileo partner banks adopting SoFiUSD infrastructure, and fee economics from institutional crypto trading. Any quantification of stablecoin or crypto-as-infrastructure revenue would likely trigger a material re-rating — this is the largest option value in the stock.

6. Interest rate trajectory. Rate cuts are net positive for loan origination volumes (student refi, home loans particularly sensitive — "gangbusters" with 100-200bps cuts per management) but compress NII on deposits (60-70% downward beta). Management guides NIM above 5% through the cycle. Macro rate path directly influences both the size and timing of the bull case.

10. Is This a Theia Investment?

Theia seeks small cap companies that aren't well covered and are strongly aligned on the core internet finance thesis with wide outcome dispersion.

(i) Market Cap Bucket: $23.7B Basic / $26.6B Fully Diluted Weak
SoFi is firmly Large Cap — well above Theia's preferred micro- ($<1B) and small-cap ($1–5B) sweet spot. The recent pullback from $32.73 (52-week high) to $18.61 has compressed market cap from ~$42B to ~$24B, but this is still a large-cap company by any measure. Only a bear scenario causing a sustained <$5B valuation would bring SoFi into Theia's preferred size range — which would also imply substantial fundamental deterioration.
(ii) Analyst Coverage: 19 Broker Contributors Weak
SoFi is well-covered at 19 analysts, above Theia's under-covered threshold of <5. Active coverage by JPMorgan, Goldman Sachs, UBS, Jefferies, Mizuho, Citigroup, TD Securities, Needham, William Blair, Stephens, and Citizens JMP Securities means consensus expectations are well-informed, sell-side EPS models are detailed and frequently updated, and alpha from an information edge is difficult to establish. This is the opposite of the "undiscovered" profile Theia targets.
(iii) Blockchain / Internet Finance Thesis Alignment: First National Bank in Crypto/Stablecoin Strong
SoFi is perhaps the most direct institutional expression of the internet finance thesis in US public markets: (a) the first nationally chartered bank to offer consumer crypto trading, (b) the first national bank to issue a stablecoin on a public permissionless blockchain (SoFiUSD, December 2025), (c) blockchain-based international remittance (SoFi Pay, using Bitcoin Lightning Network), (d) digital-native banking infrastructure (Galileo) powering the entire US fintech ecosystem. SoFi is not "adjacent" to the thesis — it IS the thesis, with regulatory standing that pure crypto-native competitors can't match. Alignment: Core.
