Horizon Kinetics Holding Corp. | Preliminary Memo for Theia
1. Business Overview

Horizon Kinetics Holding Corporation (OTCQX: HKHC) is a contrarian, value-oriented investment advisory and independent research firm managing approximately $9.6 billion in assets under management as of December 31, 2025. Founded in 1994 by Murray Stahl, Steven Bregman, and Peter Doyle — all former colleagues at Bankers Trust — the firm operates as a holding company for Horizon Kinetics Asset Management LLC, which was formed through the merger of the founders' two original entities: Horizon Asset Management (high-net-worth individuals) and Kinetics Asset Management (institutional/mutual funds). The firm completed a reverse merger with Scott's Liquid Gold in August 2024 to become a publicly traded entity, with legacy Horizon Kinetics members retaining approximately 96.5% of the combined company. In September 2025, HKHC upgraded to the OTCQX Best Market tier.

The firm's investment philosophy is fundamentally contrarian: it deliberately extends time horizons to exploit the short-termism of benchmark-oriented managers, runs highly concentrated portfolios, maintains exceptionally low turnover, and explicitly rejects conventional diversification. Horizon Kinetics focuses on identifying companies with "predictive attributes" — owner-operated structures, spin-off scenarios, dormant assets — in niche market segments ignored by traditional indexes. The most prominent expression of this philosophy is the firm's extreme concentration in Texas Pacific Land Corporation (TPL), which constitutes approximately 65% of total AUM. The firm generates revenue through management advisory fees (calculated as a percentage of AUM), performance-based incentive fees from private funds, and returns on its own substantial balance sheet investments including Bitcoin-linked assets, cryptocurrency mining operations through Consensus Mining & Seigniorage Corporation (CMSG), and a growing suite of actively managed ETFs including the flagship $1.4B Inflation Beneficiaries ETF (INFL).

Horizon Kinetics has recently expanded aggressively into AI data center infrastructure — investing $47.5 million in Bolt Data & Energy alongside TPL's $50 million commitment (chaired by former Google CEO Eric Schmidt) — and into cryptocurrency mining through an "indirect mining" strategy that validates alternative blockchains and converts proceeds to Bitcoin. The firm operates with zero third-party debt, targets a 70% dividend payout ratio on operating income, and retains 30% to seed new investment products.

3. Bull & Bear Cases
Bull Cases

TPL Appreciation Drives AUM Surge. Texas Pacific Land Corporation constitutes 65% of HKHC's $9.6B AUM. A sustained rally in TPL — driven by Permian Basin royalty income, AI data center land leasing, and water resource monetization — would mechanically increase AUM toward $12-15B, generating an additional $18-38M in management fee revenue at a ~0.76% blended fee rate. The Bolt Data & Energy partnership (Eric Schmidt, chairman) positions TPL land directly in the AI infrastructure buildout, creating a potential secular demand driver that is unrelated to traditional oil & gas cycles. A 50% TPL appreciation alone would add ~$3.1B to HKHC's AUM and ~$24M in annual advisory fees.

Performance Fee Optionality Returns. In 2024, HKHC recognized $51.7M in incentive fees from private funds — nearly tripling the company's operating income. Performance fees were essentially zero in 2025 due to lower asset returns, but this revenue stream is highly binary and could return dramatically in any strong market year. If crypto prices surge, TPL rallies, or the firm's concentrated positions appreciate significantly, performance fees could reappear at $30-60M+ levels, transforming earnings overnight. The market is pricing HKHC on its "normalized" $21.4M advisor-only operating income and assigning zero value to this embedded optionality.

ETF Platform Reaches Critical Mass. The ETF suite has grown to $1.5B+ in total AUM, led by the Inflation Beneficiaries ETF (INFL) at $1.4B with $89M in net inflows in 2025. Management is actively launching new thematic ETFs — TEXX (Texas), JAPN (Japan Owner-Operators), BCDF (Blockchain Development) — that target niches mainstream indexers cannot replicate. ETF management fees grew 56% YoY in 2025. If the ETF platform reaches $3-5B AUM through continued inflows and new product launches, it would generate $20-40M in high-margin, recurring fee revenue that diversifies HKHC away from TPL concentration and private fund performance fee dependency.

Bitcoin/Crypto Exposure Creates Asymmetric Upside. HKHC holds Bitcoin directly on its balance sheet, manages crypto mining operations through Consensus Mining & Seigniorage Corporation (CMSG), and runs the Blockchain Development ETF (BCDF). The firm's "indirect mining" strategy — validating altcoin networks, selling proceeds, and buying Bitcoin — provides a sustained BTC accumulation mechanism. In a Bitcoin bull market (BTC $150K+), the combined impact of (a) balance sheet appreciation, (b) higher AUM in crypto-linked strategies, and (c) increased interest in BCDF could add $30-50M+ in revenue and unrealized gains. This crypto exposure costs HKHC very little in downside (minimal ongoing OpEx) but provides substantial convexity.

Uplisting to National Exchange Unlocks Institutional Capital. HKHC currently trades on OTCQX with only 7,622 shares/day average volume and 42.7% float. Management has discussed the need for additional trading volume to qualify for a national exchange listing. An uplisting to NYSE or NASDAQ would: (a) unlock institutional capital that cannot purchase OTC stocks, (b) enable index inclusion, (c) improve analyst coverage beyond the current single broker, and (d) compress the liquidity discount in the stock. Even a modest uplisting premium of 15-25% on the current market cap would imply $38-41 per share. The path likely involves either a secondary offering to increase float or a strategic transaction to add shares outstanding.

Bear Cases

Extreme TPL Concentration Creates Single-Stock Risk. With 65% of AUM in Texas Pacific Land, HKHC is effectively a leveraged bet on a single equity. TPL declined 22% during Q4 2025, generating $15.6M in unrealized losses on HKHC's corporate balance sheet alone. A sustained TPL drawdown of 30-40% would: (a) reduce AUM by $1.9-2.5B, (b) cut management fee revenue by $14-19M, (c) generate massive unrealized losses on HKHC's direct TPL holdings ($49.7M at cost), and (d) potentially trigger client redemptions. The firm's strategy of "natural dilution" through new product launches is a multi-year process that does nothing to mitigate near-term concentration risk.

Material Accounting Weaknesses Erode Credibility. HKHC has disclosed multiple material weaknesses in internal controls over financial reporting, including insufficient trained accounting staff for complex GAAP transactions, inadequate segregation of duties, and deficient IT general controls. The firm was forced to restate its 2023-2024 financial statements to properly consolidate proprietary funds under VIE accounting rules. These weaknesses remained unresolved as of early 2025. For a company managing $9.6B in client assets, unresolved material weaknesses create regulatory risk, investor confidence risk, and practical obstacles to an exchange uplisting.

Key Person Risk Is Severe and Unmitigated. Murray Stahl (CEO, CIO, Chairman) is the central figure in every aspect of HKHC's operations — investment decisions, client relationships, product launches, and strategic direction. He simultaneously serves on TPL's board, chairs FRMO Corp, and controls the investment committee. Stahl, Bregman, and Doyle have no formal employment agreements. The sudden loss of any founder — particularly Stahl — could trigger client withdrawals, disrupt the investment process, and threaten the firm's ability to maintain its AUM base. There is no visible succession plan, and the founders are the irreplaceable cornered resource of this business.

FRMO Revenue-Share Is a Permanent Tax on Shareholders. FRMO Corp maintains a perpetual 4.199% gross revenue-share agreement across HKHC's entire business. This is not a temporary arrangement — it is a permanent structural burden that cannot be renegotiated because HKHC's founders (Stahl as Chairman/CEO of FRMO, Bregman as President/CFO) control both entities. At current revenue levels (~$73M GAAP), FRMO extracts ~$3.1M annually; if HKHC's revenue grows to $150M, FRMO captures $6.3M. This revenue-share, combined with Stahl's dual board role on TPL and FRMO's 4.42% equity stake in HKHC, creates a complex web of related-party transactions that could deter institutional investors.

Structural Illiquidity May Persist Indefinitely. HKHC's 42.7% float and average daily volume of 7,622 shares make this stock essentially uninvestable for most institutional funds. The insider ownership concentration (Horizon Common Inc. holds 44% of shares, with founders and affiliates controlling the majority) means the free float is likely to remain constrained. Management has acknowledged the volume problem but has not committed to a specific timeline or mechanism for resolution. If the stock remains on OTCQX with micro-cap liquidity, it will continue to trade at a structural discount to intrinsic value regardless of operational performance.

4. Helmer's 7 Powers

1. Scale Economies Weak
Asset management has inherent operating leverage — the marginal cost of managing the next $100M in AUM is near-zero once the research team and infrastructure are built. HKHC's 81-employee base generates $72.8M in GAAP revenue, and advisor-only operating income margins reached ~29% in 2025 ($21.4M on ~$73M). However, the firm's extreme concentration in TPL means that AUM growth is largely a function of a single stock's price, not organic scaling through client acquisition or product diversification. True scale economies require a broadening of the AUM base across multiple uncorrelated strategies, which HKHC is pursuing through ETF launches but has not yet achieved at meaningful scale. The firm also lacks the distribution infrastructure of larger asset managers and relies heavily on word-of-mouth marketing.

2. Network Effects Weak
There are no meaningful network effects in HKHC's business. The firm's investment products do not become more valuable as more clients use them — in fact, the opposite is true for concentrated strategies, where increased AUM can reduce opportunity. The firm does benefit from a modest data network effect in its proprietary research process (more investments → more pattern recognition → better research), but this is not a structural competitive advantage that prevents competitor entry. The financial exchange investments and blockchain strategies have indirect network effect exposure through their underlying assets, but HKHC itself does not operate a platform or marketplace.

3. Counter-Positioning Strong
HKHC's strongest power is its counter-positioning against mainstream asset management. The firm's extreme concentration, contrarian philosophy, and anti-diversification stance are fundamentally incompatible with how large institutional asset managers operate. A BlackRock or Vanguard cannot replicate HKHC's approach because: (a) fiduciary standards prohibit the level of concentration HKHC employs, (b) index-based business models require diversification by construction, (c) institutional risk management frameworks would flag 65% single-stock exposure as unacceptable, and (d) the founders' personal co-investment alongside clients creates alignment that corporate asset managers cannot match. This counter-positioning is genuine — HKHC occupies a niche that large competitors literally cannot enter without abandoning their core business model.

4. Switching Costs Moderate
Switching costs vary significantly by product. For separately managed accounts (the firm's legacy business), switching costs include embedded unrealized capital gains that would crystallize upon liquidation — HKHC's low-turnover, long-hold strategy creates substantial tax-related lock-in for taxable investors. For the mutual funds and ETFs, switching costs are minimal (investors can sell at any time), though the specialized thematic focus (inflation beneficiaries, blockchain, Japan owner-operators) means there are few close substitutes. For private fund investors, lock-up periods and performance fee watermark structures create contractual switching costs. Overall, the tax-related lock-in is the most powerful switching mechanism and is a direct consequence of the firm's concentrated, low-turnover investment approach.

5. Branding Moderate
Horizon Kinetics has built a respected brand within the niche community of contrarian value investors, owner-operator enthusiasts, and hard-asset/inflation-focused allocators. Murray Stahl's intellectual output (research papers, quarterly commentaries, earnings call monologues) has cultivated a dedicated following that views Horizon Kinetics as a thought leader rather than just an asset manager. The brand drives organic capital flows — the INFL ETF gathered $89M in net inflows in 2025 through largely word-of-mouth marketing with minimal paid distribution. However, the brand is unknown outside this niche, has limited institutional recognition, and is highly concentrated in Stahl's personal reputation. Brand value is real but narrow and founder-dependent.

6. Cornered Resource Strong
HKHC's cornered resource is its founding team — Murray Stahl, Steven Bregman, and Peter Doyle — and the proprietary research process they have developed over 30 years. Stahl's dual role as HKHC CEO/CIO and TPL board member provides unique insight into HKHC's largest holding. The firm's long-standing relationships with financial exchange operators, its deep expertise in owner-operated companies, and its 30-year accumulated knowledge base in hard assets and inflation-sensitive investments cannot be replicated by a new entrant. The investment in Bolt Data & Energy (alongside Eric Schmidt and TPL) and the Synteq Digital data center transaction represent relationship-driven deals that were accessible only because of Stahl's TPL board seat and decades of industry relationships. This is a genuine, non-replicable resource.

7. Process Power Moderate
HKHC has developed a distinctive investment process centered on identifying "predictive attributes" — owner-operator structures, spin-offs, dormant assets, and indexation-avoidance opportunities. This process has been refined over 30 years and is embedded in the firm's culture of extremely long holding periods, concentrated positions, and contrarian thinking. The "indirect mining" strategy for Bitcoin accumulation is a genuinely innovative process that other firms have not replicated. However, process power is difficult to separate from the founders' personal capabilities — it is unclear whether the process would survive the departure of Stahl, Bregman, or Doyle. The firm's minimal employee turnover among senior staff (cited by management as a cultural strength) suggests that some institutional knowledge transfer has occurred, but the degree is difficult to assess externally.

Summary: HKHC is a 2-power business with genuine strength in Counter-Positioning (mainstream asset managers cannot replicate its approach) and Cornered Resource (founding team, relationships, TPL board seat). Switching Costs, Branding, and Process Power are moderate but largely founder-dependent. Scale Economies and Network Effects are weak. The core risk is that the strongest powers are inseparable from the founders — this is a "key person moat" rather than a structural business moat.

5. Porter's 5 Forces

1. Threat of New Entrants Low
While the asset management industry broadly has low barriers to entry (anyone can launch a fund), HKHC's specific niche — contrarian value investing with extreme concentration, 30-year track record, TPL board representation, and a suite of specialized thematic ETFs — is very difficult to replicate. A new entrant would need decades of accumulated relationships, a proven track record through multiple cycles, and the trust of investors willing to tolerate 65% single-stock concentration. The regulatory barriers to launching new ETFs and managing $9.6B AUM also create meaningful hurdles. The most credible competitive threat is not a new entrant but an established asset manager (e.g., a boutique like Tweedy Browne or First Manhattan) extending into HKHC's niche themes.

2. Bargaining Power of Suppliers Low
HKHC's primary "suppliers" are talent (investment professionals), technology infrastructure (trading systems, portfolio accounting), and custodial/brokerage platforms for ETF distribution. The firm's 81-employee team is lean, and the founders' minimal base salaries (relying on performance-based compensation) keep fixed labor costs low. Technology infrastructure for a fundamental research-driven asset manager is largely commoditized. The main supplier cost pressure comes from third-party platform fees for ETF distribution — external brokerage platforms charge recurring fees to list HKHC's ETFs, which structurally suppress margins on registered fund products. However, the firm's strategy of grassroots marketing to bypass platform fees mitigates this pressure.

3. Bargaining Power of Buyers Moderate
HKHC's client base includes high-net-worth individuals, family offices, and institutional investors in separately managed accounts, plus retail investors in ETFs and mutual funds. For SMA clients, buyer power is moderated by the tax-related switching costs discussed above — clients with large unrealized gains in concentrated positions face substantial tax liability if they leave. For ETF investors, buyer power is higher (they can sell any day), but the specialized thematic focus (inflation beneficiaries, blockchain, Japan owner-operators) means there are few direct substitutes. The firm's single largest source of AUM concentration is TPL exposure across client accounts, meaning that any client "choosing" to leave HKHC would face the question of whether to sell a position they likely love. Overall, buyer power is moderate — mitigated by tax lock-in and specialized product offerings.

4. Threat of Substitutes Moderate
The primary substitutes for HKHC's services are: (a) passive index funds and ETFs (which attract AUM away from active managers structurally), (b) other active boutique managers with contrarian or value-oriented philosophies, and (c) direct investing by sophisticated individuals who could replicate HKHC's concentrated approach themselves (buying TPL, Bitcoin, and financial exchanges directly). The passive index fund threat is real but somewhat muted because HKHC's investment themes — owner-operators, spin-offs, dormant assets — are explicitly designed to target segments that indexes systematically miss. The "do-it-yourself" substitution threat is higher, particularly for investors who recognize that HKHC's alpha is heavily concentrated in TPL and could simply buy TPL stock directly rather than paying management fees.

5. Competitive Rivalry Moderate
The boutique asset management industry is fragmented, with numerous firms competing for investor capital. However, HKHC operates in a highly differentiated niche that reduces direct competitive rivalry. Within the narrow segment of contrarian, owner-operator-focused, hard-asset-oriented asset managers, few firms compete directly: GAMCO Investors (Mario Gabelli's vehicle) is the closest public comp in terms of philosophy and founder-centrism, but manages different strategies. Diamond Hill, Virtus, and Silvercrest compete for similar investor demographics but with conventional diversified approaches. The competitive intensity is moderate — HKHC's unique combination of TPL concentration, crypto exposure, and thematic ETFs has no direct peer, but the firm competes broadly with every alternative investment manager for investor allocations.

Summary: The competitive environment is moderately favorable for HKHC. Low barriers to entry (in its specific niche) and low supplier power create a favorable operating backdrop. The primary structural headwinds are moderate buyer power (mitigated by tax lock-in) and the ongoing threat from passive index fund substitution. Competitive rivalry is moderate — HKHC's differentiated approach insulates it from direct head-to-head competition but does not protect it from the broader secular shift toward passive investing.

6. What Drives the Stock?

The following are the 6 variables that would move HKHC's share price materially in the next 12 months. Given the stock's extreme illiquidity, even modest changes in fundamentals can produce outsized price moves.

1. Texas Pacific Land Corporation (TPL) Stock Price. BULLISH: TPL ↑ / BEARISH: TPL ↓
TPL constitutes 65% of HKHC's $9.6B AUM and ~$49.7M of on-balance-sheet corporate investments. Every 10% move in TPL's stock price translates to approximately $624M in AUM change and ~$4.7M in annual management fee revenue impact. TPL is simultaneously HKHC's greatest source of operating leverage and its greatest single-stock risk. The AI data center thesis (Bolt Data & Energy, water resources for cooling) provides a new growth narrative for TPL beyond traditional Permian Basin royalties, but a Permian Basin slowdown or regulatory setback could reverse recent gains.

2. Performance Fee Realization. HIGHLY BINARY
Performance fees were $51.7M in 2024 and ~$0 in 2025. The presence or absence of incentive fees can double or halve HKHC's operating income in any given year. The key variables are: (a) fund-level returns exceeding high-water marks, (b) concentrated position appreciation in private funds, and (c) the timing of fee crystallization. Any quarter where HKHC announces material performance fees would be a significant positive catalyst — the market is currently pricing the stock on normalized management fees only.

3. Bitcoin and Cryptocurrency Prices. BULLISH: BTC ↑ / BEARISH: BTC ↓
HKHC holds Bitcoin directly on its balance sheet, manages crypto mining through CMSG, and runs the Blockchain Development ETF. Bitcoin price appreciation flows through three channels: (a) unrealized gains on corporate BTC holdings, (b) higher AUM in crypto-linked strategies, and (c) increased revenue from CMSG mining operations. Q4 2025 included $29M of aggregate unrealized investment losses heavily driven by Bitcoin-linked exposure. Crypto volatility directly impacts quarterly earnings and book value.

4. ETF Net Flows and New Product Launches. BULLISH: NET INFLOWS / BEARISH: REDEMPTIONS
The ETF platform ($1.5B+ AUM) is HKHC's primary diversification engine away from TPL. INFL gathered $89M in net inflows in 2025, and management is launching new ETFs (7th product expected in 2026). Sustained positive ETF flows would demonstrate organic growth capability independent of TPL, improve revenue quality, and support a higher valuation multiple. Conversely, ETF outflows would signal that the platform has not reached self-sustaining critical mass.

5. Uplisting Progress or Secondary Offering. BULLISH: EXCHANGE LISTING / BEARISH: DILUTIVE OFFERING
Management has openly discussed the need for a national exchange listing and acknowledged the trading volume deficit. Any announcement of a planned uplisting (NYSE, NASDAQ) or a secondary offering to increase float would be a major catalyst — positively if structured as a non-dilutive uplisting, potentially negatively if it involves significant new share issuance. The OTCQX upgrade in September 2025 was a step in this direction.

6. Resolution of Material Accounting Weaknesses. BULLISH: REMEDIATED / BEARISH: PERSISTS OR WORSENS
The firm's disclosed material weaknesses in financial reporting controls are an overhang that prevents institutional investor engagement and exchange uplisting. Successful remediation — demonstrated through a clean audit opinion on 2025 financials — would remove a key obstacle to institutional capital inflows. Conversely, any additional restatement or expanded scope of material weakness disclosure would damage credibility and delay uplisting indefinitely.

Key themes synthesized from AlphaSense competitive intelligence, management commentary, and industry research.

Proprietary Research Moat: The Anti-Index Investment Factory. The most distinctive insight from the AlphaSense research is that Horizon Kinetics has deliberately built a business designed to exploit the structural flaws of passive indexation. The firm's research team identifies companies with "predictive attributes" — owner-operated structures, spin-off scenarios, dormant assets — that are systematically excluded from or underweighted in traditional indexes. The "Advisor-Only" Reality vs. GAAP Fog. A critical misunderstanding of HKHC stems from its GAAP financial statements, which consolidate certain proprietary funds as variable interest entities (VIEs). This consolidation inflates total assets from ~$400M (advisor-only) to ~$2B (GAAP), inflates liabilities by ~$1.56B of redeemable noncontrolling interests, and eliminates ~$7-10M of intercompany management fees from revenue.
