OppFi Inc | Preliminary Memo for Theia
1. Business Overview

OppFi Inc (OPFI) is a Chicago-based fintech lender founded in 2012 that provides near-prime personal installment loans — branded OppLoans — to approximately 150 million underserved American consumers who are declined by mainstream banks and priced out of prime credit markets. Typical borrowers have FICO scores in the 550–700 range and face urgent liquidity needs (car repairs, medical bills, unexpected expenses); OppFi offers $500–$4,000 loans at 36–160% APR with 9–36 month terms, positioned as a structured alternative to payday loans and predatory lenders. The company operates exclusively through a bank-partner origination model — loans are originated by regulated bank partners (FinWise Bank, First Electronic Bank, Blue Ridge Bank) and subsequently purchased by OppFi, allowing OppFi to operate in 37 states without a direct lending license while maintaining compliance under federal preemption doctrine. OppFi is led by CEO Jared Kaplan (named in 2023), generated $597M in net revenue in FY2025 (net of credit losses), and trades at a significant discount to book value and intrinsic value based on analyst consensus of $14.33 price target — a 73% premium to current price of $8.28.

The company's addressable market is structurally large and persistent: roughly 150M Americans are locked out of prime credit, creating a multi-billion dollar market for responsible near-prime credit. OppFi's differentiation lies in its proprietary AI/ML underwriting (OppFi Score), which uses hundreds of alternative data points to approve creditworthy borrowers who score poorly on traditional FICO, and its low-cost digital acquisition model that has driven customer acquisition costs well below traditional bank and payday lender benchmarks. The business generates high cash flow (64% FCF margin on net revenue), has a clean balance sheet, and has grown net revenue at a 13.5% CAGR in FY2025. The stock has been under pressure (-27% in 6 months) due to macro concerns about consumer credit quality and investor uncertainty around CFPB regulatory risk — both of which appear to be misunderstood at current valuation levels.

Valuation Summary

OppFi currently trades at 4.3x FY26E P/E and 4.4x FY26E EV/EBITDA, representing a 55–65% discount to the specialty finance peer group median of approximately 10x P/E and 10x EV/EBITDA. The current discount reflects three investor concerns: (1) the FY2025 GAAP net income of $26M (versus $91M in FY2024) was depressed by a large non-cash Tax Receivable Agreement charge, which creates the misleading appearance of earnings collapse; (2) CFPB regulatory risk around bank-partnership origination models and "rent-a-bank" litigation has cast a shadow over the entire vertical; and (3) macro concerns about near-prime consumer credit quality as interest rates stayed elevated through 2025. However, EBIT was $177M in FY2025 (stable), FCF was $382M (best-ever, 64% margin), and consensus calls for EPS of $1.94 in FY2026 as the TRA drag reverses — placing the stock at a 4.3x forward P/E. At current levels, OppFi appears deeply discounted: a conservative 8–10x P/E on FY2026E EPS implies a stock price of $15.50–$19.40, consistent with the analyst consensus price target of $14.33. The valuation is compelling given the business quality, strong cash generation, and durable near-prime lending moat.

3. Bull & Bear Cases
Bull Cases
Regulatory Tailwind — CFPB Reset

The Trump administration's restructuring of the CFPB in 2025 has dramatically reduced the regulatory overhang that suppressed OPFI's valuation for two years. Enforcement actions targeting "rent-a-bank" models have been deprioritized; state-level legal challenges have stalled. As regulatory clarity returns, the discount assigned to bank-partnership lending models could compress significantly, re-rating OPFI toward 8–10x P/E from its current 4.3x.

TRA Reversal — Earnings Power Revealed

FY2025 GAAP net income of $26M badly misrepresented economic earnings due to a large non-cash Tax Receivable Agreement liability charge. FY2026E consensus EPS of $1.94 strips out this distortion, implying a stock at 4.3x true-earnings — a valuation that would be alarming for a consumer financial with 64% FCF margins. When the market fully digests the TRA mechanics post-Q4 2025 earnings call, the re-rating could be swift.

Rate Cuts Improve Net Interest Margin

OppFi's cost of funds on its senior secured credit facilities is floating-rate (SOFR + spread), while loan yields are largely fixed at origination. Even modest Fed rate cuts (50–100bps) in 2026 expand net interest spread immediately, flowing directly to EBITDA with minimal lag. Management guided to incremental margin improvement of 150–200bps for every 100bps of rate cuts.

AI Underwriting Advantage Compounds

OppFi's proprietary OppFi Score incorporates 300+ alternative data variables (employment tenure, gig income volatility, bank account cash flow patterns) versus FICO's single-factor approach. As the model accumulates 13+ years of near-prime repayment data through multiple credit cycles, the accuracy advantage widens. Better underwriting means lower credit losses at comparable yields, allowing margin expansion while competitors price risk less precisely.

Product Expansion — OppFi Card & SalaryTap

OppFi Card (secured credit card) and SalaryTap (payroll-deducted installment loans) are still sub-1% of revenue but growing. SalaryTap's payroll-deduction mechanic dramatically reduces default risk and could be the most important product in the company's history if adoption scales. Each successful cross-sell extends customer LTV and reduces reliance on new customer acquisition.

Bear Cases
Bank Partnership Model Threatened

OppFi's legal structure depends on bank-partner origination (FinWise, First Electronic, Blue Ridge) to export favorable rate preemptions to states with rate caps. Successful "true lender" litigation — arguing that OppFi, not the bank, is the de facto lender — could require OppFi to comply with state usury laws and dramatically reduce allowable APRs in its most important states. A successful challenge in even 5–6 states could reduce addressable originations by 20–30%.

Consumer Credit Deterioration

Near-prime borrowers are among the first to show stress in a consumer recession. OppFi's net charge-off rate rose to ~55% of gross revenue in stressed periods. If unemployment ticks up 100–200bps above current levels, OppFi's provision expense could spike materially, compressing EBITDA. The company has limited ability to tighten credit fast enough to avoid elevated losses on already-originated loans.

Low Barrier Competitive Pressure

The near-prime installment lending market is not truly winner-take-all. LendingClub, Avant, NetCredit, and regional credit unions all compete for the same 550–700 FICO borrowers. If a better-capitalized fintech or bank decides to aggressively price this segment, OppFi's take rate compression could be severe. Its cost advantage is real but not absolute, and the product is fundamentally a commodity (cash).

Founder Float Compression / Dual-Class Overhang

The Schwartz family controls ~68% of economic/voting interest through Class V shares and has historically been slow to convert and sell. This structure creates a persistent discount to NAV, limits institutional index inclusion (S&P eligibility requires float requirements), and reduces the stock's liquidity profile. If the family converts and sells Class V shares to raise capital, the increased supply could further depress the stock.

Interest Rate Reversal — Funding Cost Squeeze

OppFi's warehouse credit facilities are variable-rate. If rates remain elevated or increase unexpectedly, the spread between fixed loan yields and floating funding costs compresses further. With ~$538M in total debt, each 100bps increase in funding rates adds approximately $5.4M in annual interest expense — manageable, but directionally negative and a source of earnings volatility.

4. Helmer's 7 Powers
1. Scale Economies Moderate

OppFi benefits from meaningful scale advantages in two dimensions. First, its fixed-cost technology and underwriting infrastructure (the OppFi platform, data pipelines, AI models) is largely fixed — marginal cost of underwriting a new loan is near-zero once the model is built, whereas a smaller entrant pays the same R&D per unit with far fewer loans over which to amortize. Second, OppFi's 13-year history of near-prime repayment data creates a training dataset that a new entrant cannot replicate regardless of capital. The barrier is moderate, not strong, because the platform itself does not have the cost-per-unit advantage of a payment network or exchange — loan processing cost scales roughly linearly with origination volume above a certain threshold. Still, OppFi's cost per loan originated is substantially below what a startup would incur to build comparable underwriting capability from scratch.

2. Network Effects Weak

Near-prime installment lending does not benefit from direct or indirect network effects in the traditional sense. OppFi's 300,000+ active borrowers do not generate more value for each other's borrowing experience. There is a mild data network effect — more borrowers generate more repayment data, which improves model accuracy, which improves credit decisions, which reduces losses — but this is a second-order data advantage, not a true network effect. A competitor with access to a large alternative dataset (a payroll processor, a major bank) could build comparable model accuracy more quickly than OppFi could build comparable origination volume. This power is genuinely weak for a product that is essentially a bilateral loan transaction.

3. Counter-Positioning Strong

OppFi's counter-positioning against traditional banks is structural and durable. A JPMorgan or Wells Fargo could theoretically serve near-prime borrowers — but doing so would require them to (a) underwrite to non-FICO criteria, threatening their credit culture and regulatory standing; (b) charge 36–160% APR, which would generate severe consumer advocate and regulatory backlash; (c) accept dramatically higher provision expense, reducing ROTCE on the product below their cost of equity. The rational incumbent response is to NOT enter, which is exactly what the market shows — no major bank competes with OppFi in this segment. This is a textbook counter-positioning dynamic: the incumbents could copy but won't, because the economics would damage their core franchise. OppFi exists in the gap that the incumbents have voluntarily ceded by choosing to serve prime borrowers at lower yields with lower risk — a choice that is deeply embedded in their operating models, capital allocation frameworks, and regulatory relationships.

4. Switching Costs Moderate

Borrower switching costs are moderate and somewhat unusual in structure. OppFi's borrowers typically have limited access to alternatives — being declined by prime lenders is the reason they came to OppFi in the first place. For a borrower who has successfully repaid an OppFi loan, their OppFi repayment history is invisible to FICO-dependent competitors (it doesn't appear on credit reports in a usable way). Critically, OppFi's relationship loyalty data — showing which customers repay reliably — is proprietary, allowing OppFi to offer returning customers meaningfully better pricing and larger loan amounts than it would offer a new customer. This creates a sticky repeat-borrower dynamic: returning customers have better terms at OppFi than they could get anywhere else, because only OppFi knows their actual repayment performance. Q4 2025 data showed approximately 40% of originations were repeat borrowers, a figure management is actively trying to grow through the OppFi loyalty program.

5. Branding Weak

OppFi does not compete on brand in the traditional sense. Near-prime borrowers facing an urgent cash need are primarily price-sensitive and availability-sensitive — they want credit, not an aspirational brand relationship. The "OppLoans" name has recognition in the segment, and the company has invested in its reputation as a responsible lender (the Fair Access to Credit Standard, fee transparency, credit reporting for successful borrowers), but this generates goodwill rather than brand premium. The company's unit economics are driven by digital acquisition efficiency, not brand pull. For B2C lending, branding is simply not a meaningful competitive moat.

6. Cornered Resource Moderate

OppFi has two meaningful cornered resources. First, its proprietary 13-year near-prime credit performance dataset — covering hundreds of thousands of borrowers with full repayment histories across the 2020 COVID shock, 2022 inflation surge, and 2023 rate spike — cannot be replicated by a new entrant. This dataset trains the OppFi Score model in ways that cannot be purchased from any data vendor. Second, OppFi's bank partnerships (FinWise, First Electronic, Blue Ridge) are not truly exclusive but represent relationship assets that are difficult for new entrants to replicate quickly — regulated bank partnerships for consumer lending require FDIC approval, significant due diligence, and multi-year relationship development. Neither resource is unassailable (a well-funded competitor could build similar data over a decade, and other fintechs have secured bank partnerships), but together they provide a meaningful head start.

7. Process Power Moderate

OppFi's organizational capability in alternative-data underwriting, bank-partnership compliance management, and state-by-state regulatory navigation represents genuine process power. Managing a lending product that operates legally across 37 states under federal preemption requires deep institutional expertise in regulatory compliance, state AG relationships, and legal defense — knowledge that is embedded across the company's legal, compliance, and operations teams. Additionally, OppFi's underwriting team has iterated the OppFi Score through multiple credit cycles, building intuitions about near-prime borrower behavior that cannot be codified in a playbook. CEO Jared Kaplan's background (previously at Goldman Sachs and a consumer lending startup) and the management team's tenure suggests this capability persists beyond any single individual.

Summary: OppFi's strongest and most durable moats are Counter-Positioning (banks will not compete) and a combination of Cornered Resource + Process Power in alternative underwriting. Switching Costs and Scale Economies provide incremental reinforcement. The absence of Network Effects and Brand is typical for a B2C lending business and not a fundamental weakness — just a ceiling on how high the competitive barriers can ultimately be.

5. Porter's 5 Forces
1. Threat of New Entrants Moderate

Entry into near-prime installment lending is technically feasible (no spectrum license or physical asset required) but practically difficult. A credible entrant must: (1) secure a bank partner willing to originate under a program agreement and accept FDIC scrutiny; (2) build or buy alternative-data underwriting capability; (3) fund a loan portfolio — requiring warehouse credit facilities from institutional lenders who want to see management track records; (4) acquire borrowers at scale, competing against OppFi's optimized digital marketing funnel. Steps 1 and 3 together typically take 18–36 months for a well-capitalized startup. The more significant barrier is step 2 — building accurate near-prime underwriting requires years of origination data and multiple economic cycle exposure. Google or Amazon could conceivably enter this market using their proprietary data, which would be a severe threat, but neither has shown interest in consumer credit risk. Well-funded fintechs like Avant and LendingClub already exist in this space — the market is competitive but not freely enterable. Net: moderate barrier, with the caveat that Big Tech entry would be a high-impact, low-probability tail risk.

2. Bargaining Power of Suppliers Moderate

OppFi's primary "supplier" is funding — the warehouse credit facilities and securitization markets that provide the capital to fund loans. As a non-bank, OppFi cannot take deposits (unlike a bank competitor) and must access institutional debt markets, which creates funding cost exposure. During the 2022–2023 rate spike, SOFR-linked funding costs rose sharply, compressing margins. However, OppFi has three bank partners (not one), and has accessed the ABS (asset-backed securities) market for term funding at fixed rates, reducing dependence on revolving warehouse lines. Its secondary supplier — bank partners themselves — has moderate power: FinWise or Blue Ridge could potentially exit a partnership agreement, which would require OppFi to qualify replacement banks. This is manageable but not costless. Net: suppliers have meaningful leverage given OppFi's non-deposit funding model, but the risk is manageable given multiple funding sources and improving ABS market access.

3. Bargaining Power of Buyers Low

Individual borrowers have very limited bargaining power. A near-prime consumer seeking $1,500 cannot negotiate the APR on an OppFi loan — take it or leave it pricing is standard. Borrower alternatives are limited: payday loans (higher cost, shorter term), credit cards (require higher credit scores than these borrowers typically have), family/friends, or going without. The concentration is also favorable — no single borrower represents more than a rounding error in OppFi's $1.5B+ receivables book. The only meaningful buyer-side pressure comes from competition: if a competing lender (Avant, NetCredit) offers a better rate, that borrower can shop elsewhere, which is why OppFi's customer acquisition economics are driven more by digital marketing efficiency than by pricing power. But this is competitive rivalry, not buyer bargaining power. In aggregate, buyers have very low power over OppFi's pricing.

4. Threat of Substitutes Moderate

The most meaningful substitutes for OppFi borrowers are payday loans (worse product: shorter term, balloon repayment, even higher effective APR), secured credit cards (require positive credit history), pawn loans (require physical collateral), BNPL (different use case: point-of-sale, not emergency cash), and employer-sponsored earned wage access products (Earnin, DailyPay). The emergence of Earned Wage Access (EWA) platforms is a meaningful watch item — if EWA penetration grows significantly among the gig and hourly workforce, it could reduce the frequency of emergency installment loan needs. However, EWA typically addresses temporary liquidity gaps of $200–$500, while OppFi's average loan is $1,500 with a 12–18 month term — a different use case. Net: substitutes exist but are largely inferior products for OppFi's core use case; EWA is the most plausible long-term disruptor but serves a different immediate need.

5. Competitive Rivalry Moderate

The near-prime installment lending market is fragmented and competitive. Key competitors include: Avant (private, ~$700M in annual originations), NetCredit/Enova ($1.8B market cap), LendingPoint (private), Possible Finance (smaller, app-native), and regional credit unions expanding into installment lending. Competition is primarily on digital acquisition cost (Google/Facebook CPL), APR pricing at the margin, and loan amount. The market is growing fast enough (the 150M underserved Americans represent enormous unmet demand) that competition has not been destructive to pricing — APRs have remained in the 36–160% range across the industry. The most important competitive dynamic is not peer-to-peer rivalry but rather whether a new technology platform (AI-native, not a legacy lender retrofitting ML onto FICO) can outcompete OppFi on underwriting accuracy. No such competitor has yet emerged at scale. Net: rivalry is present but not currently destructive to economics.

Summary: The competitive environment is moderately favorable for OppFi. The most favorable forces are low buyer power and manageable substitutes. The most significant risks are supplier (funding) power during rate spikes, ongoing competitive rivalry, and the tail risk of new entrants with superior data assets (Big Tech). OppFi's structural legal advantage (bank partnership preemption) and data advantage (13 years of credit cycle data) are the key asymmetric defenses.

6. What Drives the Stock?

1. Net Charge-Off Rate vs. Consensus. This is the single most important monthly/quarterly variable for OPFI's stock. Net charge-offs (NCOs) as a percentage of average receivables represent OppFi's realized credit losses — the difference between the high gross yield (avg ~160% APR) and net revenue. Q4 2025 NCO rate was ~51%, within management's guided 50–55% range. Any NCO print below 50% is strongly bullish (implies underwriting improvement and better net revenue); above 58% is bearish (credit deterioration, provision spike). Analysts model EPS to within $0.10 per share based on NCO assumptions alone.

2. CFPB / True Lender Regulatory Headline Risk. Any new CFPB enforcement action, state AG lawsuit against a rent-a-bank structure, or federal court ruling on "true lender" doctrine can move OPFI ±15% in a session. Conversely, regulatory clarity (court dismissals, favorable rulings, CFPB de-prioritization) would catalyze a re-rating. The Trump administration's CFPB restructuring in 2025 has been net positive, but this is a persistent background risk that depresses the multiple.

3. Earnings Quality / TRA Resolution. FY2025 GAAP EPS of $0.99 was severely impacted by non-cash TRA (Tax Receivable Agreement) charges that are mathematically temporary and non-cash. The market has been slow to strip this out and value OppFi on normalized earnings. As Q1–Q2 2026 prints show $1.94+ annualized EPS run-rates, investor confusion about earnings quality should resolve, potentially driving a multiple re-rating from 4.3x to 7–8x P/E.

4. Origination Volume Growth. Origination volume (total new loans funded in a quarter) is the leading indicator for revenue 12–18 months forward (given loan durations). Q4 2025 originations grew 8% YoY. Acceleration above 10–12% would signal OppFi is taking share, gaining marketing efficiency, or benefiting from rate cuts expanding eligible borrower pool. Deceleration below 5% would raise concerns about demand destruction or tightened underwriting.

5. Repeat Borrower Penetration. Returning borrowers are OppFi's most profitable customer cohort — zero CAC, proven repayment history, higher loan amounts, and better pricing. Management target is 40–50% repeat borrower mix (currently ~40%). Each percentage point increase in repeat mix meaningfully improves blended acquisition economics and NCO rates, creating a compounding quality improvement in the portfolio.

6. Rate Environment / Funding Cost. OppFi's senior secured warehouse facilities are SOFR-linked floating rate. Every 100bps of Fed rate reduction saves approximately $5.4M annually in funding costs and expands net interest margin. Given the Fed's 2025–2026 cutting cycle, this is a tailwind. Any reversal (rate hike, credit spread widening) in institutional lending markets would compress margins and hit consensus EPS estimates.

8. AlphaSense Insights
AlphaSense DR Report · Query: "OppFi Inc near-prime lending competitive dynamics underwriting" · Report Date: March 16, 2026 · Sources: Earnings transcripts, expert calls, sell-side research, regulatory filings

The AI Underwriting Advantage: More Durable Than Investors Appreciate. Expert call evidence from the AlphaSense report reveals that OppFi's underwriting advantage is substantially more durable than the sell-side typically credits. Former credit risk executives from competing lenders described the OppFi Score's "behavioral signal extraction" — specifically its use of bank account transaction flow data (not bank statements, but live ACH data with permissioned access) — as "genuinely proprietary" and "not reproducible from FICO + bureau data alone." One expert from a competing near-prime lender noted that when his firm attempted to replicate similar behavioral underwriting in 2023, they found the primary bottleneck was not model architecture but training data — specifically, the lack of through-cycle repayment performance data for near-prime borrowers who had been tracked by OppFi since 2011–2012. The COVID cohort (2020–2021 originations) was described as particularly valuable: "Any model that survived COVID intact, on near-prime borrowers, has empirical validation that you simply cannot shortcut." This supports a stronger "Cornered Resource" rating than the FactSet summary alone would suggest.

Regulatory Environment: A Genuine Turning Point. Multiple sources in the AlphaSense report — including a former OCC official and a fintech compliance attorney — described the post-2025 CFPB restructuring as a "regime change, not a policy adjustment." Enforcement staff attrition at the CFPB has been significant (30–40% reduction per one source), and the new leadership's stated posture is "guidance and education first, enforcement last." For OppFi specifically, the collapse of the Madden v. Midland Funding line of cases under the Trump-era OCC rules means the preemption basis for bank-partnership lending is more secure than at any point since 2021. However, experts cautioned that state attorneys general — particularly in New York, California, and Illinois — have independently increased scrutiny of "rent-a-bank" structures. This state-level risk is structurally different from CFPB risk and cannot be fully addressed by federal regulatory changes alone. The key watch item is a pending Illinois AG action that could, if decided against OppFi's structure, require a restructuring of operations in Illinois (approximately 8% of originations).

Near-Prime Consumer Resilience: Better Than Macro Headlines Suggest. Expert evidence from credit bureau analysts and former bank credit risk officers in the report described near-prime consumer performance as "bifurcated but not broadly deteriorating" as of early 2026. Borrowers with stable employment in services and healthcare (a large portion of OppFi's customer base) have maintained repayment rates; the stress has been concentrated in gig economy workers and construction-adjacent employment categories. OppFi's own Q4 2025 NCO rate of ~51% was described by one analyst as "better than we expected given October–November macro noise," and management's guided 50–55% NCO range for FY2026 was characterized as conservative. The key watch item is unemployment — experts consistently cited 5.5% U3 unemployment as the threshold above which near-prime NCOs historically spike to 60%+ levels.

SalaryTap: Underrated Option Value. The AlphaSense report contained two expert calls specifically about SalaryTap, OppFi's payroll-deduction installment product. The product — where loan repayments are deducted directly from a borrower's paycheck, fully eliminating voluntary default risk — was described by a former employer benefits consultant as "the single most credit-safe consumer lending structure I have seen outside of 401k loans." The NCO rate on SalaryTap originations is estimated at 8–12% (vs. 51% on standard OppLoans), which would imply a dramatically higher net yield. OppFi's penetration of SalaryTap is currently limited by employer partnership development — convincing HR departments to integrate the product into payroll systems is a long sales cycle. But one expert noted that "every employer HR department I have talked to about this product has been very receptive — the concept of offering an alternative to payday loans as an employee benefit resonates, particularly for hourly workers." If SalaryTap reaches even 10% of originations with an 8% NCO rate, the blended portfolio economics improve materially. This is an underappreciated option value in the current valuation.
