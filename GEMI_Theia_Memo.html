Gemini Space Station | Preliminary Memo for Theia
1. Business Overview

Gemini Space Station, Inc. (NASDAQ: GEMI) is the holding company for Gemini, the regulated U.S. crypto platform founded in 2014 by Cameron and Tyler Winklevoss. Gemini built its brand on "asking for permission, not forgiveness": it runs a NYDFS-chartered limited-purpose trust company (custody and the GUSD stablecoin), holds money transmitter licenses in every state that requires them, and has added a CFTC Designated Contract Market (Gemini Titan, December 2025), a CFTC Derivatives Clearing Organization (Gemini Olympus, April 2026) and a FINRA broker-dealer (Gemini Galactic). Since the February 2026 "Gemini 2.0" pivot, the business is organized into four lines: Invest & Trade (spot exchange, OTC and — since July 2026 — commission-free U.S. stocks and ETFs cleared through Apex); Onchain (custody, staking, GUSD, self-custody wallet); Card (the WebBank-issued Mastercard credit card that pays rewards in crypto, funded by a Ripple warehouse facility); and Predictions (CFTC-regulated event contracts on crypto, sports, politics and economics, now cleared in-house). Management's stated ambition is "the super app for the markets economy" — over 5,000 tradable instruments versus fewer than 100 two years ago.

The economics have shifted sharply. Spot exchange volume collapsed from $11.3B in Q2 2025 to $3.8B in Q2 2026 (−66%) and assets on platform fell from $18.2B to $8.4B, so exchange revenue dropped 38% to $12.5M. Offsetting that, the card has become the largest revenue line ($16.2M in Q2 2026, +231% YoY, on $219.6M of managed receivables), and services plus interest income reached 59% of net revenue. Total revenue was $179.6M in FY2025 (+26%) and $45.5M in Q2 2026 (+37% YoY), but the cost base remains far larger than the revenue base: FY2025 operating expenses were $525.2M, the net loss was $(582.8)M and adjusted EBITDA was $(258.0)M. The IPO on September 12, 2025 priced at $28 (raising $425M, plus a $50M Nasdaq strategic investment at $26.25). Within five months management exited the UK, EU and Australia, cut ~25% of staff (headcount has since fallen from ~650 to ~402) and, on February 17, 2026, the CFO, COO and Chief Legal Officer all departed the same day. In May 2026 the Winklevoss Capital Fund injected $100M of equity — paid in ~1,258 BTC at $14 per share — to shore up liquidity. Cash stood at $188.6M on June 30, 2026, against H1 2026 operating cash burn of $(105.9)M.

At $3.12 the stock is down 89% from its IPO price and trades at roughly 2x FY2026E revenue and 0.86x book value, with 27% of the float sold short. The equity is now effectively a call option on three things: (1) whether prediction markets — where Gemini owns a rare vertically integrated DCM + DCO stack and signed an August 2026 letter of intent to be the exclusive CFTC venue for crypto event contracts across Apex Fintech's broker network — become a scaled revenue line (they produced only ~$0.5M of revenue in Q2 2026); (2) whether the card can be grown profitably after a Q2 identity-fraud event pushed 30+ day delinquencies to 9.4%; and (3) whether the company can reach breakeven before it needs to raise dilutive capital again (a $100M shelf was filed in October 2026).

Valuation Summary

Gemini trades at 2.2x FY2026E revenue and ~2.0x EV/Sales after netting $189M of cash and $331M of crypto against $481M of debt — the cheapest revenue multiple among scaled U.S. crypto platforms (net-revenue peer median ~5.9x EV/Sales) and below book value (0.86x). The discount is earned. Revenue is barely growing in 2026 (+3% consensus) as spot volumes have fallen two-thirds; the cost base still runs at roughly 2.5x revenue (Q2 2026 opex $122.4M vs. revenue $45.5M); adjusted EBITDA has been $(52)–(92)M every quarter for six quarters; and H1 2026 operating cash burn of $(106)M against $189M of cash implies the company needs new capital within roughly a year unless burn falls sharply. Stock-based compensation guided at $100–115M for FY2026 would equal ~25% of today's market cap, so dilution is structural, not optional. The consensus target of $5.23 (10 brokers, rated Hold) implies 68% upside, but the target trend is down (Truist cut to $3.50, Morgan Stanley to Underweight at $4.75), and 27% of the float is sold short. The bull case rests on the prediction-markets stack (DCM + DCO + Apex distribution) and the card franchise being worth more than the market's ~$370M EV — an option the market is pricing at roughly the value of the company's cash and crypto minus its debt.

3. Bull & Bear Cases
Bull Cases

A Full-Stack Prediction Market at a Sub-$400M EV. Gemini is one of very few crypto-native firms holding both a CFTC DCM (Titan, December 2025) and a DCO (Olympus, April 2026), and its in-house clearinghouse went live on August 4, 2026 — so it captures the exchange fee, the clearing fee and the float on collateral without paying a third-party clearer. Event contracts traded rose 93% QoQ in Q2 2026 to 225M+ cumulative across 27,000+ traders, and the August 2026 Apex Fintech LOI would make Titan the exclusive CFTC venue for crypto event contracts distributed across Apex's FCM network of brokerages. Kalshi's monthly volume went from $9B to $33B in the first half of 2026 alone; if Gemini captures even a low-single-digit share of U.S. regulated event-contract volume, predictions alone could justify more than today's ~$370M EV.

The Card Is a Real, Growing Annuity. Card revenue went from $11.6M in FY2024 to $33.1M in FY2025 and $16.2M in Q2 2026 alone (+231% YoY), on $219.6M of managed receivables — a ~30% annualized revenue yield. Card pre-provision net revenue turned positive ($3.8M in Q1, $5.5M in Q2), card MTUs exceed 100k, and 55% of prediction traders also hold the card. Excluding the identity-fraud cohort, 30+ day delinquency was 3.3% — in line with prime card issuers — and the company has added Experian bust-out models and tighter identity verification. The card gives Gemini what no other crypto exchange has at scale: a daily-use consumer product with credit-card economics that feeds deposits, staking and trading.

The Cost Reset Is Already in the Run-Rate. Total opex fell from $171.7M in Q4 2025 to $122.4M in Q2 2026 (−29%), headcount from ~650 to ~402 (−38%), and Q2 included a one-off $16.1M fraud provision. Management narrowed FY2026 technology + G&A to $155–170M and guided cash compensation down 15–20%. On a normalized Q2 base (ex-fraud provision, ex-SBC) cash opex is roughly $86M per quarter; a recovery to the Q3 2025 revenue level ($50M/quarter) with flat costs takes the adjusted EBITDA loss to ~$(35)M a quarter, and a crypto upcycle that doubles exchange revenue would bring Gemini close to breakeven. Operating leverage cuts both ways — in 2026 it has been punishing, but the base is now much smaller.

Founders Keep Writing Checks, Downside Partly Underwritten. The Winklevoss Capital Fund put $100M of BTC into the company at $14 per share in May 2026 — 4.5x today's price — and its loans to Gemini are denominated in BTC (4,419 BTC at June 30) with no fixed maturity. Founders who control ~94% of the vote and own ~63% of the economics have repeatedly chosen to fund rather than sell. The stock trades at 0.86x book and ~2x revenue, against a regulated platform with an NYDFS trust charter, money transmitter licenses in every state, a DCM, a DCO and a broker-dealer — a license stack that took a decade to assemble and that an acquirer (an exchange group, a broker or a prediction-market operator) could not quickly replicate. April 2026 press reports of buyer interest in Gemini or its custody/trading units highlight the strategic floor.

Legal Overhang Is Clearing. The SEC dismissed its Earn case with prejudice in January 2026; Earn customers recovered 100% in kind ($2.18B via Genesis plus Gemini's ~$50M NYAG restitution and $40M NYDFS contribution); and Gemini won its first five bellwether Earn arbitrations, including an August 2026 ruling that it was not at fault for Earn's collapse. The remaining IPO class action (Methvin v. Gemini, S.D.N.Y.) is a typical post-IPO-drawdown suit. With the regulatory regime in Washington now constructive for crypto and prediction markets (the CFTC itself sought to vacate its 2025 consent order), the "regulated-first" positioning that was a cost center for a decade can become a distribution advantage with banks and brokers.

Bear Cases

The Funding Gap Forces Dilution at the Bottom. H1 2026 operating cash flow was $(105.9)M against $188.6M of cash at June 30, and adjusted EBITDA has been $(52)–(92)M in every one of the last six quarters. Even if burn halves, Gemini likely needs another $100–150M within 12 months; a $100M mixed shelf was filed in October 2026 and the WCF placement shares were registered for resale. Raising $150M at $2–3 per share would add 50–75M shares (40–60% dilution) on top of $100–115M of annual SBC — itself equal to roughly a quarter of the market cap. Equity holders can be right about the business and still lose money.

The Core Exchange Is Shrinking Faster Than the Market. Gemini's spot volume fell 66% YoY to $3.8B in Q2 2026 and assets on platform halved to $8.4B, while Robinhood's crypto notional was down ~38% YoY in August and global CEX spot volume was recovering by September. The exit from the UK, EU and Australia removed international users; MTUs have declined for two quarters (601k → 580k) against a 20–25% CAGR target set only nine months earlier. The rising take rate (~33bps derived) reflects a shrinking, more retail-heavy mix rather than pricing power. Coinbase, Robinhood and Kraken have far larger user bases and marketing budgets; Gemini's sub-scale exchange may never recover share.

Card Credit Risk Is the New Volatility. Card receivables of ~$220M are funded by a Ripple warehouse whose rate steps from 7% to 10% if borrowings exceed $150M after July 2026, and which requires Gemini to pledge RLUSD collateral. The Q2 2026 identity-fraud event drove a $16.1M credit-loss provision and lifted 30+ day delinquency to 9.4%; total transaction losses were $20.1M — 44% of quarterly revenue. Unsecured revolving credit to a crypto-native, fraud-targeted customer base is a cyclical business; growing the book requires more warehouse capacity and capital Gemini does not have, and another loss cycle would wipe out the card's pre-provision profit.

Predictions Revenue Is Tiny and Contested. Despite record contract counts, prediction-market revenue was only ~$0.5M in Q2 2026 (reported net of ~20% rebates), and Gemini tripled its contracted market makers and launched three maker/taker rebate programs to buy liquidity. Kalshi and Polymarket dominate volume, Robinhood, CME/FanDuel and DraftKings distribute event contracts to tens of millions of users, and the New York Attorney General sued Gemini Titan in August 2026. An ICE expert notes Gemini's direct-to-user model (no broker intermediaries) limits access to broker customer bases — the Apex LOI addresses this, but it is non-binding and exclusive only for crypto event contracts.

Governance and Execution Credibility. The prospectus sold a global growth story in September 2025; within five months the company exited three regions, cut a quarter of staff and lost its CFO, COO and CLO on the same day — the CFO role remains interim eight months later and the COO role was not backfilled. Securities class actions and a Nevada derivative suit allege the IPO concealed the pivot. The founders hold ~94% of the vote through Class B shares, are also the company's largest lender (BTC-denominated related-party loans of $259M) and its most recent equity investor — a web of related-party relationships in which minority Class A holders have little influence. Truist's analyst said the strategy "needs to change"; Morgan Stanley moved to Underweight in October 2026.

4. Helmer's 7 Powers

1. Scale Economies Weak
Exchanges, custodians and card issuers are fixed-cost businesses where scale drives unit costs, and Gemini lacks scale in every one. Its $3.8B of quarterly spot volume is a rounding error next to Coinbase or Robinhood, its ~$220M card book is tiny relative to any bank issuer, and its operating expenses ($122.4M in Q2 2026) are ~2.7x revenue. The 2026 restructuring cut costs, but cost-cutting is not scale: Gemini is still spreading a full regulated-platform overhead (compliance, security, licensing in every state, a DCM and DCO) across ~580k monthly transacting users. Competitors with 10–20x the user base amortize identical fixed costs far more efficiently.

2. Network Effects Weak
Order-book and prediction-market liquidity both exhibit network effects — traders go where the depth is — but those effects accrue to the market leaders (Coinbase and Binance in spot; Kalshi and Polymarket in event contracts), not to Gemini. Gemini's response — tripling contracted market makers and paying maker/taker rebates — is the classic sign of a venue buying liquidity rather than enjoying it. The Apex distribution LOI is the most credible attempt to manufacture a network effect by aggregating many brokers' order flow on Titan; if executed, it would materially upgrade this rating. Cross-product links (55% of predictors hold the card) are an engagement effect, not a network effect.

3. Counter-Positioning Moderate
Gemini's "regulation-first" model was historically counter-positioned against offshore exchanges (Binance, FTX, Bybit) that could not adopt U.S. trust-company standards without abandoning their most profitable products. In prediction markets, owning both the DCM and the DCO is a modest counter-position against offshore Polymarket-style venues and against broker-distributors who rely on third-party exchanges. But in 2026, with U.S. policy friendly to crypto, the large regulated incumbents (Coinbase, Robinhood, CME) can and do replicate Gemini's model without cannibalizing anything — the counter-positioning advantage has eroded as the industry has converged on regulated U.S. operation.

4. Switching Costs Weak
Retail crypto users switch exchanges easily — balances are portable and Gemini's 66% YoY spot-volume decline and halving of assets on platform show how quickly activity leaves. Custody clients (ETF issuers, treasuries such as Empery Digital) have moderate switching costs given integration and audit work, but custodial fees fell to just $0.6M in Q2 2026. The credit card creates the stickiest relationship: cardholders rarely churn a primary card, and rewards accumulate inside the Gemini account. Overall, switching costs are low for most of the revenue base.

5. Branding Moderate
The Gemini and Winklevoss brands are among the best known in U.S. crypto, associated with security and regulatory compliance, and the card's co-branded editions (XRP, Solana) generated record sign-ups (30k+ in the first month of the XRP card). However, the brand was damaged by the Earn/Genesis collapse (fully remediated only in 2024–2026) and by the post-IPO restructuring, and it does not translate into pricing power: retail users compare fees, and the institutional business is shrinking. Branding helps customer acquisition at the margin; it does not protect economics.

6. Cornered Resource Moderate
Gemini's licensing stack is its most defensible asset: an NYDFS limited-purpose trust charter (qualified custodian, GUSD issuer), money transmitter licenses in all required states, a CFTC DCM and DCO, a FINRA broker-dealer and a Singapore MPI in-principle approval. Assembling the DCM + DCO pair in particular is slow and rare. These licenses are not truly scarce — Coinbase, Kraken, Robinhood and Kalshi hold comparable ones — but they are a real barrier for new entrants and a key reason an acquirer might pay for the platform. Founder capital (the Winklevoss Capital Fund's BTC loans and equity) has also functioned as a cornered funding resource that a typical sub-scale fintech would not have.

7. Process Power Weak
Gemini has a strong track record in security and custody operations (no major hack in over a decade), which reflects genuine operational discipline. But the 2025–2026 sequence — an expansion-oriented IPO followed within months by a three-region exit, ~40% headcount reduction and the simultaneous departure of three C-suite officers, plus a Q2 2026 identity-fraud breach in card underwriting — suggests organizational processes are still being rebuilt. Process power is unproven.

Summary: Gemini is a 0–1 power business today. Its only meaningful advantages are a regulated license stack (Cornered Resource, moderate) — especially the rare DCM + DCO pairing — and a recognized brand. It lacks scale, network effects and switching costs in its core exchange, and the counter-positioning it enjoyed against offshore venues has faded as the industry has moved onshore. The prediction-markets build and the Apex distribution deal are the only plausible routes to a real power (network effects); until they produce volume, Gemini competes on licenses and brand alone.

5. Porter's 5 Forces

1. Threat of New Entrants High
Regulatory licenses are a hurdle, but the threat comes from well-capitalized adjacent players rather than start-ups. In crypto trading, every major broker (Robinhood, Schwab, Fidelity, E*Trade, SoFi) now offers crypto or plans to; in prediction markets, CME (with FanDuel), DraftKings, Robinhood, Interactive Brokers (ForecastEx) and Coinbase have all entered within two years. In cards, Coinbase and others have launched crypto-rewards products. These entrants bring existing customer bases measured in the tens of millions, which Gemini cannot match.

2. Bargaining Power of Suppliers Moderate
Key suppliers are the card issuing bank (WebBank) and network (Mastercard), the warehouse lender (Ripple, which sets a 7–10% rate and RLUSD collateral requirements), clearing partners for equities (Apex), liquidity providers and market makers (who must be paid rebates to quote predictions), and the founders' Winklevoss Capital Fund (lender of BTC). Several of these relationships are concentrated and on terms that favor the supplier — especially the warehouse and market-maker rebates — which limits Gemini's margins.

3. Bargaining Power of Buyers High
Retail traders and institutions are price-sensitive and multi-homed; they move to the venue with the lowest fees, best liquidity or biggest promotions. Gemini's institutional volume fell sharply after 2025, and the elevated take rate reflects a shrinking retail base rather than pricing power. In predictions, traders compare odds and fees across Kalshi, Polymarket and Robinhood instantly. Card customers are stickier but respond to rewards rates (Gemini pays up to 4% back), so economics are competed away into rewards expense.

4. Threat of Substitutes High
Spot BTC/ETH ETFs in brokerage accounts substitute for exchange trading and custody; stablecoin and DeFi venues substitute for on-platform trading and staking; sportsbooks substitute for sports event contracts; and any rewards credit card substitutes for the Gemini card. The products Gemini sells are, almost without exception, available elsewhere from larger providers.

5. Competitive Rivalry High
Gemini competes against Coinbase ($42B market cap), Robinhood ($86B), Kraken (~$13B private), Bullish and Binance in crypto; Kalshi, Polymarket, CME/FanDuel and DraftKings in predictions; and large banks and fintechs in cards. Most rivals are profitable or far better funded, and are spending aggressively on the same customers in a weak crypto market. Rivalry is intense on every product line.

Summary: The competitive environment is unfavorable. Gemini is a sub-scale participant in three highly contested markets (retail crypto trading, prediction markets, rewards cards) with high buyer power, abundant substitutes and much larger rivals. Its licenses keep new start-ups out but not the incumbents that matter. The structural route to better economics is distribution through partners (Apex) and a differentiated, vertically integrated derivatives stack — both still early.

1. Cash Burn and the Next Financing. Gemini 2.0: An Abrupt Pivot That Reset the Equity Story. The AlphaSense primer frames the February 5, 2026 "Gemini 2.0" announcement as "a dramatic corporate transformation that marked an abrupt departure from previous expansion plans": the wind-down of UK, EU and Australian operations (accounts moved to withdrawal-only in March, final shutdown in April), a 25% workforce reduction (~200 employees), lease exits in London, Malta and San Francisco, and restructuring charges of $7.9M in Q1 2026 (~$10.9M total). The Derivatives Stack Is the Strategic Asset. AlphaSense highlights that holding both a DCM (Titan, December 2025) and a DCO (Olympus, April 29, 2026) is "a rare combination in the United States," letting Gemini manage settlement, risk parameters and collateral in-house, eliminate third-party clearing fees and "retain the entire economic value loop." The clearinghouse went live August 4, 2026 for prediction contracts, with plans to extend to crypto futures, options and perpetuals. Volume set consecutive monthly records in Q2 2026 (+93% QoQ in contracts) and surpassed 225M cumulative contracts across 27,000+ traders by July. The August 24, 2026 Apex LOI would make Titan the exclusive CFTC venue for crypto event contracts distributed through Apex's FCM network. The counterpoint, from a former ICE Senior Director in the expert calls, is that Gemini's direct-to-end-user model (without broker intermediaries) speeds licensing but "restricts Gemini's ability to partner with brokers who maintain large established customer bases, severely limiting platform liquidity" — exactly the gap the Apex deal is meant to close.

Most Misunderstood Aspect: Gemini Is Becoming a Card and Services Company. The expert-call report argues the market still views Gemini as a volatile, spot-fee-dependent exchange, while services and interest grew 117% YoY to 59% of net revenue in Q2 2026. Credit card revenue rose 231% YoY to $16.2M on $219.6M of receivables, fueled by a 164% increase in card MTUs, partly offset by an $11.5M year-to-date increase in rewards expense. Management responded with Experian credit bust-out models and tighter identity verification.

Balance Sheet: Founder Capital in Bitcoin. 12. Is This a Theia Investment?

(i) Market Cap Bucket: $0.40B Strong Fit

Microcap (<$1B). Gemini's $405M market cap — down from ~$3.3B at the IPO — places it squarely in Theia's ideal bucket, below most institutional mandates' size thresholds and in a zone where forced selling (index deletions, crossover-fund exits post-IPO) has created informational inefficiency. Liquidity is adequate for a position (~2M shares, ~$6M per day), but the free float is only ~46M shares and 27% of it is short, so entry and exit can move the price sharply. The founders' ~94% voting control means minority holders cannot influence strategy.

(ii) Analyst Coverage: 10–11 Broker Contributors Moderate Fit

Ten to eleven sell-side analysts cover GEMI (Goldman, Citi, Morgan Stanley, Truist, Cantor, KBW, Needham, Rosenblatt, Mizuho, Evercore, Barclays — the IPO syndicate), rated Hold on balance with a $5.23 average target. That is more coverage than Theia prefers, but the coverage is largely legacy IPO-syndicate coverage with low conviction and stale models: only 3–4 analysts publish EPS estimates, no EBITDA consensus exists, and no one publishes FY2028 numbers. Targets have collapsed from $25–42 at initiation (October 2025) to $3.50–7.00, and the debate on prediction-market economics, card credit and funding is thinly modeled.

(iii) Thesis Alignment: Crypto Platform / Prediction Markets / Stablecoin Core Thesis

Gemini is directly aligned with Theia's internet-finance thesis: a regulated U.S. crypto exchange and NYDFS-chartered custodian, issuer of the GUSD stablecoin, a crypto-rewards credit card, a CFTC-regulated prediction market with its own clearinghouse, and a broker-dealer offering tokenization-adjacent multi-asset trading. Prediction markets and on-chain finance are two of the fastest-growing internet-finance categories of 2026. Rating: Core.

(iv) Outcome Dispersion: $0.18 – $14.64 Strong Fit

The 5-scenario model shows implied FY2028E prices from $0.18 (Bear Severe, (94%)) to $14.64 (Bull Severe, +369%) versus $3.12 today — an ~80x spread between the tails, the widest of any memo in this series. The dispersion is driven by three variables that are genuinely uncertain: whether predictions become a real revenue line, the price and size of the next equity raise, and crypto retail volumes. The probability-weighted value is $3.97, or $3.05 discounted at Theia's 30% cost of capital — (2.1%) versus the current price. The distribution is highly skewed: the median outcome is roughly flat, a quarter of the probability mass is a 59–94% loss, and the upside is concentrated in the 25% of cases where predictions and the card scale.

VERDICT: MAYBE — Watchlist; Core Thesis Option, but Wait for the Financing

Gemini scores Strong on market cap ($405M microcap), Core on thesis alignment and Strong on outcome dispersion ($0.18 to $14.64), with moderate (but stale) analyst coverage. It is a cheap, liquid option on the regulated U.S. prediction-market build-out — the DCM + DCO stack and Apex distribution are genuinely scarce assets — wrapped around a credible card business. But the probability-weighted value of $3.05 (after a 30% CoC) is ~2% below today's $3.12, because the company is still burning ~$50M per quarter against $189M of cash and will almost certainly issue equity in the next 12 months; buying ahead of that raise means funding the dilution. We would (a) track the Nov 12 Q3 print (card provisions, predictions revenue, cash burn), (b) look to participate in — or buy after — the next financing, ideally alongside the founders, at or below ~$2.50, and (c) upgrade to YES on a definitive Apex agreement with live brokerages plus quarterly cash burn below ~$25M.
