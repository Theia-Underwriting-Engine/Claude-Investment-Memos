Alkami Technology | Preliminary Memo for Theia
1. Business Overview
Alkami Technology (NASDAQ: ALKT) is a leading cloud-based digital banking solutions provider serving community, regional, and mid-tier financial institutions (FIs) across the United States. Founded in 2009 and headquartered in Plano, Texas, the company provides a single-code base, multi-tenant digital banking platform that enables credit unions and banks to compete against megabanks (Chase, Bank of America, Wells Fargo) by delivering modern digital experiences to their account holders. The platform supports retail and commercial banking across desktop, mobile, and SMS channels with 300+ real-time integrations to back-office systems and third-party fintechs.
The company has evolved from a pure-play digital banking interface into a Digital Sales and Service Platform (DSSP) integrating three pillars: Onboarding & Account Opening (via the $375M MANTL acquisition in March 2025), Digital Banking (core platform), and Data & Marketing (via the Segmint acquisition). This unified platform allows FIs to activate new account holders with multiple products in under five minutes, driving a 30% ARR uplift on new client wins. ALKT generates 95% of revenue from SaaS subscriptions on long-term contracts averaging ~70 months, with per-registered-user pricing that scales naturally with digital adoption.
Under CEO Alex Shootman and CSO/Founder Stephen Bohanon, Alkami has grown revenue at a 32% CAGR since IPO while building a dominant position among credit unions (serving 5 of the top 20 U.S. credit unions) and rapidly expanding into the bank market with 50 banks under contract. The company serves 301 live FI clients with 22.4 million registered digital banking users, generating $480M in ARR (+35% YoY) and $1.7B in remaining performance obligations (3.6x live ARR). FY2025 marked a critical inflection point as Alkami crossed to positive adjusted EBITDA ($59M, 13.3% margin) while guiding to 18.1% margin in FY2026 on a path to "Rule of 45" by 2030.
One-line thesis: Alkami is the leading cloud-native digital banking platform riding a massive re-platforming wave among 2,000+ community FIs stuck on legacy systems, with a compounding DSSP flywheel, AI optionality, and a profitability inflection that the market may be underpricing after a 40% drawdown.

3. Bull & Bear Cases
Bull Cases

1. DSSP Flywheel Drives 30% ARR Uplift. The unified Digital Sales and Service Platform (Onboarding + Banking + Data) creates a compounding cross-sell engine. In H2 2025, 58% of new digital banking deals became DSSP clients with ~30% ARR uplift. With only 45 of 301 clients on the full DSSP, the cross-sell runway is enormous. If full-suite adoption reaches 50%+ of the client base, RPU could approach $30+ (vs. $21.44 today), adding $200M+ to ARR without a single new logo.

2. Bank Market Penetration Accelerates. Alkami has 50 banks under contract (vs. 0 at IPO) with bank RPU of $37 vs. the company average of $21. The reorganized sales force under CRO Nathaniel Harley targets a 50/50 bank/CU split. With 78% of institutions on dominant cores still using legacy digital banking, the bank replacement cycle is just beginning. Each large bank win (500K+ users) adds $3-5M in ARR. A dozen bank wins in FY2026 could add $40-60M in contracted ARR.

3. AI Monetization Creates New Revenue Stream. Alkami is pioneering "Agentic AI" with read-write orchestration privileges connected to banking cores—autonomous processing of balance transfers, payment modifications, and inquiries 24/7. The Code Studio agent (trained on 6M+ lines of proprietary SDK code) compresses development from months to days. Community FIs lack internal AI capabilities, making Alkami the default AI infrastructure provider. New AI products could command $2-5 RPU premium.

4. Margin Expansion to "Rule of 45." Management targets 70% gross margins (vs. 64% today), 30%+ adj. EBITDA margins at $1B+ revenue scale, and 90% FCF conversion by 2030. The India GCC (150+ engineers) drives R&D cost leverage, renewal margins run ~70%, and AWS cloud hosting unit costs are down 26%. If margins expand faster than consensus (~300bps annually), earnings power doubles within 3 years.

5. Valuation Re-Rating from Growth Trough. ALKT trades at 3.2x FY26E P/Sales and 21x EV/EBITDA—near its all-time valuation low—after a 40% drawdown driven by guidance that disappointed on FY26 revenue ($528M vs. $549M consensus). With 9 Buy-rated analysts, $22.67 target (+45%), and General Atlantic buying $50M+ in shares, the selloff may be overdone. As the MANTL acquisition laps and organic growth re-accelerates in H2 2026, the multiple could recover toward 5-6x revenue.

Bear Cases

1. Competitive Pressure from Lumin & Q2. Lumin Digital is aggressively winning credit union contracts with a clean-slate architecture and lower pricing. Q2 Holdings dominates commercial banking and treasury—where Alkami admits it's still catching up. Legacy core providers (Fiserv, FIS, Jack Henry) can bundle digital banking at no incremental cost. If Alkami's win rates decline or pricing comes under pressure, the revenue growth trajectory compresses materially.

2. MANTL Integration Risk & Technical Debt. The $375M MANTL acquisition requires complex harmonization of disparate systems. Implementation timelines for the integrated DSSP stretch to 12+ months (vs. 6 months for standalone products), delaying revenue recognition. Expert sources cite growing technical debt from historical customizations creating fragile infrastructure where new feature deployments can break existing functionality—a compounding liability as the platform scales.

3. Banking Consolidation Churn. At ~3% annual FI consolidation, Alkami risks losing clients when they're acquired by larger institutions using competitor or in-house platforms. While management claims consolidation is net positive (retaining combined user bases), a wave of M&A targeting Alkami's largest credit union clients could meaningfully impair ARR. The 13.6% short interest suggests the market sees this risk.

4. Profitability May Disappoint. FY2026 adj. EBITDA guidance ($95.5M midpoint) already faced a headwind from a database vendor doubling license costs (~1pt of margin). GAAP profitability remains negative due to 14-15% SBC-to-revenue. If gross margins stall below 65% or opex discipline falters, the "Rule of 45" narrative loses credibility. The Q4 2025 gross margin decline to 63.4% (from 65.1% in Q2) raised questions about the margin trajectory.

5. Cybersecurity & Platform Stability. A material data breach at Alkami would be existential—banking clients cannot tolerate security incidents with depositor data. Expert sources cite historical platform crashes during high-traffic events (government stimulus distributions, AWS pod failures). As Alkami scales to 25M+ users, any significant outage erodes trust with the risk-averse FI buyer base and hands wins to competitors.

4. Helmer's 7 Powers
1. Scale EconomiesStrong
Alkami's single-code base, multi-tenant cloud architecture amortizes R&D and infrastructure costs across 22.4M registered users and 301 FI clients. The platform's fixed costs (engineering, compliance, AWS infrastructure, security) are spread across a growing user base while marginal costs per additional user are minimal. Cloud hosting unit costs have declined 26% from prior peaks. Smaller competitors like Lumin or Narmi cannot match Alkami's R&D investment (~$200M annually including SBC) or the breadth of 300+ integrations without comparable scale.
2. Network EffectsModerate
Alkami benefits from an indirect network effect through its integration ecosystem—300+ third-party fintech partners integrate with the platform, making it more valuable for FIs as the ecosystem grows. The data network effect is meaningful: 29M deposit accounts and 20B historical transactions feed Segmint's 12 proprietary AI models and 50,000+ descriptive data tags, improving predictive accuracy with each new client. However, these are weaker than true two-sided network effects; clients don't directly benefit from other clients using the platform.
3. Counter-PositioningStrong
Legacy core processors (Fiserv, FIS, Jack Henry) generate enormous revenue from bundled, single-tenant legacy digital banking tied to their core systems. Adopting Alkami's cloud-native, unbundled, multi-tenant approach would cannibalize their highly profitable core-plus-digital bundles. 78% of institutions on dominant bank cores still use legacy digital banking—these incumbents rationally choose to protect existing revenue rather than re-platform. This dynamic creates a sustained tailwind for Alkami as the "best of breed" unbundler.
4. Switching CostsStrong
Digital banking platform replacement is a 12-24 month migration involving core system integration, user migration, data transfer, staff retraining, and regulatory compliance validation. Alkami's digital banking ARR churn is below 1% annually (vs. 2-3% modeled), and average contract duration is ~70 months. The platform becomes deeply embedded as the primary digital channel for all consumer/business interactions. The DSSP integration (onboarding + banking + data) further increases switching costs as FIs become dependent on the unified workflow.
5. BrandingModerate
Alkami has built strong brand recognition among FI decision-makers through its Co:lab annual conference (1,000+ executives), Community portal (3,600+ active users, 96.6% adoption), and "Women in Banking" events. The brand represents modern, cloud-native digital banking—a meaningful signal in an industry where FI technology leaders are increasingly seeking vendors that signal innovation to their boards. However, this is B2B brand awareness, not consumer-facing pricing power.
6. Cornered ResourceModerate
Alkami's proprietary data lake (Segmint) processes 29M deposit accounts and 20B historical transactions with patented software for transaction cleansing and behavioral tagging. This data asset—used for "Anticipatory Banking" campaigns and fraud AI—is difficult to replicate as it requires years of core banking data accumulation. The 6M+ lines of proprietary SDK code powering the AI Code Studio agent is another non-replicable asset. However, Q2 Holdings has built comparable (if different) data capabilities.
7. Process PowerModerate
Alkami has demonstrated the ability to launch ~40 new FI clients annually—a complex process involving thousands of back-office connections and migrating entire user bases. This implementation capability, refined over 15+ years, is embedded in organizational processes and cannot be easily replicated. However, expert sources note growing implementation bottlenecks and burnout within client service teams, suggesting process power may be strained at current growth rates.
Summary: Alkami's strongest moats are switching costs (sub-1% churn, 70-month contracts), counter-positioning (legacy cores can't unbundle without self-harm), and scale economies (R&D and infrastructure leverage across 22M+ users). The data network effect and cornered resource (Segmint data lake) provide additional compounding advantages. The moat is durable for the re-platforming cycle but faces a long-term question about what happens once the conversion wave is complete and growth must come from market expansion vs. replacement.
5. Porter's 5 Forces
1. Threat of New EntrantsLow
Building a cloud-native digital banking platform requires massive R&D investment ($200M+ annually for Alkami), deep regulatory expertise (FFIEC, NCUA, OCC compliance), and years of core system integrations with hundreds of back-office providers. Only two contemporary providers (Alkami and Q2) manage ~50M total digital banking seats—entering at scale requires a decade of implementation track record that risk-averse FI buyers demand before trusting depositor-facing systems. Lumin and Narmi represent the strongest emerging threats but remain sub-scale.
2. Bargaining Power of SuppliersModerate
AWS is the primary infrastructure supplier and Alkami has limited leverage to negotiate aggressively given cloud dependency. The recent database vendor license cost doubling (creating ~1pt EBITDA margin headwind) illustrates supplier pricing power for critical components. However, Alkami is actively migrating off that vendor and can generally multi-source cloud infrastructure. Core banking system providers (potential suppliers for integration access) can theoretically restrict API access, though regulatory pressure and market dynamics limit this.
3. Bargaining Power of BuyersModerate
FI buyers are sophisticated enterprise purchasers with long evaluation cycles and RFP processes. No single client exceeds 5% of total revenue, creating a fragmented buyer base. However, FIs have growing alternatives (Q2, Lumin, core provider bundles) and the ~500 annual contract renewals industry-wide create competitive pressure. Per-registered-user pricing with tiered discounts gives larger FIs some negotiating leverage on rates, though the long contract terms and switching costs provide stability.
4. Threat of SubstitutesLow
Digital banking has shifted from a discretionary technology expense to mandatory, mission-critical infrastructure. FIs cannot serve modern consumers without a digital platform—there is no viable substitute for digital banking. The question is which provider, not whether to have digital banking. Legacy core-bundled solutions are the primary alternative, but they are technologically inferior (single-tenant, limited innovation velocity) and represent the existing installed base being disrupted, not a substitute.
5. Competitive RivalryModerate
The market is effectively a duopoly at the top (Alkami and Q2 Holdings combined ~50M seats) with a long tail of legacy providers and emerging challengers. Alkami dominates retail banking UX while Q2 leads in commercial/treasury—creating some natural segmentation. However, both are aggressively expanding into each other's strongholds. Legacy cores (Fiserv, FIS, Jack Henry) compete through aggressive bundling and pricing. Industry growth is fast enough (~20% digitization penetration of SAM) that rivalry is manageable, but head-to-head competition for large RFPs can be intense.
Summary: The competitive environment is favorable for Alkami. High barriers to entry, no viable substitutes for digital banking, and a fast-growing market with significant legacy replacement runway limit competitive intensity. The primary risk is head-to-head rivalry with Q2 Holdings and pricing pressure from legacy core bundlers, but the overall industry structure supports healthy growth and margins for the two contemporary leaders.
6. What Drives the Stock?
  1. 1. New Client Wins & Implementation Pipeline

    Alkami signed 39 new digital banking logos in FY2025 (tied best year) with $71M ARR in backlog pending implementation. The cadence of new client announcements—particularly bank wins—drives sentiment. Each new bank with 500K+ digital users can add $3-5M ARR. Watch: quarterly new logo count (bank vs. CU split), backlog ARR, and implementation pace (number of go-lives per quarter). Bullish: 40+ new logos with >50% banks, backlog grows. Bearish: Win rate declines, implementation delays stretch beyond 12 months, backlog stagnates.

  2. 2. RPU Growth & DSSP Cross-Sell

    Revenue per registered user grew 20% YoY to $21.44 in FY2025, driven by MANTL cross-sell, Segmint attach rates (90%+), and add-on product adoption. DSSP clients (45 of 301) generate ~30% higher ARR than standalone clients. The cross-sell metric (54% of TCV in 2025) is the key leading indicator. Management guides high-single-digit RPU growth for FY2026. Bullish: RPU >$23, DSSP adoption >80 clients, cross-sell >60% TCV. Bearish: RPU growth <5%, DSSP adoption stalls, Segmint attach rate declines.

  3. 3. Margin Expansion Trajectory

    The market's core debate: Is Alkami a 30%+ EBITDA margin business at scale or a permanently SBC-diluted grower? FY2026 guides to 18.1% adj. EBITDA margin (vs. 13.3% FY2025). Each quarter's margin progression validates or challenges the "Rule of 45" path. The database vendor cost headwind and DSSP implementation complexity create near-term risks. Bullish: Quarterly adj. EBITDA margins expanding sequentially, gross margin >65%. Bearish: Margins stall or compress, SBC remains >14% of revenue, non-GAAP adjustments questioned.

  4. 4. Organic Revenue Growth (Ex-MANTL)

    FY2026 reported growth decelerates to ~19% from 33% due to MANTL lapping. Organic growth (ex-MANTL) is the true underlying health metric. Management disclosed organic growth was >20% in Q3 2025. If organic growth sustains above 20%, the deceleration narrative breaks. Bullish: Organic growth 20%+ through FY2026, accelerating into H2. Bearish: Organic growth <18%, suggesting market saturation or competitive share loss.

  5. 5. Registered User Growth & Digital Adoption

    Net new registered users (+2.4M in FY2025 to 22.4M) drive the top line through per-user pricing. Growth comes from new client implementations (~1.3M users in backlog) and existing client digital adoption (+1.5M organic in FY2025). The 200M+ addressable digital users at non-megabank FIs represents a massive runway, but user growth from existing clients may moderate as digital penetration matures. Bullish: >2.5M net adds, backlog user count grows. Bearish: <2M net adds, existing client organic growth declines.

  6. 6. Short Interest & Insider Activity

    At 13.6% of float, ALKT has significant short interest—creating potential for violent short squeezes on positive catalysts but also reflecting genuine bearish conviction. General Atlantic purchased $50M+ in shares recently, and a director bought $100K in stock. Insider buying at these levels signals management confidence. Bullish: Short covering rally on earnings beat, additional insider buying. Bearish: Short interest rises above 15%, insider selling resumes.

AlphaSense Diagnostic Report · Date: April 1, 2026 · Sources: Company Primer, Expert Calls & Competitive Analysis, Q4/Q3/Q2 2025 Earnings Transcripts, Conference Transcripts · Query: Alkami Technology — Full Competitive & Strategic Analysis
Competitive Moat & Industry Structure
Alkami and Q2 Holdings are the only two "contemporary" digital banking providers managing ~50M combined seats, with 200M+ addressable seats still on legacy platforms. The competitive dynamic varies by segment: Alkami dominates retail banking with a consistently superior consumer UX and mobile experience (cited by experts as the single most significant competitive moat), while Q2 leads in complex commercial banking, small business, and corporate treasury. Alkami is rapidly closing the commercial gap, now covering the needs of ~92% of U.S. businesses utilizing treasury platforms.
Legacy core processors (Fiserv, FIS, Jack Henry) remain the largest competitive threat by volume—78% of institutions on dominant cores still use legacy bundled digital banking. However, these incumbents are structurally disadvantaged: single-tenant architectures require costly maintenance windows, innovation velocity is slow, and unbundling risks cannibalizing profitable core revenue. Alkami capitalizes on an industry-wide re-platforming wave as FIs recognize that legacy digital banking cannot support modern consumer expectations (instant account opening, mobile loan origination, real-time transfers). Emerging challengers Lumin Digital and Narmi present pricing pressure at the lower end of the credit union market with clean-slate modern architectures.
Management Quality & Strategic Vision
Founder/CSO Stephen Bohanon is lauded for technical foresight—designing a scalable single-code base microservice architecture over a decade before cloud-native became the industry norm. CEO Alex Shootman has navigated growing pains by upgrading leadership talent (new CRO Nathaniel Harley from MANTL, new CFO Cassandra Hudson) and reorganizing the sales force into dedicated bank/CU teams. The MANTL acquisition ($375M) is viewed by experts as a "brilliant strategic maneuver" that solved omnichannel account origination with minimal client overlap and provided a beachhead into banks (70% of MANTL clients were banks).
Capital allocation has been disciplined: the Segmint ($130M) and MANTL ($375M) acquisitions were natively integrated into the DSSP rather than operated as standalone businesses. Management has begun paying down the revolver ($45M repaid in FY2025) and maintains optionality for future M&A with $1B-$1.5B in capital deployment capacity over 4 years. The transition from Bryan Hill to Cassandra Hudson as CFO is a watch item—continuity of financial discipline and investor communication during a profitability inflection is critical.
Technology Differentiation & AI
AI is a net positive growth catalyst for Alkami. Community FIs lack internal data science teams and compliant infrastructure for AI, creating heavy reliance on their digital banking provider. Alkami monetizes AI through: (1) Data & Marketing products with 12 proprietary AI predictive models and 50,000+ descriptive data tags for behavior prediction, fraud prevention, and automated cross-selling; (2) Agentic AI with read-write orchestration privileges connected to banking cores for autonomous processing of complex workflows; (3) Code Studio—an AI-native development agent trained on 6M+ lines of proprietary SDK code that compresses development from months to days. Internally, specialized development agents processed 1M+ lines of code in a single month, driving 18% improvement in developer productivity, 9% faster support ticket closure, and 29% reduction in engineering escalations.
Most Misunderstood Aspects
1. Data assets underappreciated: Investors view Alkami as a digital banking interface, underappreciating the proprietary data lake—29M deposit accounts, 20B historical transactions, 20M new transactions daily. The Segmint platform transforms raw core data into behavioral data tags identifying competing P2P app usage, multiple credit card balances, and external investment transfers. This positions Alkami as an essential intelligence hub, not just a software vendor. Data & marketing products are attached to 70%+ of new client wins.
2. MANTL impact underestimated: The market focuses on MANTL's ~$60M ARR contribution, but the strategic value lies in the DSSP flywheel. Full-suite clients generate 30% higher ARR. MANTL automates 85% of application decisions, reducing account opening to under 5 minutes—a transformative capability for FIs losing deposits to neobanks. The cross-sell pipeline (161 MANTL clients, only 26 overlapping with digital banking) represents years of revenue expansion.
3. Revenue growth deceleration is optically misleading: FY2026 reported growth of ~19% reflects MANTL lapping, 75% decline in termination fees, and longer DSSP implementation cycles. Underlying demand remains robust: $71M ARR backlog, 39 new logos, and $1.7B RPO. The H2 2026 acceleration (as organic growth re-emerges and DSSP implementations complete) should demonstrate the underlying trajectory.
Key Long-Term Investor Questions
  1. Can Alkami close the commercial/treasury gap with Q2? Alkami's retail dominance is clear, but mid-market banks demand commercial capabilities where Q2 currently leads. The commercial investment is well underway (covers ~92% of treasury needs), but execution in complex commercial banking is unproven at scale. This will determine whether Alkami can sustainably capture 50/50 bank/CU new logos.
  2. How sustainable is sub-1% churn in a consolidating banking industry? With ~3% annual FI consolidation, maintaining sub-1% churn requires winning the digital platform decision in nearly every merger. Management's track record is positive (net +300K users from consolidation in FY2025), but a single loss of a top-20 credit union could meaningfully impact churn metrics.
  3. Will the margin expansion trajectory hold? The path from 13% to 30%+ adj. EBITDA margin requires gross margin expansion to 70% (from 64%), disciplined opex management, and declining SBC as a percentage of revenue. The database vendor cost issue and temporary gross margin dip in Q4 2025 raised questions. India GCC and renewal margin dynamics (70%) support the path, but execution must be demonstrated quarter by quarter.
