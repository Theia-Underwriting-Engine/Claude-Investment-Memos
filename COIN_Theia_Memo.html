Coinbase | Preliminary Memo for Theia
1. Business Overview
Coinbase Global (NASDAQ: COIN) is the largest publicly traded cryptocurrency exchange in the US and one of the largest globally. Founded in 2012 and headquartered in San Francisco, CEO Brian Armstrong leads approximately 4,951 employees. The company operates a multi-sided platform connecting retail and institutional users to trade, custody, stake, and earn rewards on crypto assets, while rapidly expanding into equities, derivatives (via the 2025 Deribit acquisition), prediction markets, and stablecoin payments (USDC via a partnership with Circle). The core thesis: Coinbase is positioning itself as the "Everything Exchange" — a one-stop financial services platform leveraging its trusted crypto brand, regulatory compliance leadership (12% of global crypto custody), and expanding product suite to capture multiple trillions in financial services revenue as blockchain technology reshapes the global financial system.

3. Helmer's 7 Powers
Scale Economies Strong
Coinbase has built a high fixed-cost infrastructure for compliance, custody, trading matching, and engineering. As trading volumes scale, these fixed costs amortize across an increasingly large revenue base. With 12% of all global crypto stored on the platform and ~$7.2B in FY25 revenue, the unit economics improve materially with incremental transaction volume. The subscription and services business (growing 23% YoY to $2.8B) is particularly scalable.
Network Effects Strong
Two-sided marketplace dynamics benefit from classic liquidity feedback loops: more trading volume → tighter spreads and better execution → attracts more traders. Coinbase's Base L2 blockchain has direct network effects (more developers → more apps → more users). USDC stablecoin creates indirect network effects through ecosystem adoption; 150 companies announced stablecoin integrations within 3 months of the GENIUS Act passing, directly benefiting Coinbase's distribution.
Counter-Positioning Moderate
Traditional brokerages (Schwab, Fidelity) find it difficult to offer full-stack crypto services (custodial storage, staking, DeFi access, derivatives) without building deep crypto infrastructure from scratch. This is why JPMorgan partnered with Coinbase rather than building internally. However, pure-play fintechs like Robinhood and Kraken are building competitive offerings, limiting the moat here.
Switching Costs Moderate
Institutional clients face high switching costs due to established custody agreements, API integrations, compliance workflows, and relationship capital with Coinbase's institutional team. Retail switching costs are lower but rising with Coinbase One membership (growing subscriber base with premium features), the Coinbase Card, loans, and the Everything Exchange making the platform progressively stickier as customers accumulate features.
Branding Strong
Coinbase has built "the most trusted brand in crypto" per management. This is a genuine competitive advantage in an industry plagued by fraud and security breaches (FTX, etc.). The company works with 5 G-SIB banks, 150+ government agencies, and is now a member of the S&P 500, reinforcing institutional legitimacy. Brand pricing power is evident in willingness to pay a premium for perceived safety and compliance.
Cornered Resource Moderate
Coinbase controls regulatory licenses across 40+ jurisdictions, holds the BitLicense in New York, and maintains the largest institutional banking relationships. The 2025 acquisition of Deribit (closed in Q4) gives Coinbase access to the dominant crypto options trading venue. 12% of global crypto custody is a significant but not fully cornered resource; competitors can acquire licenses and build cold storage systems, though Coinbase's head start is substantial.
Process Power Moderate
Deep crypto-native expertise (early adoption of DEX trading, DeFi, multiparty computation cold storage) is difficult for traditional finance incumbents to replicate. The company has shipped 12 products generating $100M+ ARR and completed 10 acquisitions/acqui-hires in 2025 alone, demonstrating rapid execution capability. However, this advantage is more about talent and culture than embedded, durable organizational processes.
Summary: The strongest moats are scale economies (fixed infrastructure + increasing volume), network effects (liquidity loops + ecosystem adoption), and branding (trust in crypto). The combination of regulatory leadership, custody dominance, and an expanding product suite creates a compounding flywheel that is difficult for any single competitor to replicate, though Robinhood and traditional finance entrants pose real competitive threats.
4. Porter's 5 Forces
Threat of New Entrants Low
Massive regulatory barriers (BitLicense, multi-jurisdictional compliance, AML/KYC infrastructure), high capital requirements for custody infrastructure, and the need for deep liquidity pools create significant moats. However, existing financial players (JPMorgan, PayPal, Schwab) are entering the space through partnerships or organic initiatives, and a well-capitalized new entrant could eventually compete on product breadth. The low threat rating reflects these barriers outweighing the risk of new competitors today.
Bargaining Power of Suppliers Low
Key "suppliers" are blockchain networks (open protocols with no leverage) and Circle (the USDC stablecoin partner). While Circle's relationship is contractual, Coinbase has significant leverage as the largest USDC distributor and end-user. The partnership benefits both parties equally, and Coinbase could theoretically launch its own stablecoin if Circle relationship deteriorated. No other material supply dependencies.
Bargaining Power of Buyers Moderate
Retail users can multi-home across Robinhood, Kraken, Gemini, and others, creating price sensitivity. Institutional clients are stickier due to custody and workflow integration. Coinbase One membership reduces retail price sensitivity by bundling services. However, take rates are under compression from competition and mix shift (advanced traders and Coinbase One members pay lower per-trade fees), creating ongoing margin pressure. Buyers have meaningful alternatives but Coinbase's brand and Everything Exchange differentiation provide some insulation.
Threat of Substitutes Moderate
Decentralized exchanges (Uniswap, dYdX) offer non-custodial trading without intermediaries. Self-custody wallets reduce the need for centralized platforms. However, regulatory compliance, fiat on-ramps, user experience, and institutional support keep most users on centralized exchanges. The Everything Exchange (equities, prediction markets, commodities) further reduces substitution risk by widening the TAM beyond pure crypto trading.
Competitive Rivalry High
The competitive set includes Binance (global dominance but regulatory risk), Kraken (strong institutional offering), Robinhood (mass market access), and emerging players (Crypto.com, OKX). Trading volumes across all platforms are growing, but market share is contested. Differentiation is increasingly important, and Coinbase's Everything Exchange, regulatory posture, and institutional relationships are significant competitive advantages. However, high rivalry and pricing pressure on take rates are ongoing headwinds.
Summary: The competitive environment is moderately favorable for Coinbase. Regulatory barriers and trust/branding provide strong protection against new entrants. However, high competitive rivalry and ongoing take rate compression from both substitutes (DEXs) and direct competitors (Robinhood) mean that success depends on continuous product innovation and market share defense.
5. What Drives the Stock?
1. Crypto Trading Volumes (Spot + Derivatives)
Direct revenue driver for transaction revenue ($3.8B in FY25). Bitcoin and Ethereum price movements drive retail engagement and retail transaction volume. Q4 2025 saw record derivatives volume via Deribit despite softer spot markets. Key watch: total platform trading volume trajectory and the mix between spot (lower margin) and derivatives (higher margin). Bullish if volumes >$300B annualized; bearish if <$200B.
2. Bitcoin / Crypto Prices
BTC is at all-time highs (~$150K range in early 2026), driving new user acquisition and trading activity. Each 10% move in BTC price correlates with ~5-7% move in retail activity on Coinbase. Higher crypto prices also increase the value of staked assets and custody assets, indirectly boosting revenue. A sustained crypto downturn would pressure both transaction volume and user engagement.
3. USDC Circulation & Stablecoin Revenue
Stablecoin revenue is now $1.35B annually (18.8% of total), growing 48% YoY. Driven by Average USDC Market Cap × Fed Funds Rate × Coinbase revenue share. USDC reached ~$75B market cap (though revised to ~$56B in corrected guidance). GENIUS Act passing (signed into law) is a major tailwind for regulated US stablecoins. A doubling of USDC market cap to $100B+ would add ~$200-250M in annual revenue. Bullish catalyst: regulatory adoption accelerating stablecoin payments.
4. Regulatory Clarity
CLARITY Act, market structure bills, and GENIUS Act represent major regulatory tailwinds. The shift in SEC posture under the new administration is positive for crypto. Clear regulatory frameworks unlock institutional adoption and corporate treasury allocation to crypto. Negative news (anti-crypto regulation) would trigger sharp sell-off; positive news (new licenses, clearer frameworks) is a catalyst for multiple expansion.
5. Everything Exchange Adoption (Equities, Prediction Markets, Derivatives)
Equities launched with ~10,000 tickers in Q4. Prediction markets rolled out to 100% of customers. Deribit derivatives integration is early but showing momentum. Success here expands TAM far beyond crypto and reduces revenue cyclicality (equities/prediction markets less correlated to BTC). Bullish if these new products reach $500M+ in combined revenue by FY27; bearish if they remain niche.
6. Take Rate Trends
Consumer take rates have compressed from mix shift (Coinbase One, advanced trading, institutional) and competitive pressure. Blended take rate is key metric. Bullish: take rate stabilizes as mix stabilizes and Everything Exchange adds higher-margin products. Bearish: continued compression from Robinhood and retail competition, or mass migration to Coinbase One (recurring revenue but lower per-trade fees).

10. Is This a Theia Investment?
(i) Market Cap Bucket: Large Cap ($48.9B) Weak
Coinbase sits well outside Theia's sweet spot. The fund targets microcaps (<$1B) and small caps ($1-5B) where information asymmetries are highest and analyst coverage is sparse. At $49B, COIN is covered by 25+ analysts and is actively traded, making it difficult to find durable informational edge.
(ii) Analyst Coverage: 25+ Analysts Weak
Heavy institutional coverage reduces the probability that the market is significantly mispricing the stock. The consensus view (OW rating, $244.63 target, implying 32% upside) reflects the bull case broadly. Finding alpha here requires contrarian views on revenue drivers, margin trajectory, or multiple compression/expansion risk that the sell-side is missing.
(iii) Blockchain / Internet Finance Thesis Alignment Strong
Direct and tight alignment. Coinbase is literally the leading crypto exchange and stablecoin infrastructure company. A successful blockchain/internet finance thesis requires large-scale crypto adoption and stablecoin payments infrastructure; Coinbase is the most direct pure-play beneficiary. This is not a tangential exposure—it's core.
(iv) Outcome Dispersion: Wide Dispersion Strong
Based on the sensitivity analysis and model, the range of plausible outcomes is massive. Bear severe case implies ~$46 (-75%), base case $262 (+42%), bull severe case $683 (+269%). This is a reflection of crypto cyclicality (highly binary outcomes depending on regulatory clarity and adoption rates) combined with multiple new business lines (Everything Exchange, stablecoins, derivatives) that are unproven at scale. The outcome dispersion is genuinely wide, which is typically favorable for Theia (more mispricing potential).
Investment Thesis Summary for Theia:

Theia seeks small cap companies that aren't well covered and are strongly aligned on core internet finance thesis with wide outcome dispersion.

Verdict: Maybe — Core Thesis Alignment with Wide Outcome Dispersion, But Market Cap & Coverage Are Headwinds

The bull case for Coinbase is compelling: regulatory clarity accelerating, USDC adoption scaling, Everything Exchange opening TAM, derivatives momentum. The bear case is also material: crypto could enter prolonged downturn, competition from Robinhood and traditional finance intensifies, take rate compression continues. This wide dispersion of outcomes means the stock could see significant repricing if sentiment shifts or new information emerges. However, at $49B market cap with 25+ analysts covering, this is a well-researched name where the market has already priced in much of the upside/downside. Better suited as a benchmark/tracking position or opportunistic entry on significant price dislocations (e.g., crypto crash) than a core Theia holding. Ideal scenario for Theia ownership: A sustained crypto winter takes COIN to $80-100, at which point the Everything Exchange and regulatory tailwinds create a 3-5x opportunity. In that scenario, Theia could buy into a dislocated market and ride the recovery. For now, monitor the stock, but focus alpha-hunting energy on smaller-cap fintech and blockchain plays where the market is more fragmented.

A. Sensitivity Table Generator
Parameterized function to generate sensitivity tables for any business driver pair:
import pandas as pd import numpy as np def sensitivity_table(driver1_name, driver1_values, driver1_unit, driver2_name, driver2_values, driver2_unit, formula_func, output_unit="$M"): """ Generate a sensitivity table for two business drivers. Args: driver1_name: Name of first driver (e.g., "Trading Volume") driver1_values: List of values for rows [200, 300, 400, 500, 600] driver1_unit: Unit label for driver1 (e.g., "$B") driver2_name: Name of second driver (e.g., "Take Rate") driver2_values: List of values for columns [70, 85, 100, 115, 130] driver2_unit: Unit label for driver2 (e.g., "bps") formula_func: Function that takes (driver1, driver2) and returns output output_unit: Unit for output (default "$M") Returns: pandas DataFrame with sensitivity table """ # Build matrix matrix = np.zeros((len(driver1_values), len(driver2_values))) for i, d1 in enumerate(driver1_values): for j, d2 in enumerate(driver2_values): matrix[i, j] = formula_func(d1, d2) # Create DataFrame df = pd.DataFrame( matrix, index=[f"{v}{driver1_unit}" for v in driver1_values], columns=[f"{v}{driver2_unit}" for v in driver2_values] ) # Format with commas df = df.applymap(lambda x: f"${x:,.0f}") return df # Example: Consumer Trading Revenue Sensitivity # Formula: Volume ($B) * Take Rate (bps) / 10,000 = Revenue ($M) def consumer_revenue_formula(volume_b, take_rate_bps): return (volume_b * take_rate_bps / 10_000) * 1_000 # Convert to $M trading_volumes = [200, 300, 400, 500, 600] take_rates = [70, 85, 100, 115, 130] consumer_sensitivity = sensitivity_table( driver1_name="Trading Volume", driver1_values=trading_volumes, driver1_unit="B", driver2_name="Blended Retail Take Rate", driver2_values=take_rates, driver2_unit=" bps", formula_func=consumer_revenue_formula, output_unit="$M" ) print("Consumer Trading Revenue Sensitivity:") print(consumer_sensitivity) #
