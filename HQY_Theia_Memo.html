HealthEquity, Inc. | Preliminary Memo for Theia
1. Business Overview

HealthEquity, Inc. (NASDAQ: HQY) is the nation's largest Health Savings Account (HSA) custodian by total accounts and the second-largest by total HSA assets, operating at the intersection of healthcare and financial services through a technology-enabled platform designed to help consumers manage tax-advantaged healthcare savings and spending. Founded in 2002 by Dr. Stephen Neeleman and headquartered in Draper, Utah, the company has grown from a niche HSA administrator into the dominant platform for consumer-directed healthcare benefits, administering 17.8 million Total Accounts — comprising 10.6 million HSAs and 7.2 million complementary Consumer-Directed Benefits (CDBs) including Flexible Spending Accounts (FSAs), Health Reimbursement Arrangements (HRAs), COBRA, and commuter benefits — with total HSA assets of $36.5 billion as of January 31, 2026. HealthEquity operates a highly scalable business-to-business-to-consumer (B2B2C) distribution model, reaching individual members through an integrated network of more than 200 Network Partners including health plans, benefits brokers, retirement recordkeepers, and pharmacy benefit managers, which collectively support over 100,000 employer clients. The company has grown its share of total U.S. HSA assets from 4% in 2010 to approximately 21% by mid-2025, establishing clear market leadership in a category with powerful secular tailwinds.

HealthEquity's corporate strategy is anchored by a self-reinforcing "Save, Spend, Invest" flywheel designed to transform the HSA from a passive tax-advantaged benefit into an active financial asset. The "Save" phase helps members build pre-tax balances through payroll deductions and employer contributions. The "Spend" phase captures a share of approximately $15 billion spent annually by members on qualified medical care through the platform, enhanced by the new HealthEquity Marketplace that connects members to eligible products and telehealth services including GLP-1 weight management programs. The "Invest" phase encourages members to transition cash balances into long-term investment vehicles — HSA invested assets surged 26% year-over-year to $18.5 billion by end of fiscal 2026, representing over half of all HSA assets for the first time. The company monetizes this ecosystem through three primary, overlapping revenue streams: Custodial revenue ($636.8M, 48.5% of FY2026 revenue), derived from interest earned on HSA cash assets held by depository and insurance company partners, optimized through the proprietary "Enhanced Rates" program that channels deposits into high-yield insurance bonds and multi-year annuity contracts; Service revenue ($485.0M, 36.9%), consisting of recurring administrative fees paid by employers and network partners; and Interchange revenue ($191.6M, 14.6%), generated from merchant payment network fees whenever members use HealthEquity physical or virtual payment cards for medical expenses.

3. Bull & Bear Cases
Bull Cases

OBBBA Legislative Tailwind Creates Massive TAM Expansion. The "One Big Beautiful Bill Act" (OBBBA), signed in July 2025, permanently categorizes all Bronze and Catastrophic ACA exchange plans as HSA-compatible HDHPs, instantly unlocking HSA eligibility for approximately 7 million Americans (90% previously ineligible) enrolled in Bronze plans. The legislation also expands qualified medical expenses to include telehealth and Direct Primary Care (DPC) arrangements. This represents a structural, non-reversible expansion of HealthEquity's total addressable market — the shift from Silver to Bronze plans on ACA exchanges has already reached 40% nationally (the highest ever), and this trend will only accelerate as enhanced Premium Tax Credits expire, forcing consumers toward lower-premium, higher-deductible Bronze plans that now come with HSA eligibility. If HealthEquity captures even 20% of the 7M newly eligible population over 3 years, that represents 1.4M incremental HSAs at an average revenue per account well above the current base given the retail direct-enrollment channel.

Enhanced Rates Program Locks In Above-Market Yields. HealthEquity's proprietary Enhanced Rates program — which channels HSA cash deposits into high-yield insurance bonds and multi-year annuity contracts — is a genuinely differentiated custodial strategy that competitors have not replicated at scale. By end of FY2027, management targets transitioning 60% of all HSA cash into these enhanced contracts, which lock in a blended base rate of approximately 3.92% across maturities through January 2028. This effectively immunizes custodial revenue against interest rate cuts — because only a small fraction of contracts reprice in any given year, HealthEquity's blended yield compression in a rate-cutting cycle would be far slower than the market assumes. Forward interest rate hedges ($2.35–$2.4B notional on U.S. Treasuries) provide additional protection. If rates remain elevated or decline only modestly, HealthEquity's custodial margins could significantly exceed consensus expectations.

Investment Flywheel Accelerates. HSA invested assets surged 26% year-over-year to $18.5 billion, crossing the threshold to represent over half of all HSA assets for the first time. Yet over 90% of HealthEquity's 10.6M HSA members have not yet opened an investment account — representing a massive untapped opportunity to increase lifetime account value without acquiring new customers. The platform's three-tier advisory model (Self-driven, GPS guidance, AutoPilot portfolio management) is designed to systematically convert cash savers into long-term investors. Each dollar that moves from cash to invested assets generates higher custodial yields (investment management fees) while simultaneously increasing account stickiness and reducing churn. If HealthEquity can increase investment account penetration from ~8% to 15% over 3 years, the incremental invested assets and fee revenue would be transformational.

AI-Driven Margin Expansion and Service Cost Reduction. Artificial intelligence is fundamentally transforming HealthEquity's cost structure. The Expedited Claims AI tool eliminates manual receipt processing, with over half of all claims processed in under two minutes — reducing service costs per account while increasing member satisfaction by 18%. The partnership with Parloa to deploy "agentic AI" across voice and chat networks enables natural conversations that understand complex member intents and execute account actions in real time. HSAnswers, an AI-powered educational assistant, deflects routine inquiries from human agents. These technologies have driven a 70% year-over-year reduction in fraud-related service costs and are bending the overall service cost curve, allowing total account volume to grow considerably faster than underlying service expenses. If AI continues to drive unit cost reduction at current trajectory, EBITDA margins could approach 50% by FY2029 versus the consensus 46%.

Healthcare Affordability Crisis Is a Structural Secular Tailwind. Employer benefit costs are growing at 2–3x the pace of wages, with average family premiums rising 26% since 2020 and 53% since 2015. Industry surveys indicate 86% of employers anticipate significant pressure through 2027. This forces enterprises to steer workforces toward HDHPs paired with HSAs — a dynamic so powerful that HealthEquity generated over 1 million new HSAs from sales in each of the last two fiscal years, even while the U.S. economy added only 181,000 jobs. National HSA assets have reached $146 billion across 39+ million accounts, and the market is still in early innings of structural adoption. HealthEquity, as the largest and most deeply integrated custodian, is the primary beneficiary of this megatrend.

Bear Cases

Interest Rate Sensitivity and Custodial Yield Compression. Custodial revenue ($636.8M, 48.5% of total) is derived from interest earned on HSA cash — making it inherently sensitive to the interest rate environment. While the Enhanced Rates program provides substantial insulation, a severe and prolonged rate-cutting cycle (e.g., Fed funds rate falling to 2% or below) would eventually compress blended custodial yields as enhanced contracts mature and reprice. The market may also perceive interest rate risk as greater than the actual exposure, creating persistent valuation overhang. In a sustained low-rate environment reminiscent of 2020–2021, custodial margins would compress meaningfully even with Enhanced Rates, potentially reducing total EBITDA margins by 300–500 basis points from current levels.

Fidelity and Institutional Competitors Gain Share. Fidelity represents the most significant competitive threat — leveraging its massive wealth management and 401(k) recordkeeping relationships to cross-sell HSAs directly to CFOs, often using aggressive zero-fee pricing strategies that undercut HealthEquity. Optum (UnitedHealth Group) competes by tightly bundling HSAs with UnitedHealthcare's medical, dental, and vision products. WEX and smaller tech-forward competitors like Lively target employer accounts with modern, customizable platforms that expose HealthEquity's innovation gaps. Because HealthEquity operates as an independent provider without a bundled insurance or wealth management offering, it can be at a disadvantage when employers seek fully integrated, multi-line benefits packages. If Fidelity or Optum accelerate their HSA strategies, HealthEquity's new account growth rate could decelerate from mid-single-digits toward low-single-digits.

WageWorks Technical Debt and Integration Risk. Despite promoting its digital wallet and cloud architecture, experts reveal that HealthEquity carries significant technical debt — particularly from the complex integration of its 2019 WageWorks acquisition ($2B). The company has historically struggled with backend technological integration, and prolonged delays leave it exposed to operational risk, client dissatisfaction, and potential loss of employer accounts to more agile providers. The migration to the V5 cloud-based platform has addressed many issues (92% faster response times, 5x platform stability improvement), but legacy system complexity remains a drag on product velocity and innovation speed relative to tech-first competitors.

Regulatory and Legislative Risk — HSA Tax Advantages. HealthEquity's entire economic foundation rests on favorable U.S. tax laws regarding Health Savings Accounts. Any legislative action that diminishes, caps, or eliminates the tax-advantaged status of HSAs would fundamentally destroy the company's core value proposition and devastate its business model. While bipartisan support for HSAs remains strong (the OBBBA expanded eligibility), a future administration could pursue single-payer healthcare ("Medicare for All") legislation that would drastically shrink the addressable market for HDHPs and their paired HSAs. Additionally, as HealthEquity increasingly deploys AI for claims adjudication and customer service, emerging regulatory risks tied to data privacy, algorithmic bias, and the legalities of using personal healthcare information to train models represent a growing compliance burden.

Cybersecurity and Fraud Vulnerability. HealthEquity has been materially impacted by cyberattacks and sophisticated account fraud in recent years. Bad actors exploited the platform's lack of deposit holds, poor identity verification systems, and easy account takeover processes to drain member funds, resulting in elevated fraud reimbursement expenses ($11M in Q4 FY2025 alone). While management has dramatically improved security — deploying passkey authentication, partnering with Visa for AI-powered fraud detection (500+ risk attributes), and reducing quarterly fraud costs to ~$0.3M — the reputational damage from past breaches lingers, and any future security incident involving sensitive healthcare and financial data could cause severe and potentially permanent reputational harm. The target run rate of 1 basis point of total HSA assets per year for security costs (~$3.7M) leaves limited room for error.

4. Helmer's 7 Powers

1. Scale Economies Strong
HealthEquity's platform exhibits powerful scale economies across all three revenue streams. Custodial revenue has near-zero marginal cost — each incremental dollar of HSA assets deposited generates interest income without proportional cost increases. The Enhanced Rates program amplifies this: larger aggregate deposits give HealthEquity greater bargaining power with depository and insurance company partners, enabling better yield terms that smaller custodians cannot access. Service revenue benefits from fixed technology platform costs spread across 17.8 million accounts — the cost to maintain the V5 cloud platform, mobile app, and AI systems is largely independent of account volume, creating strong operating leverage (EBITDA margins expanded from 28% in FY2024 to 43% in FY2026). Interchange revenue scales with card transaction volume. The company's 5-year revenue CAGR of 12.4% has been achieved while driving EBITDA margins from ~22% to 43%, demonstrating that the incremental economics are highly favorable. At 10.6M HSAs — roughly 5x the scale of most competitors — HealthEquity's cost-per-account advantage is structurally significant and widening.

2. Network Effects Moderate
HealthEquity exhibits indirect network effects through its B2B2C distribution ecosystem. The platform is deeply integrated with 200+ Network Partners (health plans, benefits brokers, retirement recordkeepers, pharmacy benefit managers) through bidirectional data integrations and customized APIs. As more partners integrate with HealthEquity, the platform becomes more valuable to employers (who want seamless connectivity with their existing benefits ecosystem) and to additional partners (who want access to HealthEquity's 100,000+ employer client base). This creates a virtuous cycle: more partners → more employer clients → more members → more data → better AI/analytics → more partners. However, this is not a classic two-sided marketplace network effect — the value accrues primarily through ecosystem integration complexity rather than direct member-to-member interactions. The network effect is meaningful but not category-defining; a well-resourced competitor (Fidelity, Optum) could theoretically build comparable integrations with sufficient investment and time.

3. Counter-Positioning Strong
HealthEquity's counter-positioning against Fidelity, Optum/UnitedHealth, and traditional banks is genuine and powerful. Fidelity's economic model is built around using HSAs as loss leaders to capture broader wealth management relationships — it competes on zero-fee pricing that cannibalizes the service revenue HealthEquity earns. But Fidelity cannot replicate HealthEquity's deep integration with 200+ diverse health plan and benefits partners, because Fidelity's partners would view deep Fidelity integration as a competitive threat to their own financial services relationships. Optum/UnitedHealth bundles HSAs with UnitedHealthcare products — but this bundling strategy alienates the majority of employers who offer multiple insurance carrier options and need a carrier-agnostic HSA provider. HealthEquity's independence and agnostic integration capability is precisely the product that bundled competitors cannot offer without undermining their core business models. This counter-positioning is durable: the more deeply HealthEquity integrates with diverse partners, the more difficult it becomes for bundled competitors to match the breadth of connectivity.

4. Switching Costs Strong
HSA switching costs are among the highest in financial services because accounts are individually owned trust accounts. Migrating members to a new administrator requires explicit member consent, significant administrative effort from HR teams, and often triggers account closure fees. Employer retention rates are in the "high 90s" — virtually no employers voluntarily leave HealthEquity's platform. The stickiness is compounded by: (a) years of accumulated transaction history, claims data, and investment account configurations that would need to be rebuilt; (b) deep integration with employer payroll systems, benefits platforms, and HR workflows; (c) member inertia — individual HSA holders rarely change custodians, especially if they have investment accounts with specific portfolio allocations; and (d) the Enhanced Rates program, which locks HSA cash into multi-year contracts that cannot be easily unwound. Customer concentration risk is low (no single partner >6% of HSA accounts, 85% of employers have 500 or fewer employees), meaning no single client defection would be material. The combination of individual account ownership, employer integration complexity, and contractual lock-in creates a switching cost moat that is genuinely formidable.

5. Branding Moderate
HealthEquity has strong brand recognition within the benefits administration ecosystem — it is the go-to name that benefits brokers, HR departments, and health plan administrators associate with HSAs. The company's 24/7, 365-day U.S.-based customer service model has become a key differentiator and brand attribute, with the "Team Purple" culture driving high member satisfaction scores. The mobile app has surpassed 3.6 million downloads, and the Expedited Claims AI tool has won industry recognition awards. However, the brand is B2B-facing rather than consumer-facing — most individual HSA holders do not actively choose HealthEquity; they receive it through their employer's benefits enrollment. The brand reduces sales friction in the broker and employer channel but does not command a meaningful pricing premium over a hypothetical equally-capable competitor. As HealthEquity builds out its direct retail enrollment platform for ACA Bronze plan members, consumer brand recognition will become increasingly important but is currently underdeveloped.

6. Cornered Resource Moderate
HealthEquity's most significant cornered resource is its integrated network of 200+ bidirectional data connections with health plans, benefits brokers, retirement recordkeepers, and pharmacy benefit managers. Replicating these bespoke integrations — each customized with specific data models, API configurations, and compliance requirements — would require years of development and relationship-building from any new entrant. The company's accumulated dataset of 17.8M accounts, $36.5B in HSA assets, and decades of transaction history also represents a data asset that powers AI tools (Expedited Claims, HSAnswers) with domain-specific accuracy that generic models cannot match. CEO Scott Cutler, who joined in January 2025 with a strong technology background from StockX, eBay, and the NYSE, is driving a security and modernization overhaul that experts view optimistically. However, no single individual is irreplaceable, and the integration network — while valuable — is a function of institutional relationships rather than proprietary technology.

7. Process Power Moderate
HealthEquity has developed sophisticated processes for onboarding acquired HSA portfolios — a critical capability in an industry undergoing rapid consolidation. The company's track record of purchasing books of business (including the BenefitWallet HSA portfolio from Conduent for $425M in May 2024), migrating accounts onto its proprietary cloud architecture, and transitioning acquired cash into the higher-yielding Enhanced Rates platform represents accumulated institutional knowledge. The open-enrollment sales execution process — which consistently generates 1M+ new HSAs from sales annually despite macro headwinds — reflects a refined go-to-market machine. The "Team Purple" culture emphasizes member-first customer service that frequently wins over HR departments during competitive evaluations. However, these processes, while well-executed, are not fundamentally unique — large financial services companies develop comparable operational capabilities, and HealthEquity's relatively small workforce (~2,814 employees) limits the depth of institutional process knowledge.

Summary: HealthEquity is a 3–4 power business with genuine strength in Scale Economies (10.6M HSAs with dramatically lower unit costs than competitors), Counter-Positioning (carrier-agnostic independence that bundled competitors cannot replicate), and Switching Costs (individually owned trust accounts with employer retention in the high 90s). Network Effects, Branding, Cornered Resource, and Process Power are moderate and compounding. This is a strong 7 Powers profile for a financial services platform — the combination of scale and switching costs creates a durable moat that should protect the business through interest rate cycles and competitive pressure.

5. Porter's 5 Forces

1. Threat of New Entrants Low
Barriers to entry in HSA administration are formidable. A new entrant would need to: (a) obtain trust company or banking charter authorizations in all 50 states, (b) build bidirectional data integrations with hundreds of health plans, benefits brokers, and retirement recordkeepers — each requiring customized APIs and compliance protocols, (c) develop or license a compliant custodial infrastructure capable of managing billions in trust assets, (d) achieve sufficient scale to negotiate competitive yields with depository and insurance company partners for the Enhanced Rates-style programs that drive custodial economics, and (e) build the regulatory, compliance, and security infrastructure required to hold sensitive healthcare and financial data. The industry is consolidating, not fragmenting: smaller banks and financial institutions are exiting HSA administration (Conduent divested its BenefitWallet portfolio to HealthEquity; smaller custodians routinely sell their books of business). The most likely competitive threat is from adjacent incumbents (Fidelity, Optum) extending their existing capabilities, not from de novo entrants.

2. Bargaining Power of Suppliers Low
HealthEquity's primary "suppliers" are depository institutions and insurance companies that hold HSA cash deposits (earning a spread on the deposits), technology infrastructure providers (cloud, AI), and its workforce. For depository partners, HealthEquity's $36.5B in HSA assets provides substantial bargaining leverage — these are stable, long-duration deposits that banks and insurance companies value highly, and HealthEquity can negotiate favorable terms by threatening to redirect deposit flows among multiple partners. For technology, the company has built proprietary cloud infrastructure (V5 platform) and AI tools (Expedited Claims, HSAnswers), reducing dependence on any single vendor. The AI partnerships (Parloa for agentic customer service, Visa for fraud detection) are strategic but not sole-sourced — HealthEquity retains the proprietary data and domain knowledge that makes these integrations valuable. Employee talent is the most constrained supplier, but HealthEquity's Utah headquarters provides access to a cost-effective talent pool relative to coastal tech companies.

3. Bargaining Power of Buyers Moderate
HealthEquity's buyers include employers (who select the HSA custodian), benefits brokers (who recommend custodians), and individual members (who hold accounts). Employers have moderate bargaining power — large employers with thousands of employees can negotiate volume-based pricing, and the availability of competing custodians (Fidelity's zero-fee model, Optum's bundled approach) creates pricing pressure. However, buyer power is constrained by switching costs (moving thousands of individually owned accounts requires member-by-member consent), the low cost of HSA administration relative to total benefits spend (immaterial for most employers), and the fragmented nature of HealthEquity's client base (85% of employers have 500 or fewer employees, no single partner >6% of HSA accounts). Benefits brokers have moderate influence on custodian selection but are themselves integrated into HealthEquity's network, creating relationship lock-in. Individual members have very low bargaining power — they typically accept the custodian selected by their employer.

4. Threat of Substitutes Low
There is no meaningful substitute for the Health Savings Account as a tax-advantaged healthcare savings vehicle. The triple tax advantage (tax-deductible contributions, tax-free growth, tax-free qualified withdrawals) is unique in the U.S. tax code — no other financial product offers equivalent benefits for healthcare savings. FSAs are partial substitutes but have inferior economics ("use it or lose it" provisions, lower contribution limits, no investment option). HRAs are employer-funded only and non-portable. Traditional savings accounts offer no tax advantage. The OBBBA legislation has further entrenched HSAs by expanding eligibility and qualifying expense categories. The primary substitution risk is structural: if the U.S. were to adopt single-payer healthcare, eliminating HDHPs entirely, the addressable market would collapse. Short of that scenario, HSAs are becoming more deeply embedded in the American healthcare financing system with each legislative expansion, making substitution increasingly unlikely.

5. Competitive Rivalry High
The HSA administration market has consolidated around four dominant players — HealthEquity, Fidelity, Optum, and WEX — with intense competition for new employer accounts and existing portfolio acquisitions. Fidelity competes aggressively on price (zero-fee models) and leverages its massive 401(k) recordkeeping relationships to cross-sell HSAs directly to CFOs. Optum bundles HSAs with UnitedHealthcare products, creating competitive pressure when employers prioritize integrated benefits platforms. WEX competes primarily in direct-to-employer sales with strong broker relationships. Smaller, tech-forward competitors like Lively and Bend offer modern user experiences that highlight HealthEquity's legacy platform limitations. Pricing pressure is real: Fidelity's zero-fee approach forces HealthEquity to compete on service quality, integration breadth, and custodial yield rather than price. However, the market is growing fast enough (national HSA assets $146B+ and expanding) that competition has not prevented HealthEquity from adding 1M+ new HSAs annually. The rivalry is intense on new account acquisition but moderate on retention (high-90s employer retention rates), suggesting that competition primarily affects growth rates rather than the existing installed base.

Summary: HealthEquity's five-force structure is favorable with one notable exception: competitive rivalry is high and intensifying. Low threat of new entrants, low supplier power, low substitution risk, and moderate buyer power create a structurally protected position — but the intensity of competition from Fidelity (zero-fee pricing), Optum (bundled products), and tech-forward disruptors means that HealthEquity must continue investing in platform modernization, AI capabilities, and the Enhanced Rates program to maintain its market leadership. The key insight is that competition affects growth velocity more than installed base retention — which is why the secular tailwind (healthcare affordability crisis, OBBBA) is so important for the thesis.

6. What Drives the Stock?

The following are the 6 variables that would move HQY's share price materially in the next 12 months. These are what analysts build tracking models around and PMs ask about in morning meetings.

1. Interest Rate Trajectory and Custodial Yield. Custodial revenue ($636.8M, 48.5% of total) is the single largest revenue line and the most profitable segment. The market's perception of HealthEquity's interest rate sensitivity — and the degree to which the Enhanced Rates program actually insulates blended yields — is the dominant driver of the stock. Each earnings call is scrutinized for average custodial yield per account, the percentage of HSA cash in Enhanced Rates contracts, and the maturity profile of those contracts. A Federal Reserve rate-cutting cycle that is slower/shallower than expected would be a significant positive catalyst; a rapid rate-cutting cycle would test the Enhanced Rates insulation thesis and likely pressure the stock regardless of actual financial impact.

2. HSA Account Growth and New HSAs from Sales. HealthEquity has generated over 1 million new HSAs from sales in each of the last two fiscal years. This metric is the leading indicator of future revenue and asset growth. The Q1 FY2027 print (May 28) will be the first data point showing whether the OBBBA-driven expansion of HSA eligibility to Bronze plan enrollees is translating into measurable new account growth. A meaningful uptick in new HSAs from sales (particularly from the direct retail enrollment channel) would validate the OBBBA bull case; a flat or declining number would suggest the legislative catalyst is overhyped.

3. Total HSA Assets and Investment Account Penetration. Total HSA assets ($36.5B, +14% YoY) and HSA invested assets ($18.5B, +26% YoY) are the balance sheet metrics that drive the custodial revenue engine. Asset growth compounds through three mechanisms: new account additions, member contributions, and investment appreciation. The investment penetration rate (currently ~8% of members) is the most under-appreciated growth lever — each percentage point increase in penetration represents roughly 106,000 new investment accounts, each generating incremental advisory and management fees. This metric is a leading indicator of the investment flywheel thesis.

8. AlphaSense Insights
AlphaSense Research Reports · Subject: HealthEquity, Inc. (HQY) · Reports Reviewed: AlphaSense Deep Dive & Company Primer, Q1–Q4 FY2026 Earnings Transcripts, AGM Transcript, FactSet Snapshot
Key themes synthesized from AlphaSense competitive intelligence, expert calls, management commentary, and industry research.

The "Fintech Innovator" Perception Is Misleading — HealthEquity Is a Legacy Provider With Deep Moats. Perhaps the most important analytical insight from expert calls is that despite promoting its digital wallet and cloud architecture, HealthEquity actually operates more like an antiquated legacy service provider carrying significant technical debt from its WageWorks acquisition — winning business through brand reputation, unparalleled network integration (200+ partners), and traditional human-led customer service rather than disruptive software. This is simultaneously a bear case (innovation gaps relative to Lively, WEX) and a bull case (the moat is not in the technology but in the ecosystem connectivity and switching costs). Experts consistently distinguish between HealthEquity's competitive positioning (extremely strong due to integration depth and account stickiness) and its technology platform (adequate but not best-in-class). The implication for investors is that HealthEquity's durability is underestimated by those who evaluate it as a fintech, and overestimated by those who assume the technology platform will be a source of competitive advantage.

Enhanced Rates Program Is the Most Misunderstood Aspect of the Business. The market's default assumption is that HealthEquity's custodial revenue will compress linearly with Federal Reserve rate cuts. Expert analysis reveals this assumption is materially wrong. The Enhanced Rates program channels HSA cash into high-yield insurance bonds and multi-year annuity contracts that lock in yields for extended periods — management targets 60% of HSA cash in these enhanced contracts by FY2027. Because only a small fraction of contracts reprice in any given year, the blended yield compression in a rate-cutting cycle would be far slower than the market models. Forward contracts on U.S. Treasuries ($2.35–$2.4B notional) provide additional insulation. The key analytical point: HealthEquity has effectively transformed what was historically a variable-rate, interest-rate-sensitive revenue stream into a significantly more durable, quasi-fixed-rate revenue stream — but the market still values it as though it were fully variable.

The Investment Flywheel Is Massively Under-Penetrated. One of the most compelling data points from the research is that over 90% of HealthEquity's HSA members have not yet opened an investment account, despite invested assets surging 26% to $18.5B and crossing the 50% threshold of total HSA assets. This represents a massive embedded option: converting even a fraction of the 9.7M non-investing HSA holders into investors would dramatically increase lifetime account value without requiring net-new customer acquisition. Management's three-tier advisory model (Self-driven, GPS guidance, AutoPilot) is designed specifically to address this opportunity. The HealthEquity Marketplace — connecting members to eligible products like GLP-1 weight management programs, diagnostics, and telehealth — further deepens engagement and positions the HSA as a healthcare financial operating system rather than a passive savings account.

OBBBA Legislation Is a Genuine Game-Changer. The "One Big Beautiful Bill Act" signed in July 2025 is not merely incremental — it represents a structural expansion of the HSA addressable market. By permanently categorizing all Bronze and Catastrophic ACA plans as HSA-compatible, the law unlocked HSA eligibility for 7 million previously ineligible Americans. The timing is significant: enhanced Premium Tax Credits are expiring, driving consumers from Silver to Bronze plans — with Bronze plan selection hitting 40% nationally (the highest ever). The law also modernizes qualified medical expenses to include telehealth and Direct Primary Care arrangements, expanding the utility of HSA funds. HealthEquity's launch of a direct retail HSA enrollment platform specifically targeting these newly eligible Bronze plan members positions the company to capture a disproportionate share of this new market segment.

Key Investor Questions. Based on the earnings transcripts and expert calls, the questions that would most sharpen conviction on HealthEquity are: (1) What is the actual pace of OBBBA-driven retail HSA enrollment — are the 7M newly eligible Americans converting into accounts at scale? (2) What is the true maturity profile and yield sensitivity of the Enhanced Rates portfolio — how much custodial yield compression would occur in a 200bp Fed rate-cutting cycle? (3) What is the investment account penetration trajectory — can HealthEquity accelerate conversion beyond the current ~8% penetration rate? (4) How is Fidelity's zero-fee HSA strategy performing — is it gaining share among large employers, and at what rate? (5) What is the timeline for AI-driven cost reduction to reduce service expense per account to a level that enables margin expansion even in a flat-growth environment?

3-year CAGR base_assets = 36.5 base_cagr = 0.12 years = 3 # FY2026 (starting) to FY2029 (ending) rows = [] for assets in starting_assets: row = [] for g in asset_cagrs: end_assets = assets * (1 + g) ** years row.append(round(end_assets, 1)) rows.append(row) df1 = pd.DataFrame(rows, index=[f"${x}B" for x in starting_assets], columns=[f"{int(g*100)}%" for g in asset_cagrs]) print("FY2029E Total HSA Assets ($B)") print(df1.to_string()) print(f"\nBase: ${base_assets}B @ {base_cagr*100:.0f}% CAGR -> ${base_assets*(1+base_cagr)**years:.1f}B") # ─── SENSITIVITY TABLE 2: FY2029E Adjusted EBITDA ($M) ─── revenues = [1400, 1500, 1625, 1750, 1900] # $M total revenue ebitda_margins = [0.38, 0.42, 0.46, 0.50, 0.54] # EBITDA margin % base_rev = 1625 base_ebitda_mgn = 0.464 rows2 = [] for rev in revenues: row = [] for m in ebitda_margins: ebitda = rev * m row.append(round(ebitda, 0)) rows2.append(row) df2 = pd.DataFrame(rows2, index=[f"${x}M" for x in revenues], columns=[f"{int(m*100)}%" for m in ebitda_margins]) print("\nFY2029E Adjusted EBITDA ($M)") print(df2.to_string()) print(f"Base: ${base_rev}M x {base_ebitda_mgn*100:.1f}% = ${base_rev*base_ebitda_mgn:.0f}M") # ─── SENSITIVITY TABLE 3: Implied Share Price ─── fcf_per_share = [4.17, 5.66, 6.45, 7.65, 9.38] # $/share p_fcf_multiples = [10, 13, 16, 20, 24] # x P/FCF base_fcf_ps = 6.45 base_pfcf = 16 rows3 = [] for fps in fcf_per_share: row = [] for mult in p_fcf_multiples: price = fps * mult row.append(round(price, 2)) rows3.append(row) df3 = pd.DataFrame(rows3, index=[f"${x}" for x in fcf_per_share], columns=[f"{x}x" for x in p_fcf_multiples]) print("\nImplied Share Price ($)") print(df3.to_string()) print(f"Base: ${base_fcf_ps}/share x {base_pfcf}x = ${base_fcf_ps*base_pfcf:.2f}") # ─── SCENARIO MODEL ─── scenarios = { "Bear Severe": {"prob": 0.05, "price": 41.67}, "Bear Mild": {"prob": 0.20, "price": 73.61}, "Base Case": {"prob": 0.50, "price": 103.22}, "Bull Mild": {"prob": 0.20, "price": 153.09}, "Bull Severe": {"prob": 0.05, "price": 225.00}, } current_price = 82.18 cost_of_capital = 0.20 prob_wtd = sum(s["prob"] * s["price"] for s in scenarios.values()) pv = prob_wtd / (1 + cost_of_capital) upside = (pv - current_price) / current_price print(f"\nProb-weighted avg: ${prob_wtd:.2f}") print(f"PV @ {cost_of_capital*100:.0f}% CoC: ${pv:.2f}") print(f"Current price: ${current_price}") print(f"Implied upside: {upside*100:.1f}%")
