BitGo Holdings, Inc. | Preliminary Memo for Theia
1. Business Overview

BitGo Holdings, Inc. (NYSE: BTGO) is the first publicly traded, federally chartered digital asset infrastructure company in the United States. Founded in 2013 by Mike Belshe in Palo Alto, California, BitGo pioneered multi-signature wallet technology (2-of-3 key architecture) and has grown into the dominant institutional-grade custody, trading, and settlement platform for digital assets. The company holds an OCC-chartered national trust bank license (BitGo Bank & Trust) and operates 7+ regulated custodial entities globally, including licenses from NYDFS, Switzerland's FINMA, Germany's BaFin (MiCA-compliant), Dubai's VARA, and Singapore's MAS, with South Korea forthcoming. BitGo completed its IPO on January 22, 2026 on the NYSE at $18/share, raising approximately $198M and valuing the company at ~$2.1B. As of year-end 2025, BitGo serves 5,322 institutional clients across 100+ countries, maintains $81.6B in Assets on Platform (AoP), $15.6B in staked assets, and supports 1,700+ digital assets. The company employs approximately 603 people.

BitGo's product suite spans five core business lines. Qualified Custody provides bankruptcy-remote, cold-storage safekeeping for institutional digital assets — the foundational product that underpins all other services. Go Network is a 24/7 off-exchange settlement layer that enables institutional counterparties to trade and settle without moving assets to centralized exchange hot wallets, directly addressing the counterparty risk exposed by the FTX collapse. BitGo Prime offers trading (spot, derivatives launched January 2026 with $3B+ notional in 3 months), lending ($207.4M book, +114% YoY), and borrowing services. Staking operates through 40+ validator partners, generating yield on $15.6B of staked assets. Stablecoin-as-a-Service (StaaS) is a new product launched in FY2025 that enables third parties to issue white-label stablecoins using BitGo's infrastructure — USD1 reached $5B+ AUM within 3 months, and SoFiUSD is in development. Additional emerging products include Crypto-as-a-Service (CaaS API) for fintechs, an MCP server for AI/agentic wallets, and a Bitcoin treasury strategy (1,673 BTC held at year-end 2025). BitGo's key partnerships include Fidelity (Solana ETF custody), 21Shares (global ETF custody), SoFi (SoFiUSD stablecoin), Susquehanna (prediction markets), and Figure (tokenized equities ATS). Critically, BitGo does not trade against its clients and does not rehypothecate client assets — a structural impartiality that distinguishes it from vertically integrated exchanges like Coinbase.

Revenue composition: FY2025 gross revenue was $16.15B, of which Digital Asset Sales (principal trading volume) comprised 96.4%. However, this gross figure is misleading — BitGo acts as a principal in digital asset trades, buying and selling crypto for institutional clients, and the gross revenue includes the full notional value of transactions. The actual economic value captured is the spread (~21 bps average take rate in FY2025). Starting in FY2026, consensus estimates shift to net revenue reporting (~$223M FY2026E), which reflects the true economic revenue. Other revenue streams include Staking ($385M gross, ~$27M net at ~7% take rate), Subscriptions & Services ($121.5M, +57% YoY), StaaS ($66.7M at ~16 bps average take rate on AUM), and Interest Income ($1.5M). BitGo operates a dual-class share structure with CEO Mike Belshe holding >50% voting power through Class B shares.

Valuation Summary

BitGo currently trades at $10.27, representing a 58% decline from its IPO price of $18 and 42% of its 52-week high of $24.50. The stock's dramatic post-IPO decline reflects a combination of crypto market weakness, broader risk-off sentiment toward newly public companies, and fundamental confusion about BitGo's revenue reporting (the gross-to-net transition makes year-over-year comparisons nearly impossible for non-specialist analysts). On a P/Net Sales basis, BitGo trades at 5.3x FY2026E net revenue of $223M and 4.0x FY2027E, which compresses to 3.0x on FY2028E. On a P/Gross Profit basis — arguably the most relevant metric given the business model — the stock trades at 5.2x FY2026E gross profit ($226M), compressing to 2.9x by FY2028E. Traditional EV-based multiples (EV/EBITDA, EV/Sales) are entirely meaningless because BitGo's $4.0B cash balance includes stablecoin reserves and client assets held in trust, generating a negative enterprise value of ($2.28B). True operating cash is approximately $178M (FY2026E estimate). BitGo reaches GAAP profitability in FY2027E ($1.20 EPS), implying 37.2x P/E on first profitable year earnings — elevated but not unusual for a high-growth infrastructure company turning the profitability corner. By FY2028E, P/E compresses to 21.4x ($1.57 EPS). The consensus target of $14.69 (11 brokers) implies 43% upside, with a unanimous Buy rating (1.15 score). The valuation is compressed by IPO lock-up overhang, post-IPO selling pressure, and the fundamental misunderstanding of the custody business model's balance sheet. The 32.87% WACC reflects the market's perception of extreme risk, which we believe is overdone for a federally chartered, OCC-regulated trust bank with $81.6B in AoP.

3. Bull & Bear Cases
Bull Cases

GENIUS Act and Stablecoin-as-a-Service Explosion. The GENIUS Act (Guiding and Establishing National Innovation for U.S. Stablecoins) is the single most important regulatory catalyst for BitGo. If enacted, it would create a federal licensing framework for stablecoin issuers that directly plays to BitGo's strengths: OCC-chartered trust bank, qualified custodian, and existing StaaS infrastructure. USD1 reached $5B+ AUM within 3 months of launch, validating the product-market fit. If the GENIUS Act passes and drives a wave of new stablecoin issuance — banks, fintechs, and corporates issuing their own branded stablecoins backed by treasuries held in BitGo custody — the StaaS revenue stream could grow from $67M (FY2025) to $300M+ within 3 years. At 15-20 bps take rate on a $200B stablecoin AUM base, this would be transformational. SoFiUSD, the next major client, validates that mainstream fintech is adopting the model. BitGo's regulatory moat (OCC charter + NYDFS + BaFin + VARA) makes it the natural infrastructure partner for any regulated entity seeking to issue stablecoins.

Go Network Becomes the Institutional Settlement Standard. Go Network addresses the single biggest problem exposed by the FTX collapse: counterparty risk in centralized exchange settlement. By enabling institutional counterparties to trade off-exchange while assets remain in BitGo's bankruptcy-remote custody, Go Network eliminates the need to fund exchange hot wallets — a structural risk that every institutional crypto allocator now understands. If Go Network achieves critical mass and becomes the default settlement layer for institutional crypto trading (analogous to DTCC for equities), the network effects would be enormous: every new participant increases liquidity, reduces settlement costs, and makes the platform more valuable for all users. This could transform BitGo from a custody provider into a systemically important financial market infrastructure (FMI), justifying infrastructure-like multiples (15-20x revenue) rather than exchange multiples (5-8x).

ETF Custody Flywheel Accelerates. BitGo already provides custody for Fidelity's Solana ETF and 21Shares' global ETF suite. As the crypto ETF market expands beyond Bitcoin and Ethereum into altcoins (Solana, XRP, staked ETH, multi-asset baskets), the demand for qualified, bankruptcy-remote custody scales proportionally. BitGo's OCC charter and multi-asset support (1,700+ tokens) position it as the custodian of choice for ETF issuers who need regulatory-grade infrastructure. Each ETF mandate brings recurring custody fees, cross-sell opportunities (staking, trading, settlement), and balance sheet growth. If crypto ETF AUM reaches $500B+ globally over the next 3 years, BitGo's share of custody mandates could drive AoP well above $100B and custody revenue into the hundreds of millions.

Derivatives Trading and Prime Brokerage Expansion. BitGo launched derivatives trading in January 2026 and executed $3B+ in notional volume within the first 3 months. The derivatives market in crypto is significantly larger than spot (typically 3-5x in mature markets), and institutional demand for regulated derivatives infrastructure (perpetual swaps, options, structured products) is growing rapidly. If BitGo builds a meaningful share of the institutional crypto derivatives market alongside its existing lending book ($207.4M, +114% YoY), it could develop a full-service prime brokerage offering that generates 3-5x the revenue per client compared to custody alone. The multi-product attach rate data is already encouraging: 70% of revenue-generating clients use 2+ products and 50% use 3+.

AI/Agentic Wallets and MCP Server Optionality. BitGo's MCP (Model Context Protocol) server for AI/agentic wallets is an early-stage but potentially transformational product. As AI agents increasingly manage financial transactions autonomously, they need secure, programmatic wallet infrastructure with multi-sig controls — precisely what BitGo has built. If autonomous agents become a significant source of crypto transaction volume (some estimates suggest 10-20% of on-chain activity by 2028), BitGo's first-mover position in agentic wallet infrastructure could create an entirely new revenue stream with no direct competitor. The multi-sig architecture (2-of-3 keys) maps naturally to agentic needs: the AI agent holds one key, the human supervisor holds one, and BitGo holds the recovery key.

Bear Cases

Crypto Price Correlation Drives Revenue Volatility. BitGo's revenue is fundamentally tied to digital asset prices. Management explicitly states that revenue "significantly fluctuates" with BTC and ETH prices. A sustained crypto bear market (BTC below $40K, ETH below $1,500) would compress trading volumes, reduce custody AUM, lower staking yields, and shrink StaaS AUM — all simultaneously. The FY2025 net loss of ($14.8M) was driven largely by BTC treasury unrealized losses, demonstrating how fair-value accounting for the 1,673 BTC position creates direct P&L exposure to crypto prices. In a severe downturn, revenue could decline 40-60% from peak levels, and the stock — with a 1.72 beta — would amplify market losses. The concentrated asset exposure (BTC and ETH represent the majority of AoP) means BitGo lacks the diversification to buffer a single-asset drawdown.

Custody Fee Commoditization. Pure custody at 15-20 bps is commoditizing as more players enter the market: Coinbase Custody, Fireblocks, Anchorage, and traditional banks (BNY Mellon, State Street) are all building or acquiring digital asset custody capabilities. As institutional custody becomes table-stakes infrastructure, pricing pressure will compress margins toward zero — similar to the fee compression in traditional custody/fund administration. BitGo's strategy of cross-selling into prime, staking, and settlement services is the correct response, but if the cross-sell fails and custody fees compress faster than new revenue streams ramp, BitGo could be left as a low-margin utility. The AlphaSense research explicitly flags this risk: custody alone is not a sustainable standalone business.

Post-IPO Execution Risk and Lock-Up Pressure. BitGo went public only 3 months ago at $18/share and has already declined 43% to $10.27. The company has never reported quarterly earnings as a public company (first report is June 3, 2026). Lock-up expiration in July 2026 will release insider shares into the market, potentially creating significant selling pressure. CEO Belshe's dual-class structure (>50% voting power) means minority shareholders have limited governance influence. The FY2026E consensus EPS of ($0.95) implies that BitGo will lose money in its first full year as a public company, which is a challenging narrative for a stock that has already lost nearly half its IPO value. The $20.06M of dilutive shares (stock compensation) represent 17.4% of basic shares — material dilution for a company that is not yet generating positive free cash flow.

Regulatory Reversal or Enforcement Risk. BitGo's regulatory moat (OCC charter, NYDFS, BaFin, VARA) is currently a competitive advantage, but the regulatory landscape for digital assets remains highly uncertain. A change in OCC leadership, new legislation that restricts bank involvement in crypto, or an enforcement action against BitGo or a client could undermine the regulatory narrative. The GENIUS Act could fail to pass, eliminating the StaaS tailwind. International regulatory environments (MiCA implementation, Dubai VARA evolution) could become more restrictive. And the mere perception of regulatory risk — e.g., an SEC enforcement action against a BitGo client — could trigger institutional client outflows and AoP declines.

Coinbase and Integrated Exchange Competition. Coinbase is BitGo's most formidable competitor: it offers custody, trading, staking, and prime brokerage in a vertically integrated platform with a massive retail and institutional client base. Coinbase's brand recognition, fiat on/off-ramps, and exchange liquidity give it structural advantages that BitGo cannot replicate as a pure infrastructure player. If Coinbase successfully defends its institutional custody market share while expanding into settlement (Base L2) and stablecoins (USDC partnership), BitGo could find itself squeezed between Coinbase's scale and Fireblocks' software-only cost structure. The "impartial infrastructure" positioning is philosophically appealing but may not generate sufficient premium pricing if clients ultimately prefer one-stop-shop convenience.

4. Helmer's 7 Powers

1. Scale Economies Moderate
BitGo's custody platform has high fixed costs (security infrastructure, compliance teams, multi-sig key management systems, regulatory licensing across 7+ jurisdictions) and near-zero marginal cost per additional asset under custody. Once the custody vaults, HSMs (Hardware Security Modules), and compliance frameworks are built, storing the 5,323rd client's assets costs roughly the same as storing the 5,322nd's. This creates operating leverage: gross profit should scale disproportionately to AoP growth. However, the scale advantages are constrained by two factors: (1) the institutional digital asset custody market is still relatively small (~$500B total addressable AoP across all providers), limiting the absolute magnitude of scale benefits, and (2) each new jurisdiction requires incremental regulatory licensing costs, compliance staff, and legal infrastructure that partially offset the marginal cost advantage. The scale economy is real within each regulated jurisdiction but does not compound as powerfully as a pure software company because the regulated trust bank model requires physical and legal infrastructure in each market.

2. Network Effects Strong
Go Network exhibits genuine network effects: every institutional counterparty that joins the off-exchange settlement network increases the liquidity available to all other participants, reduces settlement costs, and makes the platform more valuable. This is a classic two-sided network effect — traders want to be on the network where the most counterparties settle, and counterparties want to be where the most liquidity flows. The network effect compounds with BitGo's custody base: clients already custodying assets at BitGo can access Go Network settlement with zero incremental capital movement, creating a structural advantage over competitors who would require clients to pre-fund settlement accounts. The StaaS network also exhibits indirect network effects: as more stablecoin issuers adopt BitGo's infrastructure, the ecosystem of merchants, exchanges, and DeFi protocols that integrate with BitGo-issued stablecoins grows, making the platform more attractive for the next issuer. The combination of custody + settlement + stablecoin infrastructure creates a multi-layered network effect that is difficult for any single competitor to replicate.

3. Counter-Positioning Strong
BitGo's most powerful competitive positioning is its structural impartiality: it does not operate an exchange, does not trade against clients, and does not rehypothecate client assets. This is a genuine counter-position against the vertically integrated exchanges (Coinbase, Binance, Kraken) that serve as both custodian and trading counterparty — a conflict of interest that was dramatically exposed by FTX's collapse. For these exchanges to match BitGo's positioning, they would need to dismantle their most profitable business lines (exchange trading fees, market-making against client flow, rehypothecation-funded lending). Coinbase earns ~65% of revenue from transaction fees on its exchange; splitting off custody as an independent, non-conflicted service would cannibalize its core business model. Traditional banks (BNY Mellon, State Street) entering crypto custody face a different counter-positioning dynamic: they cannot build the crypto-native technical infrastructure (multi-sig, DeFi integration, 1,700+ token support) without fundamentally restructuring their technology stacks and risk frameworks. BitGo sits in the structural gap between crypto-native exchanges (conflicted) and traditional banks (technically constrained).

4. Switching Costs Strong
Custody switching costs in digital assets are extreme. Moving institutional-scale digital assets between custodians requires: (a) complete re-execution of key ceremony procedures (generating new multi-sig wallet addresses, distributing key shards to new signatories), (b) updating all counterparty settlement instructions across potentially hundreds of trading relationships, (c) migrating staking delegations (which often have unbonding periods of 7-28 days during which assets are illiquid and at risk), (d) re-establishing cold storage policies and insurance coverage, and (e) managing the operational risk of large-value transfers during the migration window. For ETF issuers, switching custodians requires SEC filings and prospectus amendments. The multi-product attach rate data confirms stickiness: 70% of revenue-generating clients use 2+ products and 50% use 3+, meaning clients who custody also trade, stake, lend, and settle through BitGo — creating a deeply integrated relationship that becomes exponentially harder to unwind with each additional product adopted. The average quarterly custody client spend of $11.1K (1,534 clients) suggests deep operational embedding.

5. Branding Moderate
BitGo has strong brand recognition within the institutional digital asset community: it is the pioneer of multi-sig technology (2013), the first OCC-chartered digital asset trust bank, and the custodian of choice for marquee clients (Fidelity, 21Shares, SoFi, Susquehanna). The brand carries critical trust signals — federal charter, 12+ years of operating history without a security breach, bankruptcy-remote structure — that matter enormously when institutions are selecting a custodian for billions of dollars of digital assets. In a market scarred by FTX, Celsius, and BlockFi failures, BitGo's track record is a genuine brand asset. However, the brand is not consumer-facing (BitGo has only 1.2M individual users, compared to Coinbase's 100M+), and in the institutional market, brand recognition reduces sales cycle friction but does not command a significant pricing premium over an equally-qualified competitor. The brand is necessary but not sufficient — it gets BitGo into the RFP, but custody pricing (15-20 bps) is ultimately competitive.

6. Cornered Resource Strong
BitGo's OCC federal charter is a genuine cornered resource. There are fewer than five federally chartered digital asset trust banks in the United States, and the regulatory path to obtaining a new charter has become significantly more difficult since 2022. The charter provides: (a) the ability to custody assets as a national trust bank with federal preemption of state money transmitter laws, (b) access to the Federal Reserve payment system (pending), (c) regulatory credibility that satisfies institutional compliance requirements, and (d) a structural advantage in stablecoin-as-a-service (the GENIUS Act would require stablecoin issuers to use chartered trust banks as reserve custodians). The combination of OCC + NYDFS + BaFin MiCA + Dubai VARA + Singapore MAS creates a regulatory stack that no competitor fully matches — Coinbase has New York BitLicense but not an OCC charter; Anchorage has an OCC charter but limited international presence; Fireblocks has no banking charter at all. Founder/CEO Mike Belshe is also a cornered resource: deeply technical (former VP of Engineering at Google, co-invented SPDY/HTTP2 protocol), he has built BitGo's entire technology architecture and holds >50% voting control through the dual-class structure.

7. Process Power Moderate
BitGo has developed sophisticated institutional onboarding, key management, and compliance processes refined over 12 years and 5,322 client deployments. The multi-sig key ceremony process — generating, distributing, and securely storing key shards across geographically distributed vaults — is a complex operational procedure that requires deep expertise in cryptographic security, physical security, and regulatory compliance. BitGo's ability to support 1,700+ digital assets across multiple chains, each with unique wallet architectures, signing mechanisms, and network requirements, represents accumulated technical knowledge that would take a new entrant years to replicate. However, this process power is not unique — Coinbase Custody and Anchorage have developed comparable capabilities — and the company's relatively small workforce (~603 employees) limits the depth of institutional process knowledge compared to traditional custody banks with thousands of operations staff.

Summary: BitGo is a 3-4 power business with genuine strength in Network Effects (Go Network settlement layer), Counter-Positioning (structural impartiality vs. conflicted exchanges), Switching Costs (extreme custody migration friction), and Cornered Resource (OCC federal charter, global regulatory stack, founder-CEO). Scale Economies, Branding, and Process Power are moderate and developing. This is a strong 7 Powers profile for a digital asset infrastructure company — the combination of counter-positioning and cornered resource creates a regulatory moat that is difficult to replicate and becomes more valuable as institutional adoption of digital assets accelerates.

5. Porter's 5 Forces

1. Threat of New Entrants Low
The barriers to entry in institutional digital asset custody are formidable and increasing. A new entrant would need to: (a) obtain an OCC charter or equivalent state trust charter — a process that takes 2-5 years with no guarantee of approval and has become significantly harder since the OCC paused new crypto charter applications in 2022; (b) build multi-sig wallet infrastructure supporting 1,700+ tokens across dozens of blockchains; (c) establish physical cold storage vaults with military-grade security; (d) hire compliance teams with expertise in securities law, bank regulatory compliance, and digital asset-specific regulations across multiple jurisdictions; (e) obtain insurance coverage for digital asset custody (a small and expensive market); and (f) build a 5-10 year track record of secure operations to win institutional trust. The capital, time, and regulatory requirements are substantial. The most likely competitive threats come from adjacent incumbents — traditional custodian banks (BNY Mellon, State Street) adding crypto capabilities, or crypto-native exchanges (Coinbase) deepening their custody offerings — rather than true new entrants. Both face the counter-positioning dynamics discussed above.

2. Bargaining Power of Suppliers Low
BitGo's primary "suppliers" are cloud infrastructure providers (for non-custodial services), hardware security module (HSM) manufacturers, blockchain validator operators (40+ staking partners), and engineering talent. Cloud and HSM providers are competitive markets with multiple alternatives. Staking validator partners operate in a commoditized market where BitGo can switch validators without client impact. Engineering talent is the most constrained supplier, but BitGo's Palo Alto location, crypto-native culture, and founder-led technical team give it access to motivated engineers interested in blockchain infrastructure. The MCP/AI partnerships are early-stage and not yet material supplier dependencies. Overall, supplier power is low — BitGo's infrastructure is built on widely available components, with the differentiation coming from the integration, security, and regulatory layers that BitGo itself controls.

3. Bargaining Power of Buyers Moderate
BitGo's 5,322 institutional clients include some of the world's largest and most sophisticated financial institutions — hedge funds, ETF issuers, sovereign wealth funds, and crypto-native funds with professional procurement functions. Buyer power is moderated by several factors: (a) switching costs are extreme (as discussed above), reducing the credibility of buyer threats to leave; (b) custody fees (15-20 bps) are small relative to clients' total AUM management costs; and (c) the number of qualified, federally chartered custodians is very small, limiting alternatives. However, buyer power is elevated by two factors: (1) the largest clients (ETF issuers, major funds) have significant individual bargaining power due to the size of their mandates, and (2) as more competitors enter the custody market, the number of viable alternatives increases, giving clients more negotiating leverage. The top 10 clients likely represent a disproportionate share of AoP, creating concentration risk. Overall: moderate — switching costs and limited alternatives protect pricing, but large clients can negotiate favorable terms.

4. Threat of Substitutes Low
For institutional digital asset custody, the substitutes are: (a) self-custody (institutions managing their own multi-sig wallets), which is operationally complex and creates single-point-of-failure risk; (b) exchange custody (leaving assets on exchanges), which was discredited by FTX; and (c) MPC (multi-party computation) wallet software like Fireblocks, which provides wallet-level security but not qualified custody or bankruptcy-remote protection. For institutions with fiduciary obligations (ETF issuers, pension funds, RIAs), a qualified custodian is a regulatory requirement — self-custody and exchange custody are not permissible substitutes. The Go Network settlement layer has no close substitute: there is no other off-exchange, 24/7 institutional settlement network operating at comparable scale. The StaaS product is also difficult to substitute: building white-label stablecoin infrastructure requires a trust bank charter, reserve management capabilities, and integration with blockchain settlement rails — capabilities that only a handful of institutions possess. The MCP server for agentic wallets has no direct substitute today.

5. Competitive Rivalry Moderate
The institutional digital asset infrastructure market features several well-funded competitors: Coinbase (vertically integrated exchange + custody, ~$60B market cap), Fireblocks (wallet infrastructure, private, $8B last private valuation), Anchorage (OCC-chartered, private), and emerging traditional players (BNY Mellon, State Street, Fidelity building in-house). AlphaSense expert calls characterize the competitive landscape as: BitGo strongest in enterprise/institutional (impartial, OCC-chartered), Coinbase strongest in integrated/fiat (exchange liquidity, brand), Fireblocks strongest in wallet software (developer-friendly, MPC), and Anchorage strongest in SMB/emerging crypto companies. Competition is increasing but not yet destructive because the market is growing faster than competitive intensity — total institutional crypto AUM is expanding as ETFs, tokenized assets, and stablecoins drive new inflows. The risk is that as the market matures, custody becomes a commoditized utility and competition shifts entirely to prime brokerage, settlement, and stablecoin services. Overall: moderate — growing market absorbs competitive entry, but fee compression on pure custody is a structural trend.

Summary: BitGo's five-force structure is favorable for a digital asset infrastructure company: low threat of new entrants (regulatory barriers), low supplier power, moderate buyer power (mitigated by switching costs), low substitution risk (qualified custody is a regulatory requirement), and moderate competitive rivalry (growing market, but custody fee pressure). The key structural risk is custody commoditization — which makes the cross-sell into settlement (Go Network), stablecoins (StaaS), and prime brokerage critical to long-term margin defense. The regulatory moat (OCC charter) is the single most important barrier protecting BitGo's competitive position.

Regulatory Moat: The Deepest in Digital Assets. The most important analytical insight from the AlphaSense research is that BitGo's competitive advantage is not simply "custody" but specifically the regulatory infrastructure stack: OCC federal charter + NYDFS trust license + BaFin MiCA license + Dubai VARA + Singapore MAS, with South Korea forthcoming. The GENIUS Act would further cement this advantage by requiring stablecoin issuers to use chartered trust banks for reserve custody — effectively creating a regulatory mandate for BitGo's services.

Pure-Play Infrastructure: The Impartiality Advantage. A recurring theme in expert call transcripts is BitGo's structural impartiality: unlike Coinbase, which operates an exchange and trades against client flow, BitGo is a pure infrastructure provider that does not compete with its clients. This positioning was significantly strengthened by the FTX collapse, which demonstrated the catastrophic consequences of commingling custody and trading (FTX used client custodied assets to fund Alameda Research's trading losses). Post-FTX, institutional allocators have become acutely focused on custodian independence — segregation of custody from trading, bankruptcy-remote structures, and no rehypothecation. BitGo's architecture was designed from inception to satisfy these requirements, giving it a structural credibility advantage that exchange-affiliated custodians must overcome. Expert interviewees note that this impartiality is particularly valued by ETF issuers, pension funds, and sovereign wealth funds — the largest and most compliance-sensitive institutional categories.

Misunderstood Revenue: Institutional Infrastructure, Not Retail Trading. The AlphaSense analysis highlights a critical market misperception: many generalist investors and analysts treat BitGo's revenue as if it were retail crypto trading revenue — highly volatile, correlated to crypto prices, and similar to Coinbase or Robinhood. In reality, BitGo's revenue base is predominantly institutional infrastructure: custody subscriptions, settlement fees, stablecoin management fees, and enterprise API integrations. These revenue streams are more recurring, less volatile, and less price-sensitive than retail trading. The gross-to-net revenue transition has exacerbated this confusion: the $16.15B FY2025 gross revenue figure suggests BitGo is a massive trading operation, when in reality the economic revenue (net) is ~$223M, predominantly from infrastructure services. This misclassification leads to: (a) incorrect comps (comparing BTGO to retail crypto platforms rather than infrastructure/custody businesses), (b) overestimating crypto price sensitivity by ignoring the S&S and StaaS components, and (c) failing to credit the recurring nature of custody and subscription revenue.

Client Stickiness and Multi-Product Attach Rates. The AlphaSense research emphasizes BitGo's cross-sell data as the most important leading indicator of business health. As of Q4 2025: 70% of revenue-generating clients use 2+ BitGo products, 50% use 3+, and attach rates are growing quarter-over-quarter. This multi-product penetration creates compounding revenue per client (average quarterly custody client spend: $11.1K) and dramatically increases switching costs (unwinding custody is hard; unwinding custody + trading + staking + settlement + StaaS simultaneously is nearly impossible). Expert interviewees describe this as the "BitGo flywheel": custody is the wedge product (clients must choose a custodian first), which then enables cross-sell into trading, staking, settlement, and stablecoins. The lending book ($207.4M, +114% YoY) is the latest product gaining attach rate momentum. If the flywheel continues to compound, BitGo's revenue per client should increase 3-5x over the next 3 years without adding a single new logo.
