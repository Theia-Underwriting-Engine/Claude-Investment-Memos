Toast, Inc. | Preliminary Memo for Theia
1. Business Overview

Toast, Inc. (NYSE: TOST) is the leading vertical software platform for the restaurant industry, serving 164,000 restaurant locations across the United States and select international markets as of year-end 2025. Founded in 2011 by Aman Narang (CEO), Jonathan Grimm, and Steve Fredette (Co-President), Toast was built on a single insight: the restaurant industry — a $1 trillion economy employing 12 million workers — ran on fragmented, generic POS systems that failed to address the operational complexity of food service. Toast's answer was a purpose-built, cloud-native, hardware-integrated platform that replaced 5–8 separate vendor relationships (POS, payroll, scheduling, online ordering, marketing, inventory, payroll loans) with a single OS for the restaurant.

The revenue model has three components. Financial Technology Solutions (82% of revenue, $5.0B in 2025A) is effectively a payment processing business: Toast earns 48 basis points on every dollar of Gross Payment Volume processed through its platform, plus 10 bps from non-payment fintech products (Toast Capital lending, Toast Payroll, Toast Pay). Subscription Services (15% of revenue, $936M) are pure SaaS ARR — recurring monthly fees for platform modules including point-of-sale, kitchen display systems, online ordering, scheduling, and the newly launched ToastIQ AI suite. Hardware & Professional Services (3% of revenue, $180M) covers the sale of Toast Go handhelds, kiosks, and kitchen equipment; this segment carries negative gross margins and is treated as a customer acquisition mechanism rather than a profit center.

The scale and unit economics are exceptional. Toast processes $195 billion of Gross Payment Volume annually across 164,000 locations — a 22% GPV growth rate in 2025. The SaaS net retention rate is 109%, meaning existing customers expand their spend at Toast by nearly 10% per year through module adoption — without a single new restaurant sale. Adjusted EBITDA reached $633 million in 2025 (34% of recurring gross profit streams), with free cash flow of $608 million, reflecting a business that has structurally crossed the profitability threshold. The company now powers approximately 20% of US SMB and mid-market restaurants, having nearly doubled market share in three years, and has articulated a clear path from its current $2.0 billion ARR to $5–10 billion through four growth vectors: deeper US penetration, enterprise QSR chains, retail expansion, and international markets (Canada, UK, Ireland, Australia).

3. Bull & Bear Cases
Bull Case — 5 Catalysts

Core Market Share Doubles Again. Toast reached 20% of US SMB/mid-market restaurants after nearly doubling share in three years. The remaining 80% of the market — roughly 650,000 locations still on legacy systems like NCR Aloha, Revel, or manual cash — represents a $15B+ annual revenue opportunity at current ARPU. The unit economics are exceptional: mid-teen month payback periods on a subscription product that retains at 109% NRR. If Toast achieves 35–40% US market penetration by 2028E, total ARR could approach $5B ahead of management's own long-term guidance, driven purely by the core US business without any contribution from new markets.

ToastIQ Monetization Creates a New ARR Stream. ToastIQ — Toast's conversational AI assistant — was used by more than 50% of locations within four months of launch, with over 1 million queries sent collectively. The product currently is included in base subscriptions, but the depth of adoption (weekly active use by tens of thousands of locations for demand forecasting, email campaign generation, and menu optimization) creates the foundation for a premium tier. A ToastIQ Pro subscription at $200–400/month across 50% of the location base would generate $500M–$1B+ of high-margin incremental ARR — roughly 25–50% of the current total SaaS ARR — at near-zero marginal cost. This optionality is completely absent from consensus models.

Enterprise + Retail Scale Faster Than Expected. Applebee's, Firehouse Subs, Papa Murphy's (1,000+ units), and MTY Group demonstrate that enterprise QSR chains are ready to migrate off legacy systems. Each major chain commitment represents 500–2,000+ simultaneous location deployments — years of net adds in a single contract. Separately, the retail vertical signed La Carniceria (25-location butcher chain) and launched an Instacart inventory sync partnership, suggesting Toast's platform is restaurant-agnostic and can serve the broader food retail market. If enterprise contributes 10,000 locations and retail 5,000 locations by 2028E (vs. near-zero today), the total location count could reach 300,000+ — 30% above the consensus ~266K estimate.

International Replicates US Economics. Australia launched as Toast's fourth international market in 2025 with "strong activity." Canada, UK, and Ireland are active markets. If international collectively reaches 15,000–20,000 locations by 2028E at US-equivalent ARPU, this contributes $500–750M of additional revenue against minimal incremental corporate overhead — directly accretive to margins. The restaurant industry is structurally identical across English-speaking markets, and Toast's hardware + software + payments bundled model is even more differentiated globally where no vertically integrated competitor exists at scale.

Margin Expansion Exceeds Guidance. Management targets 40%+ adj EBITDA margins on recurring gross profit "over the medium term" and has already hit this in the core portfolio ahead of schedule. In 2025A, SBC fell from 17% to 12% of recurring gross profit (a 500bps improvement in one year). As SBC continues declining toward 8–9%, D&A stays flat, and operating leverage accrues on the $250M+ annual technology investment base, adj EBITDA margins could reach 38–42% of recurring gross profit by 2028E — versus ~34% today — generating $400–600M of incremental earnings above the consensus model.

Bear Case — 5 Risks

Competitive Price War Stalls Location Adds. Block (Square for Restaurants) and Clover (Fiserv) launch aggressive 0% hardware subsidy + reduced processing fee campaigns in 2026, directly targeting Toast's installed base and pipeline in mid-market SMBs. SpotOn and Toast's numerous smaller competitors follow. Location net adds slow from 30K to 12–15K per year, ARPU growth stalls as Toast is forced to match pricing, and SaaS NRR erodes from 109% toward parity. The market re-rates Toast from a high-growth SaaS business (35–40x NTM P/E) to a low-growth payments processor (12–15x), compressing the stock by 60–70% from current levels regardless of near-term earnings beats.

AI Commoditization Undermines ToastIQ Differentiation. OpenAI, Google, and Microsoft release food-service-specific AI models in H2 2026 that enterprise QSR chains can integrate directly into existing POS systems via APIs — without switching to Toast. The informational moat from $195B of annual transaction data proves more temporary than expected: hyperscalers with access to broader commerce data replicate ToastIQ's demand forecasting and menu engineering capabilities within 12–18 months. Enterprise prospects deprioritize platform switching; ToastIQ's role shifts from a switching cost amplifier to a me-too feature that doesn't justify Toast's pricing premium over Clover or Square.

Hardware Cost Headwind Persists into 2027. Toast guided a ~150bps headwind to recurring gross profit growth from memory chip cost inflation in 2026, weighted toward H2. If the chip shortage cycle doesn't resolve as expected — and hardware gross margins remain deeply negative at −15% to −20% versus Toast's typical −5% to −10% — the headwind could persist into 2027 and further compress the gross margin expansion trajectory. Combined with the competitive pressure to subsidize hardware to win new locations, Toast could find itself in a "treadmill" situation where gross margin improvement lags revenue growth, preventing the model from transitioning to the 30%+ GAAP gross margins consensus expects by 2027E.

New Markets Fail to Contribute Materially in the Model Window. Toast's "emerging TAMs" (enterprise, international, retail) doubled ARR in 2025 — but doubled from a tiny base. If enterprise rollouts at Applebee's and Papa Murphy's take 24–30 months due to franchisee resistance, POS integration complexity, and staff retraining requirements (consistent with historical QSR technology deployments), these deals generate no material revenue in the 2026–2028 model window. International faces regulatory complexity in the UK and EU VAT filing requirements. Retail remains niche. Consensus already prices 20%+ recurring GP growth; if new markets underperform, the growth rate decelerates to 14–16%, triggering a multiple de-rating.

Macro Pressure on Restaurant Industry Reduces GPV. Restaurant industry GPV is directly tied to consumer discretionary spending. A recession scenario — consumer spending down 5–8%, dining out frequency declines — would reduce Toast's $195B+ GPV base, cutting fintech revenue even if location count holds steady. Unlike a pure SaaS business where revenue is contractually fixed, 82% of Toast's revenue is transaction-based and directly correlated to restaurant traffic. A 10% GPV decline would eliminate $500M+ of fintech revenue, compressing gross profit by ~$120M and adj EBITDA by a similar amount — a 15–19% EBITDA reduction with no corresponding cost offset.

4. Helmer's 7 Powers
1. Scale EconomiesStrong

Toast's R&D investment (~$700M annually) is fixed and spread across 164,000 locations. Each additional restaurant added to the platform receives ToastIQ, online ordering, kitchen display integration, and all future features at zero marginal cost. Revenue/employee reached $947K in 2025A, up from $703K two years prior — scale economies are compounding. The hardware manufacturing cost per unit also declines with volume; the memory chip headwind in 2026 is cyclical, not structural. As Toast crosses 200,000+ locations, the incremental cost to serve each new location approaches zero on the software side, driving the adj EBITDA margin expansion pathway to 40%+.

2. Network EffectsModerate

Toast has limited classic two-sided network effects (unlike a marketplace), but exhibits meaningful data network effects: every additional restaurant location adds transactional data to the ToastIQ training set, improving demand forecasting, menu optimization, and inventory management for all restaurants on the platform. The aggregate 1M+ ToastIQ queries in four months of launch means the model improves rapidly. Within enterprise chains (Applebee's, Papa Murphy's), there are direct network effects: shared menu templates, centralized reporting, and cross-location analytics create lock-in at the chain level, not just the location level. This is a structural advantage over single-location competitors.

3. Counter-PositioningStrong

Toast's fully integrated hardware + software + payments + ToastIQ model requires any competitor to replicate the entire stack simultaneously. Square (Block) is a horizontal payments company that added restaurant features; Lightspeed is a generalist retail/hospitality POS without deep payments integration; NCR Aloha is a legacy system with no cloud architecture. Clover (Fiserv) is the closest analog but lacks the depth of restaurant-specific features (kitchen display systems, drive-thru products, restaurant-grade handhelds like Toast Go 3). Replicating Toast's 14-year head start in restaurant-specific R&D — including features like station-level heat maps, daypart optimization, and AI-driven demand forecasting — would take 5–8 years and hundreds of millions in R&D spend for any horizontal competitor.

4. Switching CostsStrong

This is Toast's most durable competitive advantage. POS replacement requires: replacing physical hardware across all stations, retraining all staff on new workflows, migrating years of menu data and customer profiles, integrating new systems with delivery aggregators (DoorDash, Uber Eats), and absorbing 2–3 weeks of operational disruption during the transition. Toast's 109% SaaS NRR is direct evidence: restaurants not only stay (near-100% gross retention) but spend more over time. The mid-teen month payback period means once a restaurant has amortized its installation investment, the switching cost analysis becomes almost entirely one-sided — the only trigger for churn is a catastrophically better product, not marginal price differences.

5. BrandingModerate

"Powered by Toast" creates modest consumer awareness in restaurants, but the brand power is concentrated on the operator side. Among restaurant owners and operators, Toast's NPS scores are among the highest in the industry, and the company's culture of restaurant empathy — exemplified by 500+ new features in 2025 and AI tools designed specifically for operational challenges — generates genuine enthusiasm. The brand has expanded to enterprise clients (Applebee's, Papa Murphy's, MTY Group) which would have been implausible for a "startup POS company" five years ago, suggesting the brand is transitioning from SMB darling to enterprise-credible platform.

6. Cornered ResourceModerate

Toast's proprietary restaurant transaction data — $195B+ in annual GPV, covering millions of dining visits across 164,000 locations — is a genuinely unique dataset for training food-service AI. No competitor has comparable volume, granularity, or time depth (10+ years of data). The founding team's deep restaurant expertise (Aman Narang and Stephen Fredette spent years shadowing restaurant operators before writing the first line of code) is a cornered resource in the talent market. The company's hardware roadmap — Toast Go 3, forthcoming drive-thru product — also benefits from engineering relationships with restaurant-grade component suppliers that horizontal POS vendors haven't cultivated.

7. Process PowerStrong

Toast has built institutional processes for restaurant-specific product development that are structurally difficult to replicate. The company's "restaurant success" methodology — embedding engineers and product managers into restaurant kitchens and front-of-house operations for extended periods — generates product insights that no generalist POS company can match. The result is 500+ new features shipped in 2025 alone, all restaurant-specific: station-level throughput dashboards, AI-driven mise en place forecasting, kitchen display timing algorithms. The Toast Go 3 handheld device was designed around the specific ergonomics of tableside ordering and payment. This deep process knowledge creates compounding advantages in product velocity that are visible in the widening feature gap versus Square for Restaurants and Clover.

5. Porter's 5 Forces
Threat of New EntrantsLow

Entering the restaurant POS market at scale requires: hardware manufacturing (or OEM relationships), payment processing licenses and banking partnerships, restaurant-grade software with deep operational integrations (kitchen display, delivery aggregator APIs, inventory, scheduling), and a nationwide support infrastructure for 24/7 restaurant operations that can't tolerate downtime during dinner service. Toast spent 14 years and several hundred million dollars in R&D building this. AI-native restaurant startup POS companies (Omnivore, Presto Automation) have targeted specific features but lack the end-to-end platform. The regulatory requirements for payment processing (PCI-DSS, state money transmission licenses) add additional friction for pure software startups.

Bargaining Power of BuyersModerate

Individual SMB restaurants (the typical Toast customer) have essentially no pricing leverage — they are price-takers in a market dominated by a few integrated POS providers. Large enterprise QSR chains (Applebee's, Dine Brands, Papa Murphy's) have meaningful negotiating leverage and will likely secure pricing concessions, custom SLAs, and longer contract terms in exchange for committing hundreds or thousands of locations. The key risk is that as Toast pursues enterprise, deal economics per location may compress even as total ARR grows. The 109% net retention for the SMB base confirms that buyer power in the core market is structurally low.

Bargaining Power of SuppliersLow

Cloud infrastructure (AWS, Google Cloud) is commoditized and represents a small fraction of Toast's cost structure. The 2026 hardware headwind from memory chip costs demonstrates that hardware component suppliers have temporary pricing power during supply chain disruptions — but this is a cyclical risk, not structural. Toast's payment card network relationships (Visa, Mastercard) are standard issuer/acquirer agreements with no proprietary terms that disadvantage Toast versus peers. The company's software supply chain (internal development) is self-controlled.

Threat of SubstitutesLow

The realistic substitutes for Toast in 2026 are: (1) Square for Restaurants, (2) Clover (Fiserv), (3) Lightspeed, (4) legacy enterprise systems (Oracle MICROS, NCR Aloha), and (5) cash-only manual operations. None offer the integrated hardware + software + payments + ToastIQ suite at comparable price points. The emergence of AI-native POS competitors is a 3–5 year threat, not an immediate substitute. For enterprise chains on Oracle MICROS (with 15+ year-old systems), Toast is actually the modernization path — not a substitute threat, but a destination.

Industry RivalryModerate-High

The restaurant POS market is genuinely competitive. Square for Restaurants, Clover, Lightspeed, Revel, SpotOn, and TouchBistro all compete in SMB. Oracle MICROS and NCR Aloha dominate enterprise. International markets have local champions (Kounta in Australia, iZettle in Europe). Despite this rivalry, Toast has grown from 8% to 20% US market share in three years — demonstrating that its integrated platform creates sufficient differentiation to win. The risk is that as Toast expands upmarket into enterprise and international, it encounters entrenched incumbents with 20+ year customer relationships that are structurally resistant to displacement regardless of product quality.

6. What Drives the Stock?

Near-Term (6–12 months): (1) Q1 2026 net location adds and recurring gross profit growth vs. the 22–24% guidance — management used unusually specific language ("record" net adds for FY2026) which sets a high bar for the May 7 earnings. A miss on location adds or guidance reduction would be severely penalized at 38x GAAP P/E. (2) Hardware gross margin recovery — the memory chip cost headwind is guided as H2-weighted; any commentary suggesting it's resolving ahead of schedule at Q1 would be a material positive. (3) ToastIQ monetization signal — any announcement of a paid premium AI tier or ToastIQ Pro SKU would represent a significant consensus EPS upgrade cycle beginning. (4) Enterprise contract announcements — Dine Brands (Applebee's) deployment velocity is the near-term enterprise proof point that will define whether enterprise is a real growth vector or a checkbox for investors.

Long-Term (2–3 years): (1) Path from $2B ARR to $5B ARR visibility — the market will re-rate Toast's multiple when the trajectory toward $5B ARR becomes visible in reported metrics, likely by late 2027E when new market contributions become material. (2) International unit economics disclosure — Australia reaching US-equivalent payback periods of mid-teen months would confirm that the international playbook is exportable and opens a multi-billion dollar TAM expansion. (3) Margin structure confirmation — whether Toast reaches 40%+ adj EBITDA margins on recurring gross profit drives $1–2B of incremental NPV at current multiples. (4) Competitive response assessment — the trajectory of Square for Restaurants and Clover market share in the 2026–2027 earnings calls will determine whether Toast's moat is widening or narrowing.

7. What Drives the Business?
Primary Driver 1: Net Restaurant Locations

Location count is the compounding base of the business. Each net add brings immediate SaaS ARR (average ~$5,700/location/year) plus fintech revenue tied to GPV ($195B ÷ 164K locations = ~$1.2M GPV/location × 58bps fintech take rate = ~$6,900 annual fintech GP per location). Toast added 30,000 net locations in 2025 (a record) and has guided for another record in 2026. The addressable universe is ~800,000 US restaurant locations, of which Toast serves 20% — leaving 640,000 locations as organic opportunity without requiring any international expansion or vertical diversification.

Primary Driver 2: ARPU per Location

Average revenue per location reached ~$37,500 in 2025A (total revenue ÷ average locations). This is expected to expand through: (a) SaaS module attach rate increasing (online ordering, scheduling, Toast Capital penetration within the installed base), (b) GPV per location growing as dining volumes recover and locations grow, (c) fintech take rate stability at 58bps as non-payment fintech products (lending, payroll, insurance) penetrate deeper. The SaaS 109% NRR is direct evidence that ARPU per location is structurally expanding — the average existing customer spends ~9% more each year through upsell without any price increase.

Primary Driver 3: Gross Payment Volume (GPV) Growth

$195B of GPV in 2025 (+22% YoY) generated the majority of Toast's fintech gross profit. GPV grows as a function of: (1) location count growth × (2) average ticket size × (3) dining visit frequency. The fintech take rate — 58bps in Q4 2025, up 2bps YoY — reflects increasing non-payment fintech product penetration and is structurally stable. GPV growth tracks GDP and consumer discretionary spending trends in addition to Toast's own location growth, creating a dual-engine structure. A 10% GPV decline in a recession would be the single most significant negative to consensus estimates.

Secondary Driver: New Market Penetration (Enterprise, Retail, International)

Management doubled new market ARR in 2025 from a small base. The enterprise pipeline is at "all-time highs" with major QSR chains (Applebee's, Papa Murphy's) signed. Retail signed its first specialty butcher chain with Instacart integration. Australia launched as the fourth international market. These are currently subscale contributors but represent the key long-term optionality that justifies valuing Toast at a premium to pure-play payments processors. Enterprise alone — if Toast converts 5% of the 350,000+ chain restaurant locations in the US — would add 17,500 locations and ~$650M of incremental ARR, without any SMB penetration gains.

The ToastIQ Moat: Data Flywheel or Feature Parity Risk? The most significant debate in the AlphaSense corpus is whether Toast's AI advantage — built on $195B+ of annual GPV transaction data across 164,000 restaurants — is a durable competitive moat or a 12–24 month lead that hyperscalers can close. The bull case is compelling: 10+ years of time-stamped, location-specific transaction data (daypart by daypart, menu item by menu item, weather-correlated) creates a training dataset that no competitor — not Square, not Clover, not any AI startup — can replicate without operating a restaurant POS at Toast's scale for a decade. The demand forecasting and menu optimization applications already showing adoption (50%+ of locations using ToastIQ weekly within four months) suggest the model has crossed the "good enough to be useful" threshold rapidly. Expert calls from former restaurant technology operators note that the specific challenge of predicting restaurant demand (high frequency, weather-dependent, menu-item-level granularity, prep-time constraints) is fundamentally different from general commerce demand forecasting — and that general LLMs are materially inferior to a specialized model trained on dense restaurant operational data. The bear argument — that OpenAI or Google can fine-tune a general model on restaurant transaction data and offer it via API without a POS relationship — underestimates the data density advantage. The AlphaSense corpus suggests this moat is real and wider than consensus pricing implies.

Enterprise Execution: The Deployment Gap Between Signing and Revenue. Multiple expert calls from former QSR technology executives emphasize the gap between "signing a chain" and "generating revenue from that chain." Enterprise POS deployments at major QSR chains (1,000+ locations) historically take 18–36 months from contract signing to full rollout, due to franchise owner buy-in requirements, simultaneous kitchen display replacement logistics, staff training programs, and menu data migration from legacy systems. A former SVP of Technology at a major QSR chain (Expert Call, January 2026) estimated that even with strong corporate mandate and vendor support, "a 1,500-unit chain deployment takes 3 years minimum if you're doing it right — you can't push franchisees faster than they can absorb it." This timing risk means Applebee's, Papa Murphy's, and MTY Group may not generate material revenue contributions in the 2026–2027 model window. The AlphaSense corpus consistently identifies enterprise deployment velocity — not signing velocity — as the critical metric investors should track at each earnings call. Management's Q4 disclosure of "enterprise pipeline and rollouts at all-time highs" conflates the two; investors should probe the rollout metric specifically at May 7 earnings.

International Unit Economics: Australia as the Proof Point. The AlphaSense report highlights that Australia's restaurant market structure is particularly favorable for Toast: English-language operations, high card penetration (90%+), high dining frequency, and fragmented legacy POS incumbents (Lightspeed, Impos, H&L) with poor mobile/cloud capabilities. Former restaurant technology consultants in Australia (Expert Call, February 2026) describe Toast's Australia entry as "the strongest initial traction of any international POS entrant in ten years," citing the integrated hardware-software-payments bundle as uniquely competitive in a market where restaurants currently manage 3–4 separate vendors. The critical question is whether Australia unit economics — payback periods, ARPU, and churn rates — are being tracked internally against the US benchmarks. Management disclosed that "emerging TAMs achieved mid-teen month payback periods with a path to under 20 months" — but this is the aggregate of all new markets, which includes higher-churn early-stage markets. If Australia specifically is achieving 18–22 month paybacks (vs. the US's mid-teen months), it is on a credible path to US-equivalent economics within 2 years of launch, which would validate the international expansion thesis entirely. The AlphaSense corpus does not contain direct Australian unit economic disclosures beyond management's aggregate guidance, making this a key information gap that Theia would need to close through primary research.

Competitive Dynamics: Square's Toast Response and What It Reveals. The AlphaSense corpus contains a notable insight from expert calls with former Square for Restaurants engineers (Expert Call, November 2025): Block has internally acknowledged that Toast's kitchen display system (KDS) and table management integrations are "3–4 years ahead" of Square's restaurant capabilities, and that matching them would require a dedicated restaurant engineering team of 200+ people building exclusively on restaurant features for 3+ years. Block has not made this investment because restaurant vertical SaaS is not core to its broader financial services strategy — Block is fundamentally a payments company that also sells POS software, while Toast is fundamentally a restaurant operating system that also processes payments. This strategic asymmetry — which is precisely Helmer's Counter-Positioning power — means competitive pressure from Square is likely to remain primarily on pricing (hardware subsidies, processing fee discounts) rather than on product capability parity. Clover (Fiserv) faces the same issue: Fiserv's acquisition of Clover gave it distribution but not deep restaurant product expertise. The AlphaSense corpus suggests that Toast's competitive moat is wider and more durable than the generic "large competitors with more resources" bear argument implies.

Key Long-Term Investor Questions Not Adequately Addressed by Consensus. Three questions from the AlphaSense corpus that sell-side models do not adequately address: (1) Does the Class A/B dual-share structure create governance risk at scale? Founders retain voting control, and any large acquisition (say, a $3–5B deal) would face limited minority shareholder recourse. The risk of a value-destructive enterprise software acquisition (to accelerate retail or international) is not in any sell-side model. (2) What happens to ARPU when GPV per location plateaus? At $195B GPV / 164K locations = $1.2M GPV per location annually, Toast is already at the structural ceiling for a typical SMB restaurant (doing ~3,300 covers/year at $36 average check). Future fintech ARPU growth must come from take-rate expansion (products like Toast Capital, Toast Payroll) or new fintech products — not GPV growth. The trajectory of non-payment fintech revenue (currently $51M quarterly, 10bps take rate) is the most underappreciated growth lever in the model. (3) Is there a disruptive AI-native POS threat on the horizon? Presto Automation has deployed AI-driven drive-thru order-taking at major QSR chains with 95%+ accuracy. If an AI-native POS company builds a full restaurant OS from scratch using modern LLM architectures — without the legacy hardware dependencies and training-based implementations that Toast manages — the switching cost moat could be disrupted within 5–7 years. This is a long-term risk that consensus models do not price, though it is a genuine structural concern raised in multiple AlphaSense expert calls from restaurant AI engineers.
