Miami International Holdings | Preliminary Memo for Theia
1. Business Overview

Miami International Holdings, Inc. (NYSE: MIAX) is a technology-driven operator of regulated financial marketplaces across multiple asset classes and geographies. Founded in 2007 by a team with extensive trading and technology experience, MIAX launched its first options exchange in December 2012 with a mission to build a differentiated exchange model focused on customer-centricity, competitive pricing, and outstanding technology. The company has grown from a single options exchange into the 13th largest global derivatives exchange operator following a successful $397 million IPO in August 2025.

MIAX operates four distinct business segments: Options (81.2% of FY2025 revenue), which encompasses four U.S. national securities exchanges (MIAX Options, MIAX Pearl, MIAX Emerald, MIAX Sapphire) that achieved a record 17.1% annual market share of the U.S. multi-listed options market in 2025; Equities (11.3% of revenue), operating MIAX Pearl Equities for cash equity trading; Futures (6.4% of revenue), anchored by MIAX Futures (formerly Minneapolis Grain Exchange) with an exclusive 10-year Bloomberg licensing agreement for equity index derivatives launching Q2 2026; and International (1.1% of revenue), including the Bermuda Stock Exchange and the newly acquired International Stock Exchange (TISE) in Guernsey. The company's proprietary, in-house-built technology platform featuring deterministic architecture and 99.9998% operational uptime since 2012 serves as its primary competitive moat, enabling sub-200 microsecond latency that outpaces Cboe (250-400μs) and NYSE (400-700μs).

3. Bull & Bear Cases
Bull Cases

Bloomberg Index Futures Launch. MIAX secured a 10-year exclusive licensing agreement with Bloomberg to launch equity index futures (B500, B100Q) and "Tini" retail-friendly contracts in Q2 2026. These proprietary products will clear at OCC, providing margin efficiencies. If successful, this represents a strategic shift from commoditized multi-listed products to high-margin proprietary derivatives — the holy grail of exchange economics.

Market Share Momentum Continues. MIAX Options achieved a record 18.2% market share in Q4 2025 (up from 15.1% in 2024). The company's sub-200 microsecond latency advantage continues to attract HFT market makers. If MIAX can push through the 20% threshold by expanding its institutional investor base, the operating leverage would be substantial.

Fortress Balance Sheet. Post-IPO, MIAX ended 2025 with $434M cash and virtually no debt ($1.5M). This provides firepower for: (a) accretive M&A, (b) share buybacks at attractive valuations, (c) funding Bloomberg index futures launch without dilution. The clean capital structure supports management credibility.

Elevated Volatility Environment. Management expects elevated volatility to persist through 2026 driven by domestic policy shifts, tariff impacts, and AI investment cycle uncertainty. Volatility is a direct catalyst for options volume — more hedging, more speculation, more contract volume. MIAX's infrastructure scales with volume at high incremental margins.

Operating Leverage Inflection. Adj. EBITDA margins expanded from 30% in FY2024 to 46% in FY2025, reaching 50% by Q4. With major infrastructure investments (MIAX Sapphire floor, Onyx Futures platform) completed in 2025, management guides for moderating expense growth relative to top-line expansion. Incremental margins could exceed 60-70%.

Bear Cases

Market Share Ceiling at 20%. Industry experts warn MIAX's options market share may hit a structural ceiling near 20%. Breaking through requires attracting institutional asset managers who fear adverse selection on HFT-dominated venues. Large pension funds and foreign institutions face strict compliance hurdles when onboarding new exchanges.

Equity Rights Program Overhang. MIAX's early success was heavily subsidized by Equity Rights Programs that granted market makers warrants for meeting volume thresholds. Skeptics question whether market share can persist purely on technological merit now that warrant programs have matured. Post-IPO secondary offerings by strategic members could signal future selling pressure.

Severe Customer Concentration. Top three customers account for 15%, 14%, and 12% of revenues respectively. MIAX's growth strategy relies heavily on a small group of elite electronic market makers and HFT firms. Loss of a major liquidity provider would have outsized P&L impact and could trigger adverse selection spirals.

Lack of Proprietary Products. Unlike Cboe (SPX, VIX) or CME (E-Mini), MIAX currently has no exclusive proprietary products with pricing power. Multi-listed options are a commoditized, zero-sum game requiring constant fee adjustments. The Bloomberg futures launch is unproven and competes against dominant incumbents.

Regulatory Overhang. SEC maintains strict oversight preventing exclusive pricing deals with liquidity providers. Ongoing scrutiny of payment for order flow, connectivity fees, and market data sales threatens high-margin revenue streams. MIAXdx crypto subsidiary faces CFTC inquiries and heightened AML/KYC compliance burdens.

4. Helmer's 7 Powers

1. Scale Economies Strong
MIAX's exchange infrastructure has enormous fixed costs (technology platform, regulatory compliance, data center operations) and near-zero marginal cost per additional contract traded. This creates massive operating leverage: adj. EBITDA margins expanded from 30% to 50% as volume scaled. Once the technology stack is built, each incremental contract falls almost entirely to gross profit. The scale moat is substantial — new entrants face $100M+ initial investment just to meet minimum viable product requirements for an options exchange.

2. Network Effects Moderate
Exchange network effects operate through liquidity: more market makers create tighter spreads, which attracts more order flow, which attracts more market makers. MIAX has successfully bootstrapped this flywheel using its technology advantage and Equity Rights Programs. However, multi-listed options can trade on any of 16 exchanges, limiting the direct network effect compared to single-venue products. The network effect is real but interruptible — a competitor with superior latency or pricing could gradually erode it.

3. Counter-Positioning Strong
MIAX's core counter-positioning is that legacy exchanges (Cboe, NYSE, Nasdaq) cannot replicate its sub-200 microsecond latency without overhauling their legacy infrastructure — a multi-year, operationally risky project that incumbent leadership has been reluctant to undertake. CME's failed attempt to modernize via Google Cloud partnership illustrates this constraint. MIAX built from scratch specifically for HFT requirements, while incumbents must retrofit decades-old architecture. The counter-positioning is genuine and durable.

4. Switching Costs Moderate
Market makers who have invested in co-location, connectivity, and routing logic optimized for MIAX's platform face real switching costs — both technical (re-engineering latency arbitrage strategies) and operational (compliance onboarding, new vendor relationships). However, most large HFT firms trade across all major exchanges simultaneously, limiting single-venue lock-in. Switching costs provide modest protection but don't prevent multi-homing.

5. Branding Weak
MIAX has strong recognition among electronic market makers and institutional traders, but exchange "brand" doesn't command pricing power the way consumer brands do. Orders route to wherever offers best execution, regardless of brand perception. The company has successfully positioned as the technology-forward challenger to legacy incumbents, but this is more functional positioning than brand equity.

6. Cornered Resource Moderate
MIAX's most important cornered resources are: (a) SEC licenses to operate four national securities exchanges — these take years to obtain and require ongoing regulatory compliance that deters entry; (b) the proprietary technology stack built over 15+ years with deterministic architecture and 99.9998% uptime history; (c) exclusive 10-year Bloomberg licensing agreement for index derivatives. These resources are meaningful but not completely unique — other exchanges hold similar licenses and could theoretically build comparable technology.

7. Process Power Strong
MIAX has developed genuine process capabilities in high-performance exchange technology that are difficult to replicate. The engineering culture that produced sub-10 microsecond latency on the Onyx platform reflects institutional knowledge accumulated over 15 years. CEO Gallagher's leadership team has been with the company since founding, providing continuity that supports operational excellence. The customer-first approach of tailoring exchange features to HFT requirements demonstrates embedded organizational capabilities.

Summary: The strongest moats are Scale Economies, Counter-Positioning against legacy incumbents, and Process Power in exchange technology. These represent genuine structural advantages that should persist over the medium term. Network effects and switching costs provide additional but more modest protection.

5. Porter's 5 Forces

1. Threat of New Entrants Low
Barriers to entry in options exchanges are exceptionally high. Requirements include: (a) SEC approval for a national securities exchange license (multi-year process); (b) $100M+ investment in technology infrastructure; (c) clearing relationships with OCC; (d) ability to attract initial liquidity from skeptical market makers. Industry experts note that barriers are significantly higher than in equities due to the complexity of options quoting. MIAX was the last successful new entrant in 2012, and no credible new competitor has emerged since. The threat is low.

2. Bargaining Power of Suppliers Low
MIAX's primary "suppliers" are technology vendors (servers, networking equipment, data center services) and clearing infrastructure (OCC). The company builds most technology in-house, reducing dependency on external vendors. OCC is a shared industry utility with standard pricing. AI infrastructure demand poses a potential supply chain risk for hardware, but MIAX has no single-source dependencies. Overall supplier power is low.

3. Bargaining Power of Buyers High
MIAX's customers — primarily electronic market makers and HFT firms — have substantial bargaining power. The top three customers represent 41% of revenues. These sophisticated institutions can easily route order flow to any of 16 competing options exchanges based on fee structures and execution quality. MIAX must compete aggressively on pricing tiers and rebates to win flow. The reliance on a small number of elite HFT firms creates meaningful customer concentration risk.

4. Threat of Substitutes Moderate
The primary substitutes for listed options are OTC derivatives, futures contracts, and synthetic option strategies using other instruments. For most hedging and speculation purposes, listed options on regulated exchanges remain the preferred instrument due to transparency, liquidity, and regulatory acceptance. However, crypto derivatives and other alternative products represent emerging substitutes for certain use cases. The threat is moderate and evolving.

5. Competitive Rivalry High
The U.S. options exchange market is intensely competitive, with 16 exchanges operated by major incumbents (Cboe, Nasdaq, NYSE, MIAX). Competition primarily occurs through: (a) pricing — constant adjustments to maker-taker rebates and fee tiers; (b) technology — latency, uptime, determinism; (c) product features — allocation models, order types, market structure. The rivalry is zero-sum for multi-listed products: every contract MIAX wins is a contract lost by competitors. Only proprietary products (like Cboe's SPX/VIX) escape this dynamic. Overall competitive intensity is high.

Summary: The competitive environment is challenging. High buyer power and intense rivalry create persistent margin pressure on multi-listed products. However, exceptionally low threat of new entry and low supplier power provide structural protection. MIAX's path to premium economics requires successful expansion into proprietary products (Bloomberg index futures).

6. What Drives the Stock?

The following are the key variables that would move MIAX's share price materially in the next 12 months:

1. Options Market Share Trajectory. MIAX achieved 18.2% market share in Q4 2025. Every quarterly update will be scrutinized for continued momentum vs. a ceiling forming at ~20%. Share gains are bullish (operating leverage); share loss or stagnation would trigger de-rating as investors question competitive durability.

2. Bloomberg Index Futures Launch Success. The Q2 2026 launch of B500/B100Q futures is the single most important catalyst for the growth narrative. Success metrics include: (a) market maker adoption; (b) initial volume trajectory; (c) open interest buildup. A successful launch validates MIAX's ability to compete in proprietary products; failure would confirm the bear thesis that MIAX remains stuck in commoditized multi-listed options.

3. U.S. Options Industry Volume. MIAX's revenues are ~60% transaction-based and directly tied to industry-wide options volume. Elevated volatility drives options usage for hedging and speculation. VIX levels, macro uncertainty, and retail trading activity all influence total addressable volume. A volatility collapse would compress industry volumes regardless of market share.

4. Revenue Per Contract (RPC) Trends. Options RPC improved 18.7% in 2025 to $0.108 due to favorable product mix. Analysts will track RPC trajectory as an indicator of pricing power vs. competitive fee pressure. RPC compression would indicate market share gains are coming at the cost of economics.

5. Adjusted EBITDA Margin Progression. Q4 2025 margins reached 50%. Management guides for continued operating leverage as infrastructure investments mature. Margin expansion validates the scale economics thesis; margin compression would signal either competitive pricing pressure or operating cost creep.

6. Capital Allocation Announcements. With $434M cash and negligible debt, MIAX has significant deployment optionality. M&A (accretive exchange acquisitions), buybacks (at current discount to target), or dividend initiation would all be discrete catalysts. Aggressive, dilutive acquisition would be negatively received.

12. Is This a Theia Investment?

Theia seeks small cap companies that aren't well covered and are strongly aligned on core internet finance thesis with wide outcome dispersion.

(i) Market Cap Bucket: $3.6B Weak
MIAX at $3.6B market cap is solidly mid-cap, above Theia's ideal microcap (<$1B) sweet spot. The company's successful IPO and subsequent secondary offerings have expanded its public float and institutional ownership. While not outside Theia's range entirely, this is less attractive from a size perspective than typical Theia targets.

(ii) Analyst Coverage: 6 brokers Moderate
With 6 sell-side analysts covering MIAX, coverage is moderate but not thin. This is better than heavily-covered mega-caps (15+ analysts) but offers less edge than true under-coverage situations (<5 analysts). The August 2025 IPO brought new coverage, but institutional awareness is still building.

(iii) Blockchain/Internet Finance Thesis Alignment: Strong
MIAX is directly aligned with the digitization of financial services. The company represents the technology-forward disruption of legacy exchange infrastructure — precisely the internet finance thesis. Sub-200 microsecond latency, deterministic architecture, and the push toward proprietary derivatives all embody digital-native financial infrastructure. The MIAXdx crypto derivatives platform (10% stake retained after Robinhood JV) provides direct blockchain exposure. This is a core thesis fit.

(iv) Outcome Dispersion: Strong
The scenario analysis shows wide dispersion: Bear Severe at $18.90 (−52%) to Bull Severe at $91.20 (+133%). Key binary outcomes include: (a) Bloomberg futures success/failure; (b) market share ceiling at 20% or breakthrough; (c) proprietary product economics activation. This is exactly the kind of asymmetric setup where fundamental research can add value.

Verdict: Maybe — Thesis Aligned, But Size Exceeds Typical Target
