USD.AI (CHIP) | Preliminary Memo for Theia
1. Business Overview

USD.AI is an on-chain credit market that lends stablecoins against NVIDIA GPUs. Depositors mint USDai (backed by PYUSD and other stablecoins) and stake it as sUSDai, which funds senior loans to GPU neoclouds. Each loan sits in a bankruptcy-remote Delaware SPV at 70-80% LTV with ~3 months of debt-service reserve and insurance, and title to the hardware is tokenized under the CALIBER framework. GPU Finance Ltd, a CHIP Foundation subsidiary, is lender of record. USD.AI Inc. (formerly Permian Labs) originates and underwrites the loans.

The business model changed in Oct-26: from hold-to-maturity to originate, season and sell. Under V1, sUSDai funded each loan for its full 3-year life and the protocol earned the origination fee. Since GLX launched on 1-Oct-26, sUSDai works as a warehouse. Loans are funded, seasoned for a few months, then sold to institutional buyers and the cash relent; large loans now bring in co-lenders at origination (Silver Point and Victory Park on the $106M NexGen loan). GLX handled more than $200M in its first month. Growth is now limited by loan buyers rather than deposits, which USD.AI says hit the utilization cap against more than $25B of inbound demand. The origination economics moved off-chain with it, to USD.AI Inc. Next on the roadmap: asset-backed securities, then profit-share products (Conor Moore, 18-Nov-26).

Four loans ($387M) have been placed through GLX so far. USD.AI says it has originated $531M across 20 deals (12-Nov-26). Live today: 16 active loans ($177M) at an 11.5% weighted APR, plus a $128.9M GB200 loan for a listed GPU cloud in escrow, for $305M deployed against a $463M asset base.

The governance token, CHIP, has a narrow cash claim. The protocol keeps 10% of sUSDai yield, about $3.5M a year today. On 28-Oct-26 the Foundation said it would route 100% of that NIM, net of its own costs, to CHIP purchases. On 18-Nov-26 it put this to governance (CRFC #47): from 1-Jan-27, 100% of sUSDai NIM net of Foundation opex buys CHIP on-chain and programmatically, at least quarterly (target monthly), with the CHIP held by the protocol rather than burned; changing it needs a new proposal. This memo models the CRFC as passed. The two October CRFCs each passed with three voters, so passage is likely, but the economics barely move: Foundation opex comes out first and absorbs most of today's ~$3.5M, so the routing adds ~$0.0001 to the Theia EV. What matters is NIM growth and the opex line, not the vote. Origination fees (2-3%) and 60% of gain on sale are earned off-chain by USD.AI Inc.; the other 40% of gain on sale funds a Yield Reserve that backs a 7% CHIP Hurdle Rate for sUSDai.

3. Bull & Bear Cases
Bull
1. The product works. Loans went from ~$1M to $305M in a year at an 11.5% weighted APR with no defaults reported. Borrowers include an investment-grade, listed GPU cloud.
2. The pipeline is larger than the book. $220.6M of executed term sheets at 11.5-15% and $526M committed; projected sUSDai APY is 11.6% once deployed.
3. GLX can scale origination without scaling deposits. Selling seasoned loans at par or better recycles sUSDai like a warehouse line, so origination volume can run several times AUM.
4. Value accrual is switching on. The NIM is pointed at CHIP and USD.AI Inc. has started discretionary buys ($1M on ~27-Oct). The USDai NIM is a large dormant lever at scale.
Bear
1. CHIP gets 10% of the yield. ~$3.5M of protocol revenue against a $287M FDV (81x, vs Maple 7.6x). The origination economics sit in a private company.
2. Deposits are leaving. sUSDai fell 21% in three weeks ($558M → $442M) and stablecoin reserves fell to $157M. 30-day redemptions are funded from a thin buffer.
3. A subsidy ends in January. PayPal's PYUSD incentive is 1.6pp of the 8.01% gross yield; realized APY (6.8%) is already below the 7% hurdle.
4. Concentrated, correlated collateral. One $128.9M loan is 42% of deployed capital; GPUs depreciate fast and together. The anchor facility's borrower (QumulusAI) flagged going-concern doubt.
4. Helmer's 7 Powers
Counter-PositioningModerate
Banks and private credit can lend against GPUs, but not with permissionless stablecoin deposits, tokenized title and on-chain secondary sales. Incumbents copying it would have to rebuild their funding stack. Silver Point and Victory Park co-lending on the NexGen Cloud loan shows they will partner rather than compete for now.
Cornered ResourceModerate
CALIBER (GPU title as ERC-721 under UCC Article 7) and the GPU underwriting data behind "Compute IQ" scores are proprietary, but legal structures can be replicated.
5. Porter's 5 Forces
Bargaining Power of Suppliers (depositors)High
Capital is the input, and depositors move for 50bps. $118M left on 1-4 Nov. sUSDai must out-yield T-bill products after the PYUSD subsidy ends.
RivalryHigh
GPU-backed lending is a crowded private-credit trade (Blackstone, Magnetar, Silver Point, bank warehouses). Spreads compress as capital floods AI infrastructure.
Bargaining Power of Buyers (borrowers)Moderate
Investment-grade borrowers negotiate down to 9% (the $128.9M loan); smaller neoclouds pay 12-15% but carry more risk.
Threat of SubstitutesModerate
For depositors: Maple, Ethena and T-bill tokens. For borrowers: vendor financing and leasing.
Threat of New EntrantsModerate
On-chain GPU credit needs legal structure, underwriting and deposits; several crypto credit protocols could add it.
6. What Drives the Token?
  • Proof that CHIP purchases are happening: the first published programmatic buy amount.
  • Deposit trend: sUSDai back above $500M, or a further run toward the 90% utilization cap.
  • Supply: the 16-Apr-27 cliff (~2.76B CHIP, more than today's float) will dominate price action as it approaches.
  • Credit events: any markdown, liquidation or below-par GLX liquidity sale.
7. What Drives the Business?

The market. USD.AI lends into the compute middle market: $5-250M GPU clusters outside the hyperscalers and the top neoclouds. Theia sizes debt origination in this segment as AI hardware capex × middle-market share × debt-financed share. That gives $23B in FY2026E and $77B in FY2029E (Exhibit 7.1). The model itself keeps it simpler: its market is total AI hardware capex ($525B in FY2026E, $1.0T in FY2029E in the base case), and USD.AI's originations are a share of that spend (0.11% today, 0.32% in the base case by FY2029E after a three-year ramp). This build is the cross-check on that share. The observed floor is USD.AI's own gpuloans.com tracker of public middle-market GPU financings (≤$500M): $0.20B in 2023, $0.51B in 2024, $0.77B in 2025 and $3.79B in 2026 to date (34 deals). That is $5.27B across 44 deals since 2023, from 20 lenders, with USD.AI the most active (20 deals, $531M). The model's base implies 5.3x more private and unannounced debt than the public tally in 2026, down from 11.7x in 2025. USD.AI's own framing is broader: "$2T outside the hyperscalers by 2030" and $25B of loan demand seen across ~700 deals.

Who the borrowers are. A neocloud rents out GPUs for AI, usually cheaper and simpler than AWS or Azure. USD.AI's natural borrower is a neocloud with contracted take-or-pay offtake: a customer that pays for the capacity whether or not it uses it (NexGen's $106M loan is backed by take-or-pay covering the full capacity). The loan is repaid from a known stream of customer payments over a term matched to the contract, like a solar farm financed against a power purchase agreement, so the lender underwrites the contract and the hardware rather than the neocloud's own credit. Neoclouds are growing fast: >$25B of revenue in 2025 (Synergy; Q4 $9B, +223%), $35B in 2026E rising to $237B by 2031E (Mordor), ~190 operators worldwide (JLL). But revenue is dominated by giants: CoreWeave alone guides $12.4-13.2B of 2026 revenue on $35-39B of capex. Those giants borrow billions from banks and private credit and don't need USD.AI. USD.AI's market is the long tail: $10-300M deals by smaller operators, plus enterprises buying their own clusters. No credible independent estimate of that segment exists. USD.AI's own demand signal is 700+ requests worth $26.5B since Aug-25 against $531M originated (~2% converted). Theia's base market is a build, and the "$2T compute credit gap" should be read as marketing until someone independent sizes it.

The loan book so far. USD.AI has originated $531M across 20 financings since Jul-25 (escrow-entry basis, ties to its own figure). The monthly pace rose from ~$1M in Jan-26 to ~$100M in Sep-Oct, but it is lumpy: four deals ($128.9M GB200 cloud, $105.8M NexGen Finland, $98.1M Duos, $54.4M Hydra Host) are 73% of the total. Only one loan has been sold (Duos, at par on 1-Oct-26, after ~3.8 months of seasoning), and one was syndicated at close (NexGen Finland, with Silver Point and Victory Park). Duos's 8-K describes the buyer acquiring the borrower's SPV and repaying ~$87.8M, so the first "sale" looks closer to a payoff. Everything else (~$327M) is held in sUSDai. The $1B YE-26 target needs ~$470M more in six weeks.

The engine. Originations are either co-lent at close or funded by sUSDai. sUSDai-funded loans are then sold through GLX after seasoning or held to maturity. Faster seasoning means more sales and more USD.AI Inc. fees but a smaller book and less NIM. Deposits cap the book at the 90% utilization limit: the base case needs sUSDai to grow ~5x to $2.3B by FY2029E to fund $3.2B of originations, unless more loans are sold or co-lent. Revenue splits three ways: the protocol keeps 10% of sUSDai yield; USD.AI Inc. keeps origination fees (2-3%) and 60% of gain on sale; and 40% of gain on sale goes to the Yield Reserve. CHIP's NIM is capped by deposits, not originations. Originations only reach CHIP through USD.AI Inc.'s discretionary buys.

What a loan looks like: Corvex Mid-Atlantic 1 (16-Nov-26). The deployment is 1.6 MW of HGX B200s (>1,000 GPUs) costing $78M, against a $103M three-year take-or-pay contract with an AI inference platform (~$21M of contract revenue per MW a year). It is funded by a $38M non-recourse USD.AI loan (49% of cost), a $26M customer prepayment and $14M of Corvex equity. At an assumed 11.5% APR with straight-line amortization, DSCR averages ~1.24x (1.14x in year 1). USD.AI Inc. earns a ~$0.95M origination fee up front; the protocol earns ~$0.29M a year of NIM while sUSDai holds the loan. At $23.75M of loan per MW, the base case's $3.2B of FY2029E originations would finance ~135 MW and ~83,000 GPUs.

10. 3-Year Model — 4 Scenarios (long form)

Long-form model in the Theia lending format used for Morpho and Aave (Theia_USDai_Model.xlsx): Assumptions (Failure / Bear / Base / Bull drivers, a block per driver with a row per year, active-case switch; the case build at the bottom runs the forecast for every case) → Drivers (loans by customer segment on the Model tab's columns, FY2026E-35E, tied to the Model) → Model (DefiLlama quarterly history plus an FY2025A-FY2035E income statement, balance sheet, cash flow, CHIP buybacks and sUSDai deposit and loan roll-forward) → Valuation Summary → Scenario Analysis (viewpoints and stress weights) → Sources → Raw_Daily. Originations = total AI hardware capex × USD.AI share. Loans retained to maturity plus a seasoning inventory make up the sUSDai book, and deposits = loans ÷ utilization. Credit is the same in every case: 2% of the held book defaults each year, USD.AI repossesses and resells the GPUs (75% LTV, GPUs sold 30% below their normal-path value plus 15% costs), so each default loses ~17% and expected loss is ~0.33% a year, in line with equipment finance; losses run through the Yield Reserve before sUSDai holders, and defaulted loans earn nothing beyond the 3-month debt-service reserve. Protocol revenue = sUSDai NIM (take rate × average loans) + USDai NIM + platform services + the 60% of gain on sale kept by the business, which funds operations (legally USD.AI Inc.'s under CRFC #44; the other 40% goes to the Yield Reserve). Cost of revenue (same in every case, DRAFT) is 0.15% of loans co-lent or sold (placement, legal, trustee) plus 40% of platform-services revenue (partners' cut). Net income to CHIP = revenue − cost of revenue − Foundation opex − depreciation of capitalised software + USD.AI Inc.'s CHIP purchases − 21% tax; free cash flow (net income + D&A − capex) is routed to buybacks under CRFC #47. Capex follows specialty lenders' 10-K capex (mostly capitalised software): 7-14% of revenue in FY2027E, Bull highest (Better 13.6% at $72M of revenue), easing to 2-6.5% by FY2035E (Upstart / Pagaya ~2%, Figure 4%, SoFi / Affirm 6-7%). Exit equity = MAX(net income × Gordon-growth P/E, revenue × 1.0x P/S) + net cash, ÷ (10B FD − CHIP held by the protocol). PV is at a 40% hurdle over 3.12 years. 12. Is This a Theia Investment?
Not yet. Re-underwrite once the first CRFC purchases print (from Jan-27) and deposits stabilize, with the Apr-27 cliff in view. The model shows a real origination business: $531M in a year, 0.32% of $1.0T of FY2029E AI hardware capex, and a base case of $3.2B originated with 79% distributed. But that engine mostly enriches USD.AI Inc.: in the base case CHIP gets ~12% of the economics it generates, and sUSDai has to grow ~5x to $2.3B to fund it. On draft weights CHIP is worth $0.0655 a token, 2.28x MOIC and a 30% IRR, with P(loss) 75%. The base case alone is worth $0.0278, just below today's $0.0287, so the upside rests on the full-platform Fannie-for-compute case.

What would change the answer:

  • CRFC #47 passing and the first quarterly purchases printing on-chain, with Foundation opex disclosed so the net NIM can be checked; a later burn of the protocol-held CHIP.
  • sUSDai back above $600M after the PYUSD roll-off, with utilization below 85%.
  • USD.AI Inc. committing a fixed share of origination / gain-on-sale revenue to CHIP.
  • The escrowed $128.9M loan funding cleanly, and no QumulusAI impairment.
  • Price near the draft PV (~$0.023) ahead of the cliff, or USD.AI Inc. committing 20%+ of its revenue to CHIP.
