Bullish | Preliminary Memo for Theia
1. Business Overview

Bullish (NYSE: BLSH) is an institutionally focused, globally regulated digital asset platform that runs the Bullish Exchange (spot, perpetuals, dated futures and options) and owns CoinDesk, the industry's leading index, data, media and events franchise. Incorporated in the Cayman Islands in June 2021 and seeded by Block.one with ~164k BTC, $100M of cash and 20M EOS, Bullish launched its exchange in late 2021 and has since processed more than $1.5 trillion of cumulative volume. Its core technical differentiation is a patented hybrid matching engine that combines a high-performance central limit order book (CLOB) with Automated Market Making Instructions (AMMI) — an on-exchange, oracle-independent AMM that lets institutions post price-at-quantity liquidity curves with user-defined spreads and volatility parameters, backed by the company's own multibillion-dollar digital asset balance sheet. The company monetizes in two ways: (1) adjusted transaction revenue — trading volume multiplied by an average trading spread (2.26bps in Q2 2026), which is volatile and tied to the crypto cycle; and (2) Subscription, Services & Other (SS&O) — CoinDesk Indices licensing (#1 digital asset indexer, 15 of 39 global digital asset ETF listings in Q4 2025), CoinDesk Data, CoinDesk Insights/Consensus events, liquidity services, custody and interest income. SS&O hit a record $62.7M in Q2 2026 and is now 68% of adjusted revenue, versus 28% in FY2024 — the business has quietly rotated from a crypto exchange into a crypto information-services and market-infrastructure company.

Bullish IPO'd on the NYSE on August 13, 2025 at $37 (above a $32–33 raised range), raising ~$1.1B — received largely in stablecoins — and closed day one at $68. The stock has since round-tripped: at $29.13 it sits 21% below the IPO price and 57% below the first-day close, dragged by a 2026 crypto drawdown that cut BTC spot volumes and forced ~$1.3B of IFRS mark-to-market and impairment charges in H1 2026 alone on the BTC/ETH inventory that funds the AMM (FY2025 IFRS net loss $(785.5)M; H1 2026 $(884.9)M). The defining strategic event is the pending $4.2B acquisition of Equiniti from Siris (announced May 5, 2026; ~$2.35B in BLSH stock at a $38.48 VWAP ≈ 61.1M new shares, plus $1.85B of assumed debt; all antitrust clearances secured; close expected January 2027). Equiniti is the transfer agent and system of record for ~3,000 public companies — ~35% of the S&P 500 and half of the FTSE 100 — with 20M+ verified shareholders and ~$500B of annual payments. Management's thesis is that the transfer agent "defines the legal ownership" of a share, so owning the register plus a regulated venue lets Bullish run the full issuer-sponsored tokenized-equity lifecycle — an architecture the SEC's September 2026 Innovation Exemption explicitly favors. Pro forma, management guides FY2026 combined adjusted revenue of $1.25–1.35B, EBITDA less capex of $490–530M and adjusted net income of $270–290M, with ~6–8% revenue growth, ~20% annual EPS growth and ~$1B of cumulative FCF over 2027–2029.

Bullish holds an unusually broad Tier-1 license stack — NYDFS BitLicense, BaFin MiCAR (EEA passport), Hong Kong SFC, Gibraltar GFSC (tokenized-securities trading live since August 2026), a UK FCA benchmark license and pending CFTC DCM/DCO applications. Options, launched October 2025, reached 14% of global BTC options open interest by April 2026, making Bullish the #2 venue behind Deribit.

Valuation Summary

Bullish screens as one of the most expensive names in fintech on a standalone basis — 63x FY2026E adjusted EPS and 12.4x adjusted revenue — but those headline multiples are misleading on two counts. First, roughly half of the market cap is balance sheet: $2.1B of net liquid assets at June 30 book, plus ~$450M of BTC appreciation since. Netting that out, the operating business trades at an EV of ~$2.1B, or ~16x FY2026E adjusted EBITDA — fair for a 36%-margin franchise growing SS&O 40%+, full for a transaction business whose volumes fell 45% from Q4 2025 to Q3 2026. Second, the case is no longer the standalone exchange: on management's pro forma FY2026 guidance (~$1.26 EPS on ~222M shares) the stock trades at 23x earnings and ~12.7x EBITDA less capex — in line with the market-infrastructure median (21x FY1 P/E) and Computershare (~19.5x). The $40.82 consensus target (12 brokers, Hold) implies 40% upside, but targets are falling (JPMorgan $25, Rosenblatt $30, Wells Fargo $35) and the stock trades 24% below the $38.48 at which ~61M shares go to Siris. This is a "show-me" setup on whether Equiniti earns a transfer-agent multiple plus any tokenization credit — with ~21% of a thin float sold short.

3. Bull & Bear Cases
Bull Cases

Equiniti Makes Bullish the Owner of the Register. The SEC's September 2026 Innovation Exemption requires tokenized NMS stocks to carry rights identical to conventional shares and gives issuers a 30-day veto over third-party tokenizers — which structurally favors the issuer-sponsored model over synthetic wrappers (Robinhood's EU stock tokens, xStocks). The entity that controls the issuer relationship and the legal register is the transfer agent. Post-close, Bullish will own the register for ~3,000 issuers (~35% of the S&P 500, ~50% of the FTSE 100) and 20M+ holders, plus a regulated venue already trading issuer-sponsored tokens in Gibraltar. If even 5% of Equiniti's issuers tokenize by 2028 and Bullish captures a primary venue role, tokenization/blockchain services (guided at ~+20% growth) could add $50–150M of high-margin revenue on top of a base that "rounds to zero" today — and re-rate the combined company from a 15–20x transfer agent toward a 30x+ platform multiple.

Pro Forma Earnings Power Is Hidden by Standalone Consensus. Street numbers still model Bullish standalone ($0.46 FY2026E / $0.72 FY2027E adjusted EPS) because the deal has not closed. Management's combined FY2026 guidance — $1.25–1.35B adjusted revenue, $490–530M EBITDA less capex, $270–290M adjusted net income — implies ~$1.26 pro forma EPS on ~222M shares, and the 2027–2029 framework (EBITDA less capex compounding ~$100M/yr toward a 50%+ exit margin, $25–50M net cost synergies, ~20% EPS CAGR, ~$1B cumulative FCF) puts FY2028E EPS at ~$1.80–1.90. At $29.13 that is ~16x FY2028E, for a business where ~85% of revenue will be recurring fees with >95% client retention (Q2'26 call; ~99% cited at announcement) and 15+ year tenure. When consensus migrates to pro forma numbers in Q1 2027, the optical P/E collapses from ~63x to ~19x.

Crypto Cycle Recovery on a Leaner Cost Base. Q3 2026 trading volume of $122B is the lowest since the IPO (vs. $252B in Q1 2025) while the average spread has widened to 2.26bps (from 1.30bps in Q2 2025) as the mix shifts toward options and higher-spread spot. Adjusted transaction revenue therefore bottomed at only ~$27–30M/quarter despite a 52% volume decline. A return to FY2025 volumes (~$800B) at a 2.0–2.2bps spread would add $40–50M of transaction revenue at near-100% incremental margin; a 2021-style cycle ($1.2T+) would add $120M+. Meanwhile the ~$1.6B BTC position (marked) and CoinDesk index fees (which scale with ETF AUM, e.g., $400M+ of Q2 inflows into Morgan Stanley's CoinDesk-benchmarked ETPs) give the equity convex upside to a BTC recovery.

Options Franchise Becomes a Durable #2 to Deribit. Bullish went from zero to 29% peak share of global BTC options volume and 14% of open interest within six months of its October 2025 launch, and has listed options out to January 2029 — the longest-dated in crypto — which is precisely the tenor structured-products desks (Marex, 3,400 clients) need to hedge. With Coinbase now owning Deribit, Bullish is the only scaled, regulated, independent alternative for banks and brokers that will not route to a competitor. Portfolio margining (up to 50% lower margin on delta-hedged books) and integrations with Elwood, Kemet and Immersive Finance deepen institutional stickiness. Options and perps also bring in the higher-frequency, more spread-rich flow that lifts blended take rates.

CoinDesk Cross-Sell Flywheel and Regulatory Moat. SS&O has grown from $61M (FY2024) to a $222M LTM run-rate, beating guidance in every quarter since IPO, and ~55% of 2026 Consensus sponsorship revenue came from clients who also buy liquidity services or index licensing. CoinDesk Indices is the #1 digital-asset ETF indexer (15 of 39 global listings in Q4 2025) with an FCA benchmark administrator license; every new spot ETF (SOL, XRP, basket products) is an annuity. Combined with the BitLicense, MiCAR passport, SFC, GFSC and pending CFTC DCM/DCO licenses, Bullish is one of a handful of venues a regulated bank can actually onboard to — an advantage that compounds as the GENIUS Act brings bank-issued stablecoins (Bullish was first to list SoFiUSD) onto exchanges.

Bear Cases

The Equity Is a Levered Crypto Treasury in Disguise. Bullish carries ~$1.2B (book) of BTC to fund its AMM, plus ~$950M of digital asset financial assets and ~$270M of crypto loans. IFRS treats most of the BTC as intangible assets — impaired on the way down, not written back up through P&L — which produced $(785.5)M of FY2025 net loss and $(884.9)M in H1 2026 alone, cutting book equity from $3.98B (Q3 2025) to $2.46B. Net liquid assets have fallen from $3.49B to $2.10B in three quarters. A further 30% BTC drawdown would erase another ~$450M of equity and weaken the liquidity buffer that management has promised to keep above $500M post-close — just as the company assumes $1.85B of Equiniti debt.

Equiniti Is a Low-Growth, Rate-Sensitive Transfer Agent Bought at the Top. Of Equiniti's ~$915M 2026E revenue, ~$230M is float interest income on client cash (~2/3 USD, 1/3 GBP, unhedged) — near-100% margin revenue that compresses directly with Fed and BoE cuts. Core issuer and holder services ($685M) grow mid-single digits. Siris, a sophisticated PE seller, took ~61M Bullish shares at $38.48 (now worth ~$1.8B, a 24% markdown) and carved out the non-core lines before close. If 150bp of rate cuts arrive by 2028, float income could fall $70–80M (≈ $0.25–0.30 of EPS), and the "~20% EPS CAGR" framework becomes ~10%. Investors would then be paying a crypto multiple for a Computershare.

Transaction Revenue Is Concentrated and Losing Share. In FY2023, the top five customers accounted for 71% of trading volume and 69% of adjusted transaction revenue — the exchange is effectively a venue for a handful of market makers and arbitrageurs. Management admitted on the Q2 2026 call that spot volumes and market share declined ("Crypto is a lousy environment for trading right now"), and options OI share has slipped from the April 2026 14% peak as Deribit (now inside Coinbase) and CME compete. Spreads have widened in a thin market; if volume recovers, competitive pressure from Coinbase, OKX and Binance could compress spreads back toward the ~1.3bps of mid-2025, capping the transaction recovery.

Tokenization Is Years From Material Revenue. Management explicitly does not expect tokenized equities to lift transaction revenue in 2026 and only calls 2027 a "contributor." Equiniti's blockchain revenue "rounds to zero" today. The SEC exemption is conditional, five years in duration, and permissioned; PartnerCap calls it "investable, not scalable." Issuers must opt in one by one, broker-dealers (Robinhood, Schwab, IBKR) own the retail order flow, and DTCC is building its own tokenization rails. If adoption follows the multi-decade path of past market-structure changes, Bullish shareholders will have issued 40% more stock to buy a stable transfer agent whose optionality never pays.

Governance, Float and Overhang. Chairman Brendan Blumer and director Kokuei Yuan together own ~50% of the company; insiders hold ~64–74%, leaving a ~25% float with ~21% of it sold short. Post-close, Siris and rollover holders will own ~27.5% with two board seats, creating a large future seller. Bullish is a Cayman-domiciled FPI reporting under IFRS with non-standard adjusted metrics, a $496M on-demand loan from a Block.one-related SPV at 7%, and no buyback. The combination of a controlled register, related-party funding, opaque SS&O disclosure (no split by line) and complex crypto accounting is a meaningful discount factor for generalist investors.

4. Helmer's 7 Powers

1. Scale Economies Moderate
Exchange economics are classic fixed-cost businesses: the matching engine, compliance stack and licensing footprint cost roughly the same whether the venue clears $120B or $250B a quarter. Bullish's adjusted opex has been held to ~$48–63M per quarter with a ~400-person team while EBITDA margins ranged from 14% (Q2 2025, low volume) to 48% (Q4 2025, high volume) — the operating leverage is real. Equiniti adds a second scale layer: transfer agency is a processing business where register maintenance, proxy, dividend payment and corporate action workflows are amortized across 3,000 issuers and 20M holders, and management's $50–75M gross cost-takeout target reflects platform consolidation. However, Bullish's exchange is sub-scale relative to Binance (7x Coinbase volume) and Coinbase, so it does not enjoy a unit-cost advantage versus the leaders; its scale benefits are relative to smaller regulated venues, not to the industry.

2. Network Effects Moderate
Liquidity begets liquidity: tighter spreads and deeper books attract more institutional flow, which in turn tightens spreads. Bullish has engineered a partial shortcut around the cold-start problem by seeding the AMM with its own multibillion-dollar balance sheet, which the company showed preserved materially more order-book depth than offshore venues during the October 10, 2025 flash crash. Options open interest exhibits a stronger network effect (open positions anchor to the venue until expiry, and long-dated listings to 2029 lock in structured-product hedgers). CoinDesk Indices also has a benchmark network effect — once an ETF tracks a CoinDesk index, the AP, market-maker and derivative ecosystem standardizes around it. That said, top-5 customer concentration (71% of FY2023 volume) shows the exchange network is still narrow, and crypto liquidity is notoriously mobile across venues.

3. Counter-Positioning Strong
Bullish's strongest strategic power is counter-positioning against offshore crypto exchanges and against legacy transfer agents. Binance, OKX and Bybit built enormous businesses on retail leverage and lighter regulation; adopting Bullish's full-reserve, segregated, multi-Tier-1-licensed model would require them to exit lucrative jurisdictions and products. Conversely, incumbent transfer agents (Computershare, Broadridge) earn stable fees and float income from the existing DTC/Cede & Co. "street name" structure; a world where the token is the legal share and holders sit directly on an on-chain register disintermediates parts of the custody and settlement chain those incumbents partner with, so they have weak incentives to lead it. Bullish has nothing to cannibalize — it can embrace issuer-sponsored tokenization with 24/7 trading and atomic settlement precisely because it has no legacy profit pool to protect. The Equiniti acquisition is the purchase of an incumbent's customer base by the counter-positioned entrant.

4. Switching Costs Moderate
Switching costs differ sharply by segment. For the exchange, they are low-to-moderate: market makers connect via FIX/REST/WebSocket to many venues and can re-route flow within days, though portfolio margining across spot/perps/options and institutional integrations (Elwood, Kemet, Marex) add friction. For CoinDesk Indices, switching costs are high — changing an ETF's benchmark requires board approval, shareholder notice and tracking-error risk. For Equiniti, switching costs are very high: management cites 95–99% average retention and 15+ year issuer tenure; migrating a shareholder register, dividend reinvestment plans and employee share plans is an operationally risky, rarely attempted project. Post-close, the weighted average switching cost of Bullish's revenue base rises dramatically (from ~35% to ~85% sticky revenue), which is the heart of the strategic shift.

5. Branding Moderate
CoinDesk is arguably the most recognized media brand in crypto, and Consensus (26,000+ attendees across Miami and Hong Kong in 2026) is the industry's flagship convening, giving Bullish a marketing funnel no competing exchange can replicate without the reputational baggage of owning the press that covers it. Tom Farley's NYSE pedigree and the "regulated, full-reserve, post-FTX" positioning carry genuine weight with bank and asset-manager risk committees. But the "Bullish" exchange brand itself has limited awareness outside institutional circles, and in trading, brand rarely commands a price premium — institutions route to the best spread. Branding helps win mandates (index licensing, Consensus sponsorships, coalition leadership); it does not raise take rates.

6. Cornered Resource Strong
Three assets qualify. (a) The licensing stack: the BitLicense, MiCAR passport, SFC VATP, GFSC tokenized-securities approval, FCA benchmark license and a registered U.S. transfer agent took years and are not purchasable on demand; few venues globally hold all of them. (b) Equiniti's register relationships: being the legal system of record for ~3,000 issuers is the scarce chokepoint for issuer-sponsored tokenization under the SEC framework — a tokenizer needs the issuer's consent and the transfer agent's cooperation. (c) The patented AMMI technology and seeded balance sheet: the hybrid CLOB/AMM is patented, and the ~20k BTC inventory funding it is a resource competitors would need billions to replicate. CoinDesk's 40%+ share of digital asset ETF index mandates is a fourth, smaller cornered resource.

7. Process Power Weak
Bullish is a five-year-old company with ~400 employees; it has not yet demonstrated embedded organizational processes that competitors cannot replicate. Its execution record is good — options scaled to #2 globally within months, SS&O has beaten guidance every quarter, and licensing was secured across six jurisdictions — but these reflect leadership quality more than institutionalized process. The coming test is integration: merging a ~400-person crypto-native exchange with a much larger, regulated, UK-centric transfer agent (Equiniti) across technology, compliance and culture is the kind of complex execution where process power would be built or found wanting. Greenwich Research flags integration friction as the central execution risk.

Summary: Bullish is a 2–3 power business today, with genuine strength in Counter-Positioning (regulated, full-reserve, tokenization-native against offshore exchanges and legacy transfer agents) and Cornered Resource (licensing stack, patented AMMI plus seeded balance sheet, and — post-close — the Equiniti register). Scale Economies, Network Effects, Switching Costs and Branding are moderate; switching costs step up sharply once Equiniti's 95–99%-retention register business is consolidated. Process Power is unproven. The moats protect the option on tokenization; they do not yet protect the transaction revenue line, which remains contestable.

5. Porter's 5 Forces

1. Threat of New Entrants Moderate
For an offshore crypto exchange, barriers to entry are low — dozens launch every cycle. For a regulated, institutional venue, barriers are high: the BitLicense, MiCAR authorization, SFC VATP and CFTC registrations take years, require audited capital and controls, and are increasingly the price of admission for bank and asset-manager flow. The more relevant entrant threat is TradFi incumbents moving into crypto and tokenization with existing licenses and client bases: CME (crypto futures and options), Cboe, Nasdaq and ICE (tokenized securities initiatives), DTCC (tokenized collateral and settlement), and brokers like Robinhood and Schwab (direct crypto and tokenized-stock offerings). These incumbents do not need to build a register — they already sit next to one. In transfer agency, entry is slow and capital-light competitors (Vertalo, Securitize as an SEC-registered TA) exist, but displacing a 15-year incumbent at a large issuer is rare.

2. Bargaining Power of Suppliers Low
Bullish's key "suppliers" are market makers and liquidity providers, third-party custodians (BitGo, Fireblocks, Coinbase Custody, with BitGo carrying $250M cold-wallet insurance policies), cloud infrastructure, stablecoin issuers (Circle, Paxos, SocGen-FORGE, SoFi) and engineering talent. The proprietary AMM reduces dependency on third-party market makers, and multiple custodians remove single-point-of-failure risk. The most important "supplier" relationship is regulatory licensing, which is not priced but can be withdrawn. Talent in crypto market structure is scarce, but Bullish's NYSE-heritage leadership and equity currency make it competitive. Overall supplier power is low; the one notable dependency is the $496M related-party loan from a Block.one-affiliated SPV, which is repayable on demand.

3. Bargaining Power of Buyers High
On the exchange, buyer power is high. Institutional traders are sophisticated, price-sensitive and multi-homed; the top five customers have historically generated ~70% of volume and transaction revenue, and they negotiate rebates and fee tiers aggressively. Spreads fell from 2.6bps (Q2 2024) to 1.3bps (Q2 2025) before widening again in the 2026 lull. For CoinDesk Indices, buyers (ETF issuers) have moderate power — fees are a small fraction of the expense ratio, but issuers compare benchmark providers (CF Benchmarks, MarketVector, S&P). For Equiniti, issuer buyer power is moderate: large-cap issuers run RFPs and negotiate fees, but switching is rare and costly. Weighted by today's revenue mix, buyer power is high; post-Equiniti it moderates.

4. Threat of Substitutes High
Substitutes are abundant on the trading side: CME-listed futures and options, spot BTC/ETH ETFs (which pull activity off crypto venues into brokerage accounts), on-chain decentralized exchanges and perp DEXs (Hyperliquid, Jupiter), OTC desks and internalizing brokers (Robinhood, Coinbase Prime). In tokenization, synthetic stock tokens (xStocks, Robinhood EU tokens), DTCC-led tokenized settlement, and broker-led "tokenized share classes" all compete with the issuer-sponsored, transfer-agent-led model. CoinDesk Data competes with Kaiko, Amberdata and Bloomberg; CoinDesk media competes with free content. Only the transfer agent function itself — maintaining the legal register — has few substitutes, which is why the Equiniti deal matters strategically.

5. Competitive Rivalry High
Rivalry in crypto trading is intense and global: Binance (volume >7x Coinbase), Coinbase (now owning Deribit, the #1 options venue), OKX, Bybit, Kraken (private, ~$20B valuation, IPO-bound), Gemini, Robinhood and CME all compete for overlapping institutional flow. Coin Metrics data from mid-2025 showed Bullish at ~22% of spot volume within the peer set of IPO-track exchanges (parity with Kraken; Coinbase 49%), but globally Bullish is a mid-tier venue. In transfer agency the market is an oligopoly (Computershare, Equiniti, Broadridge, plus smaller players) with stable, rational pricing. In indices, CoinDesk competes with CF Benchmarks (Kraken-owned) and S&P/MarketVector. Overall rivalry is high on the revenue lines that matter most to today's numbers and moderate on the lines that matter most to the 2028 thesis.

Summary: Bullish's five-force structure is challenging today and improving post-close. The standalone exchange faces high buyer power, abundant substitutes and intense rivalry from far larger players — a structure that explains volatile, compressing take rates and top-5 customer concentration. The Equiniti register, CoinDesk Indices and the regulatory license stack operate in much more favorable structures (oligopoly, high switching costs, few substitutes). The investment case therefore depends on the combined company's mix shifting toward the structurally advantaged businesses — and on using those positions to capture a share of tokenized-equity trading rather than competing head-on with Binance and Coinbase for crypto spot flow.

6. What Drives the Stock?

The following are the 6 variables that would move BLSH's share price materially in the next 12 months. These are what analysts build tracking models around and PMs ask about in morning meetings.

1. BTC Price and Crypto Trading Volumes. BLSH is still traded as a crypto beta: the stock fell from $56.92 to $20.55 as BTC slid toward ~$60k in mid-2026, then rallied 33% over three months as BTC recovered to ~$80k. BTC drives three things at once — the mark on ~20k BTC held (≈ $20M of book value per $1k move in BTC), CoinDesk index fees (AUM-linked) and exchange volume. Monthly metrics (released ~6th of each month) are the highest-frequency datapoint: Q3 2026 volume was $122B; a return above $200B/quarter at a 2bps+ spread would be bullish, while sub-$100B quarters would signal share loss rather than a cyclical trough.

2. Equiniti Closing and the Pro Forma Re-Base. The January 2027 close is the most important re-rating event. Upon close, Street models will be rebuilt on pro forma numbers (~$1.3B revenue, ~$280M net income vs. $357M / $74M standalone). Watch for: (a) final close timing and any remaining FCA/regulatory conditions, (b) the post-close capital structure — how much BTC/stablecoin liquidity is used to pay down the $1.85B of assumed debt (management targets net leverage ~0 and >$500M net liquid assets), (c) first combined guidance for FY2027 (likely with Q4 results in February 2027). Guidance at or above the ~$1.25–1.35B revenue / ~$600M EBITDA-less-capex trajectory would be bullish; a reset would be severely punished.

3. Interest Rates and Equiniti Float Income. ~$230M of Equiniti's 2026E revenue is interest earned on client cash balances (~2/3 USD, 1/3 GBP, unhedged). This is the single largest profit line in the combined company and near-100% margin. With the U.S. 10-year at ~5.0%, the market is not pricing aggressive cuts, but every 25bp reduction in short rates is worth roughly ~$13–14M of pre-tax income (~$0.05 per pro forma share). A higher-for-longer rate path is quietly bullish for BLSH — an unusual exposure for a crypto name and one the market may not yet have priced. Any decision to hedge the float would reduce volatility and could be well received.

4. Tokenized Equity Adoption Signals. The stock will react to evidence that the issuer-sponsored model is winning: (a) Equiniti issuers announcing tokenized share classes, (b) Coalition membership growth (now 8 firms including QRT, Vertalo, zerohash), (c) Bullish obtaining U.S. approval to run tokenized-equity secondary trading under the SEC Innovation Exemption, (d) tokenized-equity volumes on Bullish Gibraltar, and (e) broker distribution agreements (Alpaca, DriveWealth, Apex). Management has guided that tokenization will not lift 2026 transaction revenue and will only be a "contributor" in 2027 — so the bar is narrative evidence, not revenue, for the next 12 months.

5. SS&O Trajectory vs. Guidance. FY2026 SS&O guidance is $225–245M, implying H2 of $108–128M split ~45/55 between Q3 and Q4 (Q3 ≈ $48–57M). SS&O has beaten guidance every quarter since the IPO; a Q3 print at or above ~$55M and a Q4 outlook at the top of the range would confirm the CoinDesk cross-sell engine is resilient to the trading downturn. Index revenue is the swing factor (it tracks AUM in licensed ETPs), along with interest income on stablecoin and fiat balances. A miss would undercut the "diversified, recurring" story just before the Equiniti close. On the Q2 call, Bonanno flagged interest rates "down almost 20%" year over year (a drag on SS&O interest income) and "further headcount reductions in the third quarter."

6. Short Interest, Float and Lockup Dynamics. With ~21% of a ~39M-share float sold short (5.5 days to cover) and insiders controlling ~64%+ of shares, BLSH is prone to violent squeezes and air-pockets (e.g., +9% on Q2 results, (19%) over the last month). Post-close, ~61M new shares go to Siris and Equiniti rollover holders — roughly 1.6x today's float — creating a potential overhang once any holding restrictions lapse. Conversely, the larger float should support index inclusion and broaden the shareholder base. Any secondary sale, block trade or announced buyback is a material stock driver.

12. Is This a Theia Investment?

(i) Market Cap Bucket: $4.4B Strong Fit

Small Cap ($1B–$5B). Bullish's $4,419M basic market cap ($4,661M fully diluted) sits at the top of Theia's small-cap sweet spot. Post-Equiniti the share count rises to ~222M, taking the pro forma equity value to ~$6.5B (mid-cap) at today's price, so the "small cap" window is closing in January 2027. More important than the headline bucket is the tradable float: only ~39M shares (~$1.1B) are free-floating, with insiders controlling ~64%+ and ~21% of the float sold short. That thin float is a source of mispricing (and of liquidity risk for a position) — exactly the type of structural inefficiency where Theia can build an edge, but sizing must respect ~1.5M shares/day of volume.

(ii) Analyst Coverage: 12 Broker Contributors Moderate Fit

Twelve sell-side analysts cover BLSH (JPMorgan, Jefferies, Citi, Deutsche Bank, Cantor, Bernstein, UBS, KBW, Canaccord, Compass Point, Rosenblatt, Wells Fargo), rated Hold on average (5 Buy / 6 Hold / 1 Sell) with a $40.82 average target and a wide $25–$53 range. That is moderately covered — above Theia's ideal of fewer than five analysts. However, the coverage gap is real where it matters: no Street model we found incorporates Equiniti (all FY2026–FY2027 consensus is standalone), none appears to model float-income rate sensitivity, and estimate dispersion is extreme (FY2026E EPS $0.28–$0.54; targets cut by JPMorgan to $25 and Rosenblatt to $30). The re-basing of consensus to pro forma numbers in early 2027 is an information event the Street has not yet done the work on.

(iii) Thesis Alignment: Blockchain Market Infrastructure / Tokenization Core Thesis

Bullish is about as directly aligned with Theia's internet-finance thesis as a listed equity can be. It is a regulated digital asset exchange (spot, perps, options), the owner of the leading crypto index/data/media franchise (CoinDesk), a stablecoin distribution venue (first to list SoFiUSD; EURCV/USDCV under MiCAR), an on-chain lender (USD.AI GPU credit facility) and — after the Equiniti close — the only public company combining a licensed crypto venue with a top-3 global transfer agent to pursue issuer-sponsored tokenization of public equities. It also tokenized its own cap table on Solana and launched the first issuer-sponsored tokenized equity trading on a regulated venue. Every leg of the Theia thesis — institutionalization of crypto, stablecoins, RWA tokenization, migration of capital-markets plumbing on-chain — runs through this company. Rating: Core.

(iv) Outcome Dispersion: $10 – $89 Strong Fit

The 5-scenario model shows implied FY2028E prices from $9.90 (Bear Severe, (66%)) to $88.64 (Bull Severe, +204%) versus $29.13 today — a ~9x spread between the tails, driven by three largely independent variables: crypto volumes (±$0.58/share of EPS across the cycle), short-term rates on Equiniti float (~$0.20/share per 100bp) and whether tokenization earns a platform multiple (11x–32x). The probability-weighted value is $36.28, or $27.90 discounted at Theia's 30% cost of capital — (4.2%) versus the current price. In other words, the dispersion is genuinely wide, but at $29.13 the market is pricing the distribution roughly fairly; the edge is not in today's price but in the timing — the stock is likely to be volatile through the January close and the first pro forma guidance, offering entry points below the PV.

VERDICT: MAYBE — Core Thesis Fit, Fairly Priced Today; Build on Weakness Into the Equiniti Close

Bullish scores Strong on market cap ($4.4B, thin float), Core on thesis alignment and Strong on outcome dispersion ($10 to $89), with moderate analyst coverage (12 brokers, all still modeling standalone).
