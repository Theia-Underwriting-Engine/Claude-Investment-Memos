Figure Technology Solutions, Inc. | Preliminary Memo for Theia
1. Business Overview

Figure Technology Solutions, Inc. (FIGR) is a San Francisco-based fintech company founded in 2018 by Mike Cagney (former CEO of SoFi) that has built the first vertically integrated, blockchain-native capital markets platform for consumer lending. The company operates across three interlocking layers: (1) FT Intermediate, which originates home equity lines of credit (HELOCs), first-lien mortgages, SMB loans, and DSCR loans through a proprietary LOS that cuts origination cost to under $1,000 and time-to-fund to 5 days versus the industry average of $11,000 and 45 days; (2) Figure Connect, a two-sided capital markets marketplace where originators sell standardized, blockchain-registered loans to institutional buyers at a ~3% ecosystem fee with ~80% contribution margins; and (3) Democratized Prime, a DeFi-native warehouse financing platform where originators borrow against tokenized loan collateral at ~50bps, paired with YLDS, a yield-bearing SEC-registered stablecoin serving as the settlement layer. All loans are registered on the Provenance Blockchain via DART (Digital Asset Registry Technology), creating homogeneous, immutable assets with transparent performance history — the infrastructure that management believes constitutes a durable institutional moat ("you can't AI your way into AAA"). CEO Michael Tannenbaum leads day-to-day operations alongside CFO Macrina Kgil; the company IPO'd on NYSE in August 2025, raising $787.5M. For FY2025, Figure reported $506.9M in revenue (+84% Y/Y), $251.1M in Adjusted EBITDA (48.8% margin), and $8.4B in consumer loan marketplace volume (+63% Y/Y).

3. Bull & Bear Cases
Bull Cases
First-Lien Inflection: Management declared 2026 the "Year of the First Lien" — existing partners originate $300B+ annually in first-lien mortgages that currently route to Fannie/Freddie. At $1K origination cost (vs $11K industry avg), Figure's technology is materially cheaper for first-lien. Capturing even 2% of partner first-lien volume at current take rates doubles marketplace volume.
GSE / DART Acceptance: If Fannie Mae or Freddie Mac formally accept DART as equivalent to MERS, Figure instantly becomes eligible to originate and sell agency mortgages on its blockchain rails. The conforming mortgage market exceeds $2T annually — a single percentage point of market share is $20B in origination volume.
Operating Leverage Flywheel: Q4 2025 demonstrated the thesis: volume +131% Y/Y while OpEx (ex-SBC) grew only 13%. The blockchain infrastructure is fully built; marginal cost of additional volume is near zero. Management targets 60%+ Adj. EBITDA margins vs. 51.6% today, with every incremental dollar falling disproportionately to the bottom line.
DeFi/YLDS Ecosystem Monetization: YLDS crossed $328M in circulation at Q4 2025-end, growing 20x in one quarter. Democratized Prime reached $206M in matched offers after 10x Q/Q growth. As YLDS and DP scale into the billions, they generate recurring fee income independent of origination cycles — transforming Figure from a volume-driven lender into a financial infrastructure platform.
Multi-Asset Expansion (Auto, SMB, DSCR): The Agora Data auto partnership opens the $1.6T auto lending market with no incremental origination cost — pure margin infrastructure fees. SMB loans doubled Q/Q in Q4. DSCR volume grew 4.3x Q/Q. Each new asset class adds supply to Figure Connect, strengthening buyer liquidity and making the marketplace harder to displace.
Bear Cases
Rate Sensitivity / HELOC Demand Shock: Figure's core HELOC business exists because consumers are rate-locked into their primary mortgages. If the Fed cuts rates aggressively (e.g., Fed Funds below 4%), the rate lock-in effect dissolves and consumers rush to cash-out refis. The HELOC TAM could contract 20-40% in a rapid rate-cut environment, and Figure has no revenue protection from this scenario.
Pricing Pressure from Better and NFTYDoor: NFTYDoor has already captured ~38% of HELOC volume at one major partner by undercutting Figure 40-50bps. Better operates 30bps cheaper with comparable technology. If pricing pressure forces Figure to lower its rates broadly, net take rate could compress from 3.8% toward 3.0%, reducing revenue per dollar of volume by ~20%.
DART vs. MERS — GSE Rejection Risk: Until GSEs formally accept DART, Figure cannot originate or sell conforming agency loans. Figure's entire first-lien thesis requires a regulatory breakthrough that faces deep institutional inertia. A multi-year delay keeps Figure confined to the non-QM/non-conforming niche, limiting the TAM ceiling.
"Crypto Stigma" and Reputational Risk: Experts consistently note that major banks refuse to engage with blockchain-branded lenders due to post-FTX reputational concerns. The Q4 2025 phishing breach (SSNs exposed for ~12,400 customers) is exactly the kind of event that reinforces this stigma. A second serious security incident or a Provenance Blockchain vulnerability could be catastrophic for institutional trust.
Customer Concentration and Revenue Fragility: Top 7 customers account for 91% of Figure Exchange trading volume. CMG Financial alone represents ~60% of HELOC unit count and ~50% of HELOC dollar volume within its Figure channel. If even one or two top partners reduce allocations or pivot to competitors, Figure could face acute, short-term revenue shocks disproportionate to their aggregate partner count.
4. Helmer's 7 Powers
1. Scale EconomiesStrong

Figure's cost structure is a textbook scale economy story. The underlying blockchain infrastructure — Provenance Blockchain, DART, the proprietary LOS, and the Figure Connect marketplace — was built over seven years at significant capital investment and is now largely amortized. Each incremental dollar of origination volume requires almost no additional infrastructure spend. This was made explicit in Q4 2025: consumer loan marketplace volume grew 131% year-over-year while operating expenses (excluding stock-based compensation) grew only 13%. That "drop-through" dynamic means that as Figure scales from $8.4B to $20B+ in annual volume, a disproportionately large share of every revenue dollar flows directly to Adjusted EBITDA — the core of the 60%+ margin target. Critically, scale also reinforces the capital markets side: larger aggregate origination volume means more performance history, more data for securitization rating agencies, and broader investor familiarity with the asset class, creating a self-reinforcing credibility cycle that is structurally unavailable to smaller competitors. A new entrant would need 3-5 years of origination history at comparable scale before institutional ABS buyers would consistently purchase their securitizations at comparable spreads.

2. Network EffectsModerate

Figure's marketplace exhibits meaningful multi-sided network effects, though they are still developing relative to mature two-sided markets. On the origination side, 307 active lending partners creates adoption contagion among non-bank mortgage brokers: IMBs increasingly want to use the same rails as their peers, making Figure the industry-standard non-QM HELOC platform. On the investor/liquidity side, each additional institutional buyer (asset managers, insurers, sovereign wealth funds) who participates in Figure Connect improves price discovery and bid-ask spreads for loan sellers, which in turn attracts more originators, who bring more volume, which attracts more buyers — a classic positive feedback loop. Democratized Prime adds a third network dimension: the more assets that are tokenized and pledged on-chain, the deeper the DeFi liquidity pool becomes for all borrowers, further lowering warehouse financing costs. YLDS adds a fourth layer: the more it circulates, the more useful it becomes as a settlement medium for new partners exploring YLDS-funded loan origination (as Newtek is evaluating). These effects are not yet at "winner-take-all" scale — Better and NFTYDoor can still grow simultaneously — but the trajectory is toward a multi-dimensional flywheel whose defensiveness compounds with each passing quarter.

3. Counter-PositioningModerate

Large traditional banks are structurally constrained in their ability to fully replicate Figure's model without damaging their existing franchise. Banks derive significant revenue from the legacy HELOC origination model — branch-intensive, human-underwritten, slow — and have compliance departments, rate committees, and thousands of staff optimized for that old process. Moving to a blockchain-native, DeFi-integrated system that eliminates most human touchpoints would require dismantling the very infrastructure that employs their mortgage workforces, inviting regulatory scrutiny from conservative bank examiners, and associating their regulated institution with a "crypto" brand that post-FTX remains radioactive for mainstream financial institutions. As one expert noted: major banks would rather exit the non-bank HELOC market entirely than risk a "PR nightmare" from a blockchain-associated lending failure. The result is that the most formidable potential capital sources — JPMorgan, Bank of America, Wells Fargo — act as correspondent lenders routing business TO Figure rather than competing with it. However, well-funded fintech competitors like Better (with its Tinman platform) and NFTYDoor face no such incumbency burden, which limits how structural this advantage truly is at the fintech competitive layer.

4. Switching CostsModerate

Once a mortgage origination partner integrates with Figure's white-label platform, the switching costs are meaningful but not insurmountable. The integration process involves API connectivity between the partner's LOS and Figure's system, retraining loan officers on a new application workflow, replacing co-branded marketing materials, and potentially renegotiating capital markets agreements — a process AlphaSense expert calls suggest takes several months and requires sustained grassroots sales effort at the loan-officer level. The key insight is that switching must be sold not just to the partner's technology team but to individual loan officers who have built "muscle memory" around Figure's UI, and who must be personally convinced the new system is meaningfully better before they change their daily behavior. At 307 active partners, Figure has created organizational entrenchment across a broad swath of the non-bank lending ecosystem. However, switching costs have clear limits: if Better or NFTYDoor sustain a 40-50bps pricing advantage for 6-12 months, the economic incentive to endure integration friction grows substantially — meaning these switching costs protect margin erosion in the short-term but are not a permanent moat against sustained, material price competition.

5. BrandingWeak

Figure's brand is primarily B2B, not B2C. The majority of loan volume flows through the partner-branded channel: the end borrower transacts with their mortgage broker's co-branded product and may never know Figure originated the loan. This is the intentional design of the white-label architecture, and it entirely prevents Figure from building consumer brand equity that could drive direct demand or justify rate premiums at the borrower level. Within the institutional B2B ecosystem, Figure's brand connotes speed, technical innovation, and reliability — Mike Cagney's track record with SoFi is widely respected across non-bank lending — but this institutional credibility is not transferable to consumer pricing power. The Q4 2025 phishing breach (approximately 12,400 Social Security numbers exposed) is exactly the kind of reputational incident that can damage the institutional brand in the short-to-medium term, particularly with risk-averse bank correspondents and institutional securitization buyers who are already cautious about blockchain-associated counterparties.

6. Cornered ResourceModerate

Figure controls three proprietary assets that are difficult to replicate. First, DART: the first blockchain-native lien registry providing an on-chain alternative to MERS (Mortgage Electronic Registration Systems). DART automatically updates loan ownership by "listening" to immutable Provenance Blockchain activity in real time, eliminating the periodic manual reconciliation required by MERS and reducing assignment fees by an estimated $50/loan while cutting third-party diligence costs by up to 80%. Building an equivalent registered system requires years of industry acceptance, legal precedent establishment, and institutional relationship-building with rating agencies and GSEs — none of which can be bought quickly. Second, the accumulated performance data: Figure has $20B+ in tokenized, standardized consumer lending performance history on-chain — the AAA-eligible track record that institutional ABS buyers require. Third, YLDS: a first-mover yield-bearing stablecoin with SEC registration, natively integrated into mortgage settlement infrastructure. Note that DART's value as a cornered resource is contingent on GSE acceptance; if MERS retains monopoly in agency channels indefinitely, DART's moat is constrained to the non-conforming, private credit segment.

7. Process PowerStrong

This is Figure's most durable competitive advantage. The organizational capability to originate a mortgage in 5 days for under $1,000 — compared to the industry average of 45 days and $11,045 — is not a software feature that can be downloaded or copied. It is a deeply embedded operational process comprising a fully proprietary LOS, automated AVM (automated valuation model) for property assessment, digital title review, blockchain lien perfection, automated underwriting rules, and integrated capital markets execution, all built and refined over seven years of production origination. Management is now layering AI across this stack: an AI customer service agent (75% chat containment rate), AI-assisted title review, and AI-based underwriting validation — and, critically, these AI agents trained on Figure's origination workflows can be deployed on third-party assets to certify them ("Figure Certified" program), extending the process moat into new asset originator onboarding. The key insight: process power in financial services requires not just the technology but the organizational judgment, the institutional relationships with rating agencies and securitization buyers, and the operational trust that can only be earned through years of reliable performance. As management puts it, "you cannot AI your way into AAA" — and this is precisely right. A competitor starting today would need 3-5 years of at-scale origination before AAA-rated securitizations became routinely accessible.

Summary: Figure's strongest moats are Process Power (the vertically integrated origination + capital markets stack) and Scale Economies (infrastructure leverage on incremental volume). Network Effects and Counter-Positioning provide meaningful but still-developing structural advantages. Branding is the weakest link, creating vulnerability to direct competitor pricing strategies.

5. Porter's 5 Forces
1. Threat of New EntrantsModerate

Entering the digitized non-QM HELOC origination market meaningfully requires building a compliant LOS, establishing blockchain infrastructure with institutional acceptance, cultivating capital markets relationships with ABS investors and warehouse lenders, accumulating years of origination performance history for securitization purposes, and building a 300+ partner network — a 3-5 year timeline and several hundred million dollars at minimum. These are substantial barriers. However, the front-end user experience (the point-of-sale, the application interface) can be replicated far faster with modern SaaS development tools, as Better and NFTYDoor have demonstrated. The primary entry barrier is the back-end: Figure's institutional ABS buyer relationships, its credibility with rating agencies who have issued AAA ratings on its securitizations, and its $20B+ of performance history. Large tech companies (e.g., a fintech arm of Apple, Google, or Stripe) would have the capital to accelerate entry, though the regulatory complexity of mortgage origination creates additional drag. The threat is "Moderate" — significant infrastructure barriers exist but are surmountable for well-capitalized, focused entrants.

2. Bargaining Power of SuppliersLow

Figure's "suppliers" are primarily the Provenance Blockchain network, cloud infrastructure providers (Amazon Web Services and similar), automated valuation model (AVM) data providers, and credit bureau data services. The Provenance Blockchain is a public, permissioned ledger with no single supplier who can extract rent — notably, Figure's co-founders were involved in its creation, giving them deep protocol influence and favorable positioning. Cloud computing is highly commoditized, with multiple competing providers. AVM services (CoreLogic, Clear Capital, etc.) and credit bureau data are widely available from multiple competing vendors. Figure has intentionally built its most critical functions in-house — the LOS, capital markets operations, servicing — eliminating dependence on external vendors for core revenue-generating capabilities. Supplier power is minimal and not a material business risk.

3. Bargaining Power of BuyersModerate

Figure serves two buyer groups with meaningfully different power dynamics. Origination partners (the 307 IMBs, banks, and broker networks) have growing alternatives in Better, NFTYDoor, and Spring EQ, and are increasingly willing to allocate volume to whichever provider offers the best combination of speed and economics. One expert confirmed NFTYDoor captured ~38% of HELOC volume at a major partner solely through pricing; another noted Better's pricing is roughly 30bps cheaper than Figure's. As the market matures and alternatives proliferate, partners will have more credible alternatives and more leverage in negotiations. Institutional capital markets buyers (ABS investors, insurance companies, sovereign wealth funds who buy loans on Figure Connect) also hold meaningful power: they can demand tighter spreads or higher premiums on less familiar products (e.g., first-lien or auto loans that lack Figure's years of HELOC performance history). That said, the depth of institutional demand for standardized, blockchain-native home equity assets appears to be structurally growing, and Figure's monopoly on DART/Provenance-registered assets provides some protection against competing for the exact same asset-class buyers.

4. Threat of SubstitutesModerate

The primary economic substitutes for a Figure HELOC are: (1) cash-out refinance — directly competes for the same consumer need (unlocking home equity) but is currently unattractive because consumers hold low fixed-rate mortgages from pre-2022; (2) bank HELOCs — slower and often unavailable (many large banks exited this market post-COVID), but trust and rate subsidies occasionally win for bank-loyal consumers; (3) personal loans at much higher rates for smaller amounts; (4) home equity investments (HEIs) from Unison, Point, etc. — a niche but growing substitute. The single most important substitute threat is the rate environment: a sustained Federal Reserve easing cycle that brings 30-year mortgage rates below 5.5% would dissolve the rate lock-in effect driving HELOC demand and make cash-out refis economically rational for millions of households. Figure's strategic response to this risk — expanding aggressively into first-lien originations (now 19% of volume) — directly converts the substitute threat into an addressable market, which is strategically sound but requires time and execution to fully realize.

5. Competitive RivalryModerate

In the non-bank digitized HELOC space, rivalry has intensified meaningfully since Figure's IPO. NFTYDoor is already capturing ~38% of volume at one major partner with a 40-50bps price advantage; Better's Tinman platform is described by industry experts as "comparable in user experience" and priced ~30bps cheaper. Spring EQ and other non-QM originators are also growing. That said, traditional bank competitors remain structurally constrained (see Counter-Positioning above), and the overall mortgage origination market is large enough ($2T+ annually) that multiple competitors can coexist. Figure's competitive position is strongest in the marketplace infrastructure layer (Figure Connect and Democratized Prime), where no direct competitor yet offers equivalent institutional-grade tokenized liquidity. The risk is that rivalry intensifies specifically in the origination layer (HELOC pricing), where Figure's premium is most exposed. Figure is executing a rational strategic response — shifting volume toward the capital-light Connect model and diversifying into first-lien, auto, and SMB — which reduces its dependence on any single origination product's competitive intensity.

Summary: The competitive environment is moderately challenging. The origination layer faces intensifying fintech rivalry on pricing, but the capital markets infrastructure layer (Figure Connect, Democratized Prime, YLDS) remains effectively uncontested. The industry structure is more favorable than a commoditized mortgage lender but less protected than a pure enterprise software marketplace.

6. What Drives the Stock?

These are the 6 variables that would move FIGR materially in the next 12 months. They are, in priority order:

1. Consumer Loan Marketplace Volume: The primary KPI. Full-year 2025 came in at $8.4B (+63% Y/Y); consensus implies ~$12B for 2026E. Any quarter where volume growth meaningfully exceeds or disappoints vs. street models will drive the stock proportionally. Watch for sequential Q/Q trends (seasonal: Nov-Feb typically slower), partner count expansion beyond 307, and any announced "mega-partner" relationships. This is the single most read-through metric for the entire thesis.

2. Net Take Rate Trajectory: Management guided 3.5%-4.0% NTM for 2026. A compression toward 3.3% (due to pricing pressure from Better/NFTYDoor or unfavorable product mix) signals competitive erosion and/or margin risk. A hold at 3.8%+ signals pricing power retention. The market will interpret any sustained take rate compression as a sign that Figure's pricing premium is fading — highly negative for multiple. This is the most important "tell" on competitive dynamics.

3. Figure Connect Penetration Rate: Connect volume was 54% of total in Q4 2025, up from 0% 18 months prior. The migration toward Connect is the key driver of Adj. EBITDA margin expansion (80% contribution margin on Connect vs. balance-sheet-heavy legacy model). Every 5-10 percentage points of additional Connect penetration represents meaningful structural margin improvement. The market has begun pricing in the capital-light model; any reversal (balance sheet growing as % of volume) would be viewed negatively.

4. First-Lien / New Product Traction: Management declared 2026 "The Year of the First Lien." First-lien grew from 12% to 19% of mix in 2025. Any data points confirming first-lien acceleration (toward 30%+) validate the multi-trillion dollar TAM expansion story and would likely expand the stock's multiple. Conversely, slow first-lien adoption signals that the GSE/conventional mortgage channel remains structurally closed to Figure.

5. YLDS Circulation / Democratized Prime Balance: YLDS crossed $328M at Q4 end (up 20x in one quarter). Democratized Prime reached $206M in matched offers (+10x Q/Q). These DeFi metrics are optionality-priced by the market — they're small today but carry significant embedded value if they scale. The OPEN on-chain equity marketplace (first public company to issue blockchain-native shares in Q4 2025) adds another metric to watch. Growth in these emerging verticals signals that Figure is becoming a financial infrastructure platform, not just a mortgage company — a potentially significant multiple-expanding narrative.

6. GSE/DART Regulatory Developments: A binary catalyst. Any public announcement that Fannie Mae or Freddie Mac are formally testing or accepting DART as equivalent to MERS would be a major positive re-rating event — opening the $2T+ conforming mortgage market. Conversely, regulatory headwinds against DeFi or blockchain-based lien registries (particularly from CFPB or state regulators) could suppress the multiple. This is a low-probability/high-impact driver that the market is not fully pricing into current levels.

The Moat Debate: Infrastructure vs. User Experience

The most critical finding from AlphaSense expert interviews is a distinction that sell-side research largely misses: Figure's moat is in the back end, not the front end. Management frequently highlights the speed (5 days) and cost efficiency (<$1,000 origination) of its technology, which is visible and marketable. But experts unanimously argue that this front-end user experience — the automated AVM, digital data collection, e-sign workflow — is the most replicable part of Figure's business. Large legacy players or well-funded competitors could build comparable point-of-sale experiences within 12-18 months, as Better has demonstrated. The genuine moat is Figure's back-end capital markets infrastructure: standardized, blockchain-registered loan assets with transparent, immutable performance history that allows securitization at attractive spreads, a liquid two-sided marketplace (Figure Connect) providing price certainty to originators, and the institutional relationships with ABS investors who trust the underwriting standardization. As one expert bluntly noted, smaller brokers and non-bank lenders "lack the capital markets capability to sell loans effectively on the back end" — making Figure's integrated liquidity pool "incredibly difficult to displace." This distinction has significant investment implications: the correct question is not "can competitors build a faster HELOC app?" (yes, they can) but "can competitors build institutional-grade, standardized capital markets infrastructure for non-conforming consumer loans?" (no, not within a 3-5 year timeframe).

The "Convenience Store" Premium and Its Sustainability

Multiple expert calls independently converge on the same metaphor: Figure is the "convenience store" of mortgage origination — borrowers and loan officers pay a premium for exceptional speed and frictionlessness. Historically, Figure has commanded a pricing premium of 40-75 basis points over alternative products, and has maintained that premium because no comparable alternative existed at the same speed/cost combination. This pricing power is now actively being contested. NFTYDoor has captured approximately 38% of HELOC volume at one major partner (CMG Financial's VP directly confirmed this) by offering a 40-50bps cheaper rate — approaching the breakeven point where loan officers find it economically rational to absorb switching friction. Better currently operates 30bps cheaper than Figure with a Tinman platform described by industry experts as "highly comparable" in user experience quality. The strategic risk here is not binary (lose all partners) but gradual: sustained pricing pressure could force Figure to lower its ecosystem fee from ~3.0% toward 2.5%, permanently compressing net take rates below the 3.5% management guidance floor. This scenario is most acute in the second-lien HELOC product specifically — and is exactly why management's "Year of the First Lien" pivot is strategically rational.

AI: Enhancer, Not Disruptor

A key debate in FIGR's investor community is whether AI represents a threat or a tailwind for Figure's model. AlphaSense expert calls, combined with management's own commentary, suggest a nuanced answer: AI makes Figure's process faster and cheaper (tailwind for market share), but cannot replicate the institutional trust, liquidity relationships, and standardized asset history that constitute Figure's marketplace moat (AI is not a threat to the back-end moat). Specifically, Figure has deployed an AI customer service agent achieving 75% chat volume containment, AI-assisted property title review as a parallel validation step against underwriting guidelines, and AI tools for certifying third-party originated assets under the "Figure Certified" program. The most strategically significant AI development is the emergence of partner-led AI sales agents that walk consumers through the white-labeled Figure HELOC product — partners are actively choosing to divert AI workflow volume to Figure over Fannie/Freddie channels because Figure's fully digitized process is "perfectly optimized for AI workflows." This creates an unexpected AI-driven adoption flywheel: as AI sales agents proliferate across the mortgage ecosystem, Figure's product becomes relatively more competitive against legacy origination workflows. Management's "you can't AI your way into AAA" formulation elegantly captures why AI doesn't threaten Figure's institutional capital markets infrastructure: rating agencies require years of origination history, and no AI can manufacture a credible 7-year performance track record overnight.

Misunderstood Economics: Who Actually Benefits from Figure's Cost Savings

A frequently misunderstood aspect of Figure's platform economics — documented across multiple expert calls — is the distribution of value captured from Figure's origination cost efficiency. While Figure's marketing emphasizes its ability to slash borrower costs through superior technology, expert calls reveal that the $10,000+ in cost savings per origination do not necessarily flow to borrowers in the form of lower interest rates, nor do they meaningfully increase the commission of the referring loan officer. Instead, the reduced operational costs primarily benefit the lending partner's corporate bottom line, allowing them to remain profitable on smaller loan balances ($50K-$60K) that would be economically unviable under traditional cost structures. This is an important insight for modeling Figure's competitive durability: the value proposition is primarily B2B (enabling lenders to serve a previously unprofitable market segment), not B2C (giving consumers cheaper loans). This B2B orientation means customer loyalty is determined by lender economics (net margin on originations through Figure) rather than consumer preference — which creates both stickiness (lenders are addicted to the profitability unlock) and vulnerability (if a cheaper competitor can deliver similar margins to the lender, the consumer end-user won't resist the switch).

Regulatory Risk: The DART/MERS Battle Is Figure's Biggest Long-Term Bet

The most important long-term regulatory risk identified across AlphaSense sources is Figure's dependence on resolving the DART vs. MERS question in its favor. MERS (Mortgage Electronic Registration Systems), owned by MERSCORP Holdings (a Fannie/Freddie-backed consortium), is effectively a government-sanctioned monopoly over the lien registry infrastructure for conforming US mortgages. Figure's DART technology provides a demonstrably superior, blockchain-native alternative: real-time lien updates (vs. MERS periodic reconciliation), fraud prevention via immutable chain of title, and ~$50/loan in cost savings on assignments. Despite these advantages, DART faces severe structural headwinds. Its legal standing under E-Sign requirements and eNote standardization for conforming loans has not been tested in court. GSE acceptance requires lobbying a "notoriously change-resistant" institution (Fannie Mae's technology infrastructure predates the internet). Until DART achieves MERS equivalency for GSE-backed loans, Figure cannot originate or sell conforming mortgages — limiting it to the non-conforming/private credit market and foreclosing the multi-trillion dollar agency TAM. Experts estimate this barrier will require "five to ten years" to overcome through the industry's own inertia. For investors, this creates the most important scenario binary: either DART wins agency acceptance (massive upside, $100-$200+ stock) or it doesn't by 2028 (Figure remains a high-quality but TAM-constrained fintech, $50-$60 stock). The current market appears to be pricing this at near-zero probability.

Leadership and Culture: The Cagney Factor

Industry professionals and origination partners consistently identify Figure's leadership as a meaningful competitive advantage — specifically the combination of Mike Cagney's institutional credibility and the executive team's "dual fluency" in mortgage operations and decentralized finance. Cagney's SoFi track record — building a fintech from scratch to $10B+ market cap — creates trust with both traditional IMB lending partners (who respect his understanding of mortgage mechanics) and blockchain-native institutions (who respect his DeFi vision). This credibility has been crucial in maintaining partner loyalty during periods of competitive pricing pressure, as partners attribute strategic value to Cagney's long-term vision beyond immediate economics. Expert calls characterize Figure's corporate culture as "relentlessly disruptive" — praised for its pragmatic, outcome-focused approach to blockchain adoption (prioritizing actual liquidity creation and asset distribution over "buzzy technology" for its own sake). One area of concern noted across expert calls: the Q4 2025 phishing breach (approximately 12,400 SSNs exposed) exposes a potential cultural blind spot where the pace of growth may be outrunning information security investment. Any second major security incident would be disproportionately damaging to the institutional relationships that constitute Figure's back-end moat.
